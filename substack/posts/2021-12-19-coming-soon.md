*“Pricing is the moment of truth — all of marketing comes to focus in the pricing decision.”* - E. Raymond Corey.

Welcome to *The Pricing Conundrum,* a newsletter written by me, Utpal Dholakia. If you are here reading this, chances are you are interested in pricing, have read [one of my articles](https://hbr.org/search?search_type=&term=utpal+dholakia) on pricing in Harvard Business Review, or have seen [one of my posts on LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/utpaldholakia/detail/recent-activity/shares/) about some aspect of pricing. I have also written two popular and well-reviewed books on pricing (so far), [How to Price Effectively: A Guide for Managers & Entrepreneurs](https://www.amazon.com/How-Price-Effectively-Managers-Entrepreneurs/dp/099918671X), and [Priced to Influence, Sell & Satisfy](https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/B07TCSWB8Z/ref=dbs_a_def_rwt_bibl_vppi_i1) as well as books on [Digital Marketing](https://www.e-elgar.com/shop/usd/advanced-introduction-to-digital-marketing-9781803921075.html?srsltid=AfmBOooJlXmkHmNd12Z3lpNDEZYYBZXFc-BinCi18WhXLVS5QAkgVX41) and [Transparency in Business](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-031-12145-6). More are on the way.

Pricing strategy is fascinating because it is such an important part of business success, yet it seems to be an afterthought or done rather poorly in so many companies. Pricing is also often one of the weakest links for many entrepreneurs. And for consumers, understanding the principles of pricing strategy is a route to becoming a smarter and wiser consumer. Learning about pricing strategy issues is useful to pretty much everyone, whether you are a manager, an entrepreneur, a business school student, or a consumer.

![](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/d816752d-3945-4c25-80ec-eb4336a1f709_1351x1164.png)

As a subject, pricing is endlessly fascinating. It is an amalgamation of consumer psychology, microeconomics & marketing that throws up new phenomena and unintended consequences week in and week out. There’s always something new and interesting to study and learn from. Recent years have seen more of both than in the past. Companies selling AI products are introducing new pricing models in public on a monthly basis and discovering their flaws just as quickly. Subscription businesses are finding that the consent a customer gave at sign-up does not cover everything that happens afterward. And younger consumers are spending money in ways that conventional models of affordability do not predict.

In writing pieces on *The Pricing Conundrum*, my goal is to provide you with the latest insights and tools to think about pricing strategy. I rely on nearly three decades of academic research and consulting experience in marketing and pricing to provide you with this knowledge.

Each piece in *The Pricing Conundrum *focuses on one pricing topic, case study, concept, tool, or pricing phenomenon. Here are some examples grouped by category.

I also have two longer projects running alongside the regular pieces. The first is a series on the history of price-change communication, which asks how sellers have explained price increases to buyers over the past 4,000 years. The [kick-off post](https://thepricingconundrum.substack.com/p/from-diocletians-edict-to-netflixs) proposes that sellers have drawn upon five vocabularies of price legitimacy: authority, virtue, necessity, mechanism, and future value. The [second installment](https://thepricingconundrum.substack.com/p/authority-legitimacy-and-13th-century) covers antiquity through 1500, including a 13th-century episode of shrinkflation. Later installments move forward through the early modern era, the modern industrial periods, and into the present.

The second is a set of original teaching cases with companion teaching notes, written for graduate and executive classrooms. [Streamline Energy Partners](https://thepricingconundrum.substack.com/p/streamline-energy-partners) puts the reader in charge of a midstream pipeline operator that is losing money each month and must choose among four options, using incremental cost analysis. The [teaching note](https://thepricingconundrum.substack.com/p/streamline-energy-partners-teaching) works through the financial analysis and the discussion questions. Other cases include [Predicate AI: Pricing an AI-native deposition tool at launch](https://thepricingconundrum.substack.com/p/predicate-ai-pricing-an-ai-native-b8f), [Apex Blend: Establishing a promotional pricing strategy](https://thepricingconundrum.substack.com/p/apex-blend-establishing-a-promotional), [Kaval Crytoware](https://thepricingconundrum.substack.com/p/kaval-cryptoware), and [Sentinel Solutions: Constructing a price quote in oilfield services](https://thepricingconundrum.substack.com/p/sentinel-solutions-constructing-a). The cases are free to use for teaching, and I ask only that you contact me for commercial uses.

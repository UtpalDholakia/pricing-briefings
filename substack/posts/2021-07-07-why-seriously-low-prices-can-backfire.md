Managers are [far happier when they are cutting prices](https://hbr.org/2001/10/are-your-prices-too-low) than when they are raising them. There are many reasons for this, of course. A sense of discomfort when your prices are higher than competitors (even when well-deserved). A focus on [short-term performance measures](https://hbr.org/2007/07/if-brands-are-built-over-years-why-are-they-managed-over-quarters) like [sales lifts and market-share gains](https://www.amazon.com/Manage-Profit-Not-Market-Share/dp/1591395267). Overestimating how much a price cut will boost your sales. The belief that [aggressive short-term pricing leads to permanent increases in profitability](https://hbr.org/2001/10/are-your-prices-too-low). A bias for decisions that can be easily explained in investor and analyst calls. And the list goes on. When we keep pushing this managers’ bias in favor of lower prices to its logical conclusion, the company can end up with prices that are too low. And too-low prices can do more harm than good.

As I’ve [written before](https://thepricingconundrum.substack.com/p/the-eight-psychological-affordances), a product’s [price level](https://thepricingconundrum.substack.com/p/understanding-the-difference-between), in and of itself, delivers useful and even diagnostic information about the product’s quality to buyers. The diagnostic effects of price are pronounced when the buyer is not knowledgeable about the product category or the seller’s brand or does not have the motivation, the opportunity, or the ability to investigate in detail.

In such cases, which are all too common, the price tag performs many of the same functions that a brand does. The price conveys meaningful associations, many of which have to do with the product’s quality (or lack thereof). And this is when the potential danger of charging a price that is too low is the greatest. It is this:

> #### **Ask for a price that is too low, and the potential customer will immediately associate your product or service with poor quality and low value.**

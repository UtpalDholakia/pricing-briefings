### **Introduction[^1]**

On September 30, 2023, Mark Rodriguez, the CEO of Sentinel Solutions, faced a critical pricing decision for his company’s flagship green pipeline corrosion inhibitor. Sentinel’s contract with Horizon Midstream Partners (HMP), its largest client, was about to expire at the end of the year, and he had to quote a price for upcoming negotiations. The pricing context for corrosion inhibitors had changed rapidly over the last year, which meant that Rodriguez had to juggle his desire to position Sentinel as a premium green specialty chemicals brand with the real and present danger of losing a major client account because of a bad pricing decision.

Two significant developments had affected Sentinel’s recent e3pricing strategy. First, several new producers of green corrosion inhibitors had entered or were about to enter the market in 2024. Prices for corrosion inhibitors were going down quickly. Rodriguez was sure that at least some of them would bid aggressively for HMP’s 2024 business. But there was also good news. The second big development was that new EPA and PHMSA regulation proposals were moving forward briskly. When enacted (and it was only a matter of time), they would require pipeline companies to publicly disclose all chemical constituents of corrosion inhibitors used. Similar rules were also coming at the state level. That meant demand for plant-based pipeline corrosion inhibitors[^2], where Sentinel was the market leader, was likely to double in 2025.

> ##### “Even with all this energy transition talk, the truth is natural gas is booming in the United States. The demand for corrosion inhibitors, especially those that are green, is high. I’m looking at 2023 revenue forecasts of \$64.9 million, a jump of 47% from 2022 levels, when our product flew off the shelves, so to speak. We had no real competition. But now, I’m wrestling with two big questions as I think about pricing for HMP, our top customer. First, should I cut our price for the 2024 contract talks, knowing competitors might come in with bids as much as 50% lower? Second, if one of those competitors snags part of HMP’s 2024 business, can I quote a price in 2025 that will be competitive enough to win the business and still be profitable for us? It’s a real challenge.”

### **A Brief Overview of the Natural Gas Pipeline and Corrosion Protection Industries**

Natural gas is transported across the country using extensive pipeline networks spanning thousands of miles. These pipelines are subject to corrosion, which can lead to leaks, reduced efficiency, and potentially catastrophic failures if not managed properly. Corrosion occurs in natural gas pipelines through different mechanisms. Internal corrosion occurs from moisture and contaminants present in the gas, while external corrosion results from soil conditions, moisture, and microbial activity. Consequently, monitoring and minimizing corrosion with corrosion inhibitors is not just a safety and environmental protection issue for pipeline companies, but it is one with significant economic ramifications.

Since their introduction in the 1950s, pipeline corrosion inhibitors have evolved significantly. Environmentally friendlier formulations first emerged in the 1980s as a niche product. Then, driven in part by the movement from Vice President Al Gore’s warnings, green inhibitor use in U.S. natural gas infrastructure started rising in the new millennium. It grew 17% annually during 2000–2006, then accelerated to 48% between 2006-2010 as regulations became tighter. Paralleling the US, global demand for green inhibitors expanded steadily throughout the 2010s, reaching \$9 billion by 2023 with a CAGR of 3.7%[^3].

Concerns about the environment and new regulations further drove the appetite for green corrosion inhibitors, with the oil and gas sector representing nearly a third of the total market from producers and midstream companies. In late 2023, the future still looked rosy. Projections from various reputable sources forecast continued reliance on natural gas as a bridge fuel during the energy transition in the United States. The country’s pipeline infrastructure was expected to be the primary distributor of natural gas.

#### **The Process of Protecting Natural Gas Pipelines from Corrosion**

Protecting natural gas pipelines from corrosion involves a multi-faceted approach.

**Initial pipeline treatment**: Applying protective coatings and inhibitors during the pipeline’s manufacturing and installation phases typically requires 1,000-2,000 gallons of inhibitor per 100 miles of pipeline.

**Ongoing protection**: Regularly injecting corrosion inhibitors into the pipeline system to create a protective film on the internal metal surfaces requires 50-200 gallons per month per 100 miles of pipeline. These inhibitors prevent electrolytic reactions that cause metal degradation.

**Monitoring and maintenance**: The pipelines must be inspected and tested periodically, and corrosion inhibitors applied as needed to ensure ongoing effectiveness.

The corrosion inhibitor’s composition varies slightly for each customer, based on pipeline variables. Traditionally, the vast majority of pipeline operators used corrosion inhibitors that contained quaternary amines, phosphates, or film-forming amines; the inhibitor produced a protective barrier between the metal surface and corrosive elements. Additives served distinct purposes. For instance, one type of additive would prevent microbial growth in the pipeline, while another would enhance the protective film formation. These inhibitors were (and still are) used for internal pipeline protection (injected into the flow) and external coating systems (applied during construction and maintenance).

The most notable environmental concern with these inhibitors is the threat that chemicals released during pipeline leaks or maintenance pose to nearby ground and drinking water. Chemical contamination can occur from underground leaching, or, more likely, from an above-ground spill of chemical inhibitors that reach nearby water sources. Other environmental concerns include soil contamination around pipeline infrastructure, contamination of local water sources, impact on local flora and fauna, and greenhouse gas emissions, particularly from methane leakage during production and transport.

### **Sentinel Solutions: Company Background**

Sentinel Solutions was launched in 2010 by Mark Rodriguez, an entrepreneur who was interested in sustainable tech, and Dr. Amir Khan, a chemical engineer. Together, they targeted eco-friendly, chemical-free alternatives to the toxic solutions they saw dominating industries like oil and gas. In particular, they homed in on plant-based innovations that matched or outperformed the conventional, harmful options. Their timing was good; their emphasis resonated with growing environmental awareness, increasing regulation, and societal pushback to oil and gas, allowing them to carve out a solid market niche.

His colleagues described Rodriguez as “a quintessential entrepreneur, bold, confident, decisive, and relentless.” They admired his knack for spotting and seizing opportunities that others didn’t, and his grit in the face of challenges. As demand for green products grew, so did Sentinel’s revenues. However, the cyclical downturn in oil and gas and industry consolidation made things challenging. After peaking at \$46 million in 2017, Sentinel’s revenues dipped to \$27 million by 2020.

#### **Sentinel’s Expansion into Pipeline Corrosion Protection**

A series of declining revenue years tempered Rodriguez’s enthusiasm and increased his resolve. In early 2021, hungry for new opportunities, he attended an industry conference for natural gas operators and service companies. As he explained afterwards,

> ##### “Talking to executives and chemical engineers who knew the ins and outs of pipeline operations sparked an idea. Fired up, I zeroed in on creating a non-toxic solution for the sector. I put a small team of engineers to work on a plant-based corrosion inhibitor, free of any environmentally harmful chemicals. Five months later, they had produced a promising formulation, made solely from plant oils and natural, food-based ingredients. I tasked Jamie Chen, our VP of sales, with pitching the product to natural gas companies, big and small, to test the waters.
>
> ##### Almost right away, in July 2021, three companies, including Horizon \[HMP\], requested samples of this new product. To be honest, Jamie and I weren’t holding our breath for quick wins. Big gas companies move very slowly, their sourcing departments are glacial. They use formal Request for Proposal (RFP) processes that can drag on for months or even years. From my background research, I knew many of these companies either made their own corrosion inhibitors in-house or bought them as part of larger contracts with pipeline maintenance suppliers.”

In September 2021, Chen scheduled a follow-up meeting with HMP’s vice president of chemical engineering at the company’s Texas headquarters. Afterwards, Chen called Rodriguez and told him:

> ##### “Mark, we misjudged HMP’s interest in our product. It’s not just casual interest. I found out that a lawsuit has been filed by a property owner near one of HMP’s pipeline corridors, claiming their groundwater is tainted by chemicals from HMP’s corrosion inhibitors. They’re worried about the financial hit if they lose, not to mention the bad press in today’s eco-conscious world. HMP’s growing fast. From August 2022 on, they plan to roll out new pipeline segments at 15 every quarter, which amounts to about 500 miles of new pipeline. Every 100 miles of pipeline needs 1,800 gallons of inhibitor for the initial treatment and 150 gallons monthly for ongoing maintenance. They’re genuinely excited about our product because it’s the only one that satisfies their sustainability targets. I’m convinced they’ll buy it in huge quantities, and right away, if we can meet their demand. The problem is that our current capacity is only 3,000 gallons a month.”

Spurred by HMP’s strong interest, Rodriguez and Chen decided to take a chance. They viewed corrosion inhibitors as Sentinel’s best bet for quick growth in the short term, proving a pathway to offer more sustainable oil and gas products and services later on. They decided to make HMP’s business their main focus. They also targeted a second, slightly smaller competitor of HMP, Talon Energy (TE), which was just as keen on green inhibitors. (See Exhibit 2 for projected annual corrosion inhibitor requirements by customer.)

Rodriguez explained the logic behind this thinking:

> ##### “In the fall of 2021, pipeline maintenance and groundwater contamination became a political lightning rod amid rising climate activism and the push for clean energy. Social media was flooded with videos of residents near pipeline corridors grappling with water quality problems, and scenes of protests about natural gas pipelines. It was a PR disaster in the making for the natural gas industry. I knew HMP and others in this space were desperate to reduce the fallout while keeping their operations running. It was a golden chance to expand our business, but we had to move fast.”

#### **Securing a Financial Commitment from HMP and TE**

In October 2021, Rodriguez sent two senior chemical engineers to central Texas, arranging long-term housing for them. They were tasked with collaborating with HMP’s chemical engineers to modify and produce the green corrosion inhibitor HMP required by Q3 2022. Rodriguez observed:

> ##### “HMP appeared committed to quality and effectiveness, sparing no expense. When given two ingredients with nearly identical results, they consistently chose the costlier option to ensure no compromises in efficacy or safety. Sentinel’s team built a strong rapport with HMP’s engineers, who trusted the product. Beyond professional collaboration, personal connections formed between them. I heard stories of HMP engineers inviting Sentinel’s team members to their homes to eat authentic Texas barbecue.”

Rodriguez had invested Sentinel’s dwindling resources in the HMP project, and time was running out. Sentinel was on life support. Securing firm commitments from HMP and TE was an urgent priority. On December 1, 2021, Chen and Sentinel’s lead chemical engineer, Michael Torres, met with HMP’s leadership. Chen declared, “Sentinel is fully dedicated to your business. We’ve relocated our top chemical engineers to Texas, and the entire company is prepared to work overtime to meet your needs for Q3 and Q4.”

Chen then explained that to produce the volume of inhibitor HMP required from August to December 2022, Sentinel would need additional production facilities and materials. This required HMP’s financial support to begin production immediately. Sentinel’s current systems, at full capacity, could only produce 3,000 gallons of corrosion inhibitor monthly; they needed to scale up to 10 times that amount. However, due to the company’s small size and strict credit restrictions, securing the necessary \$200,000 for early expansion was impossible. Chen stated, “Without a preliminary financial commitment by January 1, we cannot guarantee delivery by August.” Silence filled the room as Chen continued, “We need at least six months to expand facilities and reach full production capacity. This timeline is non-negotiable, as confirmed repeatedly with your engineers. The final price for our product will range between \$18 and \$40 per gallon.”

Chen and Torres were asked to step out; they were summoned back two hours later. HMP presented them with a letter of intent, agreeing to cover up to \$100,000 in non-recoverable expansion costs through March 1. Chen noted:

> ##### “We walked out of HMP feeling energized, and honestly, a bit shocked. Now the real work began. After calling Mark to share the news, Michael and I headed straight to Talon Energy to make the same pitch. We’d learned that highlighting the risk of not delivering our product was the key to sparking action and securing financial commitment. We left TE with a similar deal in hand.”

Until then, Rodriguez had stayed out of the negotiations. But with firm contractual and financial commitments from both companies, Rodriguez joined Chen to hammer out the supply contract terms, particularly the price per gallon. Rodriguez said:

> ##### “I took a bold stance with HMP. I met their VP of strategic relations and laid it out. I said to him that to do business, we needed two things: (1) HMP had to buy the equipment for our manufacturing expansion and transfer ownership to us, and (2) they had to reimburse our costs for building and setting up the new facility, and fast.”

HMP’s sourcing negotiator was shocked. Rodriguez recalled:

> ##### “HMP wasn’t exactly thrilled with my approach, and tensions ran high. Their VP shot back, ‘Is this a partnership or a power play? Because it feels like you’re calling all the shots.’ He pointed a finger at me. ‘You young startups are always gunning for quick wins. You shy away from compromise, even when it’s a better deal for everyone. That’s not how we do business in Texas.’ We walked out of the meeting feeling deflated. I couldn’t shake the feeling that the deal had stalled.”

However, a few days later, HMP reached out. After crunching the numbers and weighing the pros and cons of teaming up with Sentinel, HMP’s strategy team agreed to contribute up to \$1 million in startup costs. TE also followed suit, pledging up to \$750,000 to cover initial expenses. The project was on.

#### **Pricing Negotiations for 2022 Supply**

As Sentinel dove into pricing talks, Rodriguez knew his top priority was to drive home the uniqueness of their product. He had to convince HMP and TE that no one else could deliver the same quality and environmental benefits on the tight timeline they wanted. Rodriguez also emphasized how Sentinel’s solution could help them navigate the looming regulatory requirements in Congress and the potential PR landmine.

However, TE’s chemical engineering team had a different idea. Before working with Sentinel, they’d already stockpiled a significantly cheaper, non-toxic film-forming agent and wanted Sentinel to use it in the corrosion inhibitor formula instead of their own premium component. The TE agent was only marginally less effective. Statistically, the difference was negligible. Sentinel agreed to utilize this ingredient, which would drive down production costs for the TE-bound product.

Rodriguez and Sentinel’s chemical engineers collaborated closely with the sourcing managers at HMP and TE. The sourcing managers at both companies used a similar approach. They developed a costing formula based on a judgment of the product’s formulation, and then calculated costs using current market rates for ingredients and processes. Finally, they assigned what they thought was a reasonable markup to assign the price for the final product. Rodriguez explained, “HMP’s purchasing team had a formula for breaking down component costs. Once they’d calculated the total, they’d lowball the figure to leave room for negotiation.”

In early February 2022, Rodriguez obtained HMP’s costing formula when an HMP chemical engineer inadvertently copied him on an internal email. Armed with this information, he built a mirror pricing model that used HMP’s estimates instead of Sentinel’s actual costs. Rodriguez explained, “With HMP’s formula in hand, I aligned our costs with their expectations. Sometimes their estimates were off, but luckily for us, they were way too high for a couple of the high-volume ingredients and processes. By tweaking our cost structure to match theirs, I knew we’d make a solid profit.”

#### **The Emerging Competition**

Rodriguez identified three potential competitors that could realistically introduce a similar green corrosion inhibitor by 2023. These included:

1.  ***EcoWorks***, a private company with approximately \$25 million in revenue, specializing in eco-friendly solvents for agriculture and aquaculture.

2.  ***Nexus Enterprises***, a large publicly traded oil and gas corporation with \$750 million in revenue and an in-house team of chemical engineers. They produced corrosion inhibitors for their own use and had recently announced plans to prioritize the development of green corrosion inhibitors in 2023.

3.  ***AquaWise Solutions***, a private company with \$20 million in revenue, developed and launched a proprietary ceramic-based corrosion protection system that was environmentally friendly.

Nexus, with its deep pockets, had the resources to outpace Sentinel in production capacity. Rodriguez suspected they could launch a competing product by early 2023, but Nexus claimed it wasn’t interested in selling to third parties. AquaWise had pitched to HMP in March 2022 but didn’t aggressively pursue the opportunity. Rodriguez surmised this was most likely because of a lack of awareness about HMP’s pending lawsuit and environmental concerns

Meanwhile, AquaWise approached HMP with a proposal to undercut Sentinel’s 2023 prices by 50%. However, they requested financial and engineering support from HMP, which was rejected. By summer 2022, Sentinel had secured its position with HMP, and no other supplier was close to negotiating a deal for 2023.

#### **2022-2023**

The first five months of Sentinel’s partnership with HMP and TE (August to December 2022) were smooth sailing. Sentinel’s reliability and willingness to go the extra mile paid off, earning them 100% of each company’s business for 2023. Rodriguez credited the strong relationships they’d built during negotiations and the ramp-up phase. He said, “Pipeline infrastructure is a multi-billion-dollar investment, and maintenance decisions come with huge risks. Our clients trusted us because we had forged personal connections over the past year. It was a team effort. We would work overtime, and HMP’s team would match us hour for hour.”

But by June 2023, things began to change. After over a year of collaboration, HMP’s VP of strategy told Rodriguez that this level of collaboration was no longer necessary. Instead, Alex Banfield, a sourcing manager at HMP, would take over and engage with Sentinel. Banfield was a mid-level employee who approached decisions by the book, seeking management approval for almost everything. In accounting for this change, Rodriguez speculated, “Banfield implied we’d taken advantage of HMP, and that he was there to restore the status quo. It felt personal. Our team had grown accustomed to working with talented, decisive people. Suddenly, we were stuck in a bureaucratic limbo.” Rodriguez’s relationship with Banfield was strained, to say the least. In many respects, it was adversarial.

### **Negotiations for the 2024 Contract with HMP**

In August 2023, Rodriguez decided to get a head start on pricing negotiations for 2024 supply. Now that competitors knew market interest was high, Rodriguez expected HMP and TE would field multiple bids. Also, through networking at industry conferences, HMP’s engineers were now aware of TE’s successful implementation of its cheaper film-forming agent, and HMP wanted the same ingredient used in its 2024 product. Even though Sentinel had reduced its price significantly in 2023, Rodriguez still predicted that a competitive 2024 proposal would be significantly lower than what Sentinel had quoted HMP in 2023. He observed,

> ##### “I knew that HMP would not give 100% of their 2024 business to a new, untested vendor even if the price was half of what they paid us in 2023. It was simply too risky. The real question, in my mind, was not whether we’d get the business in 2024, but how we could use our 2024 proposal to help secure HMP’s business in 2025 and 2026, when competition was sure to be fierce.”

Over the course of the next few weeks, Rodriguez emailed three different proposals to HMP, quoting prices at different supply levels. Each time, Banfield emailed a response indicating that HMP was not ready to enter negotiations yet. Rodriguez took this as a sign that Banfield was buying time to evaluate competitive offers.

Meanwhile, in August 2023, Chen, who had cultivated a healthy relationship with TE, informed Rodriguez that, after little negotiation, TE had selected Sentinel to supply corrosion inhibitors to 60% of its pipeline network in 2024, on the following terms:

TE’s remaining 40% supply would come from EcoWorks. Rodriguez knew EcoWorks would be preoccupied in the short term with meeting TE’s 2024 production needs, and therefore was of minimal threat to HMP’s 2024 business. However, once production at EcoWorks was underway, Rodriguez expected the company would hotly pursue HMP’s 2025 business.

#### **HMP’s Request for Proposal**

Rodriguez received a call from Banfield on September 20, 2023:

> ##### “Mark, we’re happy with your performance so far. We’d like you to quote us on three levels of supply of your green corrosion inhibitor for 2024: 50%, 75%, and 100% of our business. Can you get those numbers to us by October 1st, ten days from now?”

Rodriguez was caught off guard by the tight deadline. It seemed HMP wasn’t giving potential competitors much time to gear up for production. With EcoWorks tied up with TE and Nexus uninterested in selling, Rodriguez suspected HMP might be eyeing AquaWise as a backup plan. He doubted AquaWise could handle more than 25% of the business, given their six-month lead time. But then again, had he underestimated them? Was there another supplier HMP was considering that he didn’t know about?

As he reviewed the internal cost estimates for 2024 (see Exhibit 5), he weighed his strategy for the upcoming negotiations. Several factors were on his mind:

- **Competition:** Rodriguez had learned that AquaWise had approached HMP in 2023, offering to supply corrosion inhibitors at 50% of Sentinel’s price in exchange for half of HMP’s business. He believed AquaWise’s CEO was eager to secure HMP’s business and was willing to make significant concessions.

- **Regulations:** Proposed EPA and PHMSA regulations would require energy companies to disclose chemical additives used in pipeline maintenance. With several states already having passed similar laws and others considering it, Rodriguez expected the trend to continue and eventually lead to nationwide disclosure requirements.

- **Alternative Technologies:** Chemical-based green corrosion inhibitors were just one solution. Ceramic- and nanotechnology-based approaches were being developed as environmentally friendly alternatives. Rodriguez anticipated further innovations in the coming years.

- **New Customers:** After two years with HMP, Sentinel had established itself as a reliable supplier. Inquiries were increasing, and Cascade Oil and Gas, a company similar in size to HMP, was in talks with Chen about supplying them by 2026. Cascade’s future demand would be substantial, especially with their plans to develop 5,000 new pipeline miles by the end of 2028.

### **The 2024 HMP Pricing Proposal**

Rodriguez stood up and gazed out at his team through the glass wall of his office.

> ##### “Our people’s talent, drive, and teamwork have driven Sentinel’s success. Now it’s my job to protect that success. This HMP proposal is make-or-break for us; the stakes are high. I don’t want to lay off any of our employees, but that’s what I’ll have to do if I slash our prices by 50%. On the other hand, sticking to our current pricing strategy could be equally dangerous if it jeopardizes future revenue and damages our reputation as a trusted, high-quality vendor.”

**THE HEADLINES**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

“FULL DISCLOSURE OR FULL PENALTIES”

“36-MONTH COUNTDOWN FOR TOXIC CHEMICALS”

“GREEN CHEMISTRY GETS REGULATORY FAST LANE”  
*Plant-based and biodegradable alternatives receive expedited approvals and potential subsidies.*

------------------------------------------------------------------------

- FINANCIAL IMPACT: Industry compliance costs estimated at \$250-365 million over five years, with small-to-medium operators bearing disproportionate burden

- MARKET DISRUPTION: Chemical suppliers unprepared for transparency requirements face potential loss of 60-80% of pipeline market share

- COMPETITIVE ADVANTAGE: Environmentally-friendly alternatives positioned to capture premium pricing and market share

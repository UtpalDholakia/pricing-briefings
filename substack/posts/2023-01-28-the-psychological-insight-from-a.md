Here's a remarkably perceptive snippet about consumer psychology from the 22-year old film, [You’ve Got Mail](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0128853/). The process involved in a simple everyday decision to buy a \$2.95 (then, \$4.95 now) cup of coffee can instantly generate a sharp definition of one's self. (The relevant bit starts at 0:35).

[Embedded content: www.youtube-nocookie.com](https://www.youtube-nocookie.com/embed/hZ7lFV9Z2hk?rel=0&autoplay=0&showinfo=0&enablejsapi=0)

In it, Tom Hanks’ character says,

> "The whole purpose of places like Starbucks is for people with no decision-making ability whatsoever to make 6 decisions just to buy a cup of coffee. Short… tall… light… dark…nonfat… So people who don't know what the hell they're doing or who on Earth they are can, for only \$2.95, get not just a cup of coffee, but an absolutely defining sense of self. Tall… decaf… cappuccino!"

With every small decision we make during the process of buying a product, we learn something about ourselves. The fact that I chose to drink my coffee black this morning and refused slugs of hazelnut or caramel syrup or sweet cream tell me something insightful and identity-reinforcing about myself. My choice says I am a no-frills, [old school, patient, authentic](https://www.equitybank.com/your-life/what-your-coffee-says-about-your-personality), and rugged human who wants to get to the heart of things and [who loves winter](https://nypost.com/2019/09/13/what-your-coffee-preference-may-reveal-about-you/).

Isn’t this insight into one’s self-identity worth paying a few dollars for?

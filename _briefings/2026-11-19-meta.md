## 1. [The Philippines froze 190 prices for Christmas — but not rice, fuel or fares](https://sulit.ph/dti-christmas-price-freeze-basic-goods-philippines-2026/)

**Source:** Sulit, Philippines, Nov 2026, citing the Manila Times (Nov 6), PhilNews and the Palace (Nov 3): [sulit.ph](https://sulit.ph/dti-christmas-price-freeze-basic-goods-philippines-2026/)

The Philippines' Department of Trade and Industry has secured manufacturers' commitment to hold prices on 190 packaged basic goods — canned fish, milk, coffee, bread, noodles, soap, batteries — through December 31, 2026, with the official suggested-retail-price bulletin posted in supermarkets. Trade Secretary Ma. Cristina Roque confirmed the commitment on November 5. The catch is what it leaves out: rice, fuel, electricity and transport fares are not covered, and those are the categories driving October's 7.8% headline inflation — rice up 20.7% year on year, transport up 20.1%, housing and utilities up 9.3%. The freeze is also voluntary guidance, not a legal ceiling: it rests on manufacturers' agreement rather than an order, though the Price Act's ban on profiteering — a rise of more than 10% in a month without cost justification — applies year-round. A freeze on the shelf-stable aisle while the rice sack and the jeepney fare keep climbing is a price control that protects the wrong budget line.

## 2. [Gemini 3.8 Flash doubles on January 1 — and 2.5 Pro users get rerouted to it on December 1](https://kostkompass.com/gemini-api-price-increase-december-2026/)

**Source:** Kostkompass, Nov 2026, rates read from Google's Gemini API pricing page on Nov 10: [kostkompass.com](https://kostkompass.com/gemini-api-price-increase-december-2026/)

This briefing covered Gemini 4 Argon and Carbon doubling on December 1; the part not yet covered is what happens to the cheaper models. Gemini 3.8 Flash and 3.6 Flash double on January 1, 2027, from $0.75 to $1.50 per million input tokens and $3.75 to $7.50 for output, with Batch and Flex tiers doubling the same day. And from December 1, Google's changelog says all API requests to `gemini-2.5-pro` will automatically route to `gemini-3.8-flash` and be billed at Flash rates — no code change needed for the bill to change. The reroute looks like a cut in December, since Flash is cheaper than 2.5 Pro, and then the January doubling takes the line back near where it started, now running on a model nobody chose. Google has also said that from November 13, a Gemini 4 answer longer than 32,000 tokens is charged even if the request fails on a timeout. These are Google's published list prices; what any buyer actually pays under a commitment is not disclosed.

## 3. [Anthem quoted a vintage clothing chain a 59.5% premium rise — and it cannot pass the cost on](https://www.marketplace.org/story/2026/11/13/small-businesses-struggle-with-rising-healthcare-costs)

**Source:** Marketplace, US, Nov 13, 2026: [marketplace.org](https://www.marketplace.org/story/2026/11/13/small-businesses-struggle-with-rising-healthcare-costs)

Beacon's Closet, four vintage and thrift stores in New York City with nearly 50 full-time staff, got its annual renewal letter from Anthem Blue Cross and Blue Shield this fall quoting a 59.5% premium increase. The chain pays about $600,000 a year for health insurance; at the quoted rise that would pass $1 million, which the owners say would put them out of business. After appealing, the best Anthem offered was still a 34% rise, and the chain settled for an 8.3% increase — against a national average employer premium rise of 5% in 2026, according to KFF, and early estimates of another 8–9% for 2027. The owners posted the renewal letter on Instagram, where it drew thousands of likes and hundreds of comments from other small-business owners sharing the same experience. The pricing point is the constraint: they can only raise prices so much on secondhand clothes, and the owner says the cost is instead landing on wages — the biennial cost-of-living raise "becomes almost impossible".

## 4. [South Africa's energy regulator admits in court that it approved unlawful power tariffs](https://nairaenergies.com/news/nersa-admits-metro-electricity-tariffs-unlawful-south-africa/)

**Source:** Naira Energies, South Africa, Nov 12, 2026, citing The Citizen and EWN (Nov 11): [nairaenergies.com](https://nairaenergies.com/news/nersa-admits-metro-electricity-tariffs-unlawful-south-africa/)

South Africa's National Energy Regulator, NERSA, told the High Court in Pretoria that the 2025/26 electricity tariffs it approved for Johannesburg, Ekurhuleni and Nelson Mandela Bay were unlawful, calling its own approvals "indefensible". Emfuleni and Madibeng municipalities were also named. NERSA had been due to redetermine the tariffs by June 30 but missed the deadline and had not finished by November; it asked the court to let it phase in corrections over future years and apply them to all users. The challenge was brought by businesses, including Autocast SA, Mpact and Coega Steels, and the judge raised the options of an independent expert or court supervision. The case follows an earlier one in which Msunduzi municipality still owes about R200 million in credits to nearly 700 businesses. This is a different NERSA story from the Nov 6 solar-tariff consultation this briefing covered on Nov 15: that one is about future tariff design, and NERSA's decision on Eskom's 2027/28 tariffs is still due on Nov 26.

## 5. [Kenya's households pay 22 US cents a kWh — nearly three times Vietnam's rate, the World Bank says](https://www.africaupdates.com/news/world-bank-blames-governance-failures-for-kenya-s-high-electricity-costs-n2137/2026/11/06)

**Source:** Africa Updates, Kenya, Nov 6, 2026, on the World Bank's November 2026 Kenya Economic Update: [africaupdates.com](https://www.africaupdates.com/news/world-bank-blames-governance-failures-for-kenya-s-high-electricity-costs-n2137/2026/11/06)

Kenyan households pay an average of 22 US cents per kilowatt-hour for electricity, against 17.2 cents in Uganda, 11.9 in Morocco and 7.8 in Vietnam; Kenyan businesses pay 17.5 cents, against 7.8 in Vietnam. The World Bank's November 2026 Kenya Economic Update blames governance failures rather than generation costs: only 24% of 42 active power purchase agreements were awarded through open competitive tendering, several contracts were signed outside the government's least-cost plan, and "take-or-pay" deals force Kenya Power to pay private producers for capacity even when the electricity is not needed. Transmission and distribution losses run above 23%, against the regulator's 16.5% target, with the bank citing illegal connections and meter tampering alongside technical losses. The bank estimates a 10% cut in industrial tariffs would lift GDP by 0.27% and private investment by 0.33%. This is a diagnosis, not a price change — no tariff has moved — but it names the mechanism behind a price gap that a tariff decision alone would not close.

## 6. [Google Play's new fee split reaches Korea, Turkey and South Africa on Dec 31 — India waits until 2027](https://ecorpit.com/google-play-service-fee-split-2026-app-economics/)

**Source:** eCorpIT, India, updated Nov 15, 2026, citing Google's Play Console Help and Android Developers Blog: [ecorpit.com](https://ecorpit.com/google-play-service-fee-split-2026-app-economics/)

Google Play split its old bundled cut into a service fee and a separate 5% billing fee on June 30, 2026 in the EEA, UK and US, and on September 30 in Australia and Japan. The next wave lands December 31, 2026, when South Korea, Turkey and South Africa move onto the new structure; India and the rest of the world do not change until September 30, 2027. Under the new card, the service fee starts at 10% on the first $1 million of annual earnings and on all auto-renewing subscriptions, rising to 20% on standard one-time purchases from new installs — 25% for existing installs — with the 5% billing fee on top only when Google processes the payment. Developers in the Apps Experience and Games Level Up programs pay 15% on new installs. The date matters because it is also the "new install" threshold: a user whose first install falls after a region's rollout date is priced differently from one who installed before. Indian developers selling to users in the US, UK or EEA are already on the new card, because the fee follows the buyer's region, not the seller's.

## 7. [India's first UPI merchant fee is written, deferred — and still dateless after Diwali](https://www.tldr.money/articles/banking/upi-merchant-fee-myth-vs-fact)

**Source:** tldr.money, India, updated Nov 1, 2026, citing NPCI's FAQ (updated Oct 15, 2026); no start date announced as of Nov 13: [tldr.money](https://www.tldr.money/articles/banking/upi-merchant-fee-myth-vs-fact)

India's NPCI has published the first charge on UPI merchant payments since the zero-fee regime began in January 2020: a merchant discount rate of 0.4% on person-to-merchant payments above ₹2,000, capped at ₹300, with a flat ₹5 for fuel, utilities, telecom, railways and insurance. It was due on October 15, deferred on October 13 to "immediately post Diwali", and Diwali passed on November 8 with no date yet notified as of November 13. Consumers pay nothing: the fee is the merchant's, NPCI says it cannot be passed on, apps cannot add a platform fee, and payments up to ₹2,000 — over 95% of merchant payments by volume — carry no charge. Small merchants taking up to ₹1 lakh a month through a QR code pay nothing at all. The framework is being challenged in the Supreme Court, which refused a stay on September 28. The pricing question is what happens when the fee lands on a thin-margin merchant who is forbidden to show it on the bill: NPCI says shelf prices should not rise because of it, but nothing in the framework stops a quiet repricing.

## 8. [Small firms are opting out of Black Friday in record numbers — and those that join are borrowing to do it](https://www.xero.com/us/media-releases/aussie-small-businesses-turning-to-credit-to-compete-in-black-friday-sales/)

**Source:** Xero, Australia, Nov 6, 2026; MoneySuperMarket via CFOtech, UK, Nov 19, 2026: [xero.com](https://www.xero.com/us/media-releases/aussie-small-businesses-turning-to-credit-to-compete-in-black-friday-sales/)

Two surveys on the same week point the same way. Xero's survey of 500 Australian small businesses, published November 6, found 53% will take part in Black Friday, up from 43% last year — but 55% of past participants relied on credit or deferred payments to fund extra stock and staff, 23% saw their profit margins shrink, and only 43% of micro businesses with two to ten staff hit the sales they needed, against 75% of firms with 11 to 20 staff. Among businesses that never took part and are sitting out or undecided, 27% say they cannot afford to discount. In the UK, a MoneySuperMarket poll reported November 19 found 35% of small businesses will not take part in Black Friday, up from 22% a year earlier — about 1.97 million firms — and in New Zealand 31% do not intend to take part, rising to 44% among sole traders. The pattern is the margin floor: the smallest sellers are the ones leaving, and the ones staying are funding the discount with debt. Less than a third of the Australian firms use real-time profit or margin data to decide their discounts.

## 9. [Atlassian starts metering AI and automation on Dec 3 — and the free allowance is 2.5 requests a user](https://sanka.com/daily/2026-11-18/)

**Source:** Sanka, Nov 18, 2026, citing Atlassian's support pages; Atlassian Support: [sanka.com](https://sanka.com/daily/2026-11-18/)

This briefing flagged Atlassian's December 3 usage-based billing on Nov 17 and held it as a countdown item; it is now 14 days out, and the practitioner detail is in. From December 3, 2026, Atlassian bills for Rovo credits, automation steps and AI agent resolutions beyond each organisation's monthly allowance, and extra usage is switched on by default. Extra Rovo credits list at $0.01 each and extra automation steps at $0.50 per 1,000, both list prices from Atlassian's own pages. The allowances are thin: Jira or Confluence Standard includes 25 Rovo credits per user per month, and a basic request costs 10 credits, so the allowance covers 2.5 basic requests per user per month; Premium covers 7 and Enterprise 15. Allowances are pooled across the organisation and do not roll over, and if extra usage is switched off, automations stop running when the allowance runs out. Atlassian's cloud list prices also rose on October 13, so the meter arrives on top of a higher seat price. The mechanism is the story: the seat price did not change, but a second price — per request, per step — now sits next to it, billed by default.

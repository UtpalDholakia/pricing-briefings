## Key takeaways

- HubSpot changes where eligible customers can spend a commitment without reducing its value.
- A mystery discount is more attractive relative to a sure discount when that sure saving feels small.
- Buying more on a promotion and wasting more are separate outcomes.

## What happened

### 1. HubSpot lets customers repurpose a commitment without reducing it

**Source:** [HubSpot announcement](https://www.hubspot.com/company-news/flexible-pricing) · October 15, 2026

Eligible customers can exchange unassigned seats for credits during their contracts. HubSpot is also expanding spending controls. The flexibility has boundaries: converted credits cannot become seats again, and the financial commitment remains.

**Tags:** SaaS pricing, Credit-based pricing

### 2. Substack’s tax settings can change the bill without changing the price

**Source:** [Substack’s explanation](https://support.substack.com/hc/en-us/articles/53351588701588-Upcoming-changes-to-tax-on-Substack) · Updated October 5, 2026

From December 1, Substack will administer applicable subscription taxes for covered publishers. US and Canadian publishers must add tax at checkout. By default, the highest applicable rate among selected benefits applies to the whole subscription. Publishers can instead allocate value across benefits, where appropriate.

**Tags:** subscription pricing, Price transparency

### 3. Mystery discounts appeal when the certain saving feels trivial

**Sources:** [“Why (and When) Are Uncertain Price Promotions More Effective Than Equivalent Sure Discounts?”](https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucaf036) · June 20, 2025; February 2026 issue · [academic.oup.com](https://academic.oup.com/jcr/advance-article-abstract/doi/10.1093/jcr/ucaf036/8171334)

Five studies involving 8,969 participants find that chance-based promotions become relatively more attractive when the equivalent guaranteed discount feels small. Context and percentage framing can produce that feeling without changing the monetary saving. Importantly, greater relative appeal does not always mean participants prefer the gamble outright.

**Tags:** Lottery, Price Promotions

### 4. Tiny price increases deserve their own audit

**Sources:** [“Retail Price Ripples”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02564) · December 2, 2025 preprint · [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.02564)

Using nearly 79 billion weekly observations from 2006–2015, the authors find that small increases outnumber small decreases in the aggregate. The asymmetry largely disappears beyond roughly ten cents. Some categories and retailers show the reverse pattern. The sample covers 527 subcategories, not merely 527 individual products.

**Tags:** price increases, Processing Prices

### 5. Buying more on promotion does not necessarily mean wasting more

**Sources:** [“Does Cash Really Mean Trash?”](https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucad018) · March 7, 2023. Older discovery · [academic.oup.com](https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article/50/4/663/7071580)

A Dutch household field study found no positive relationship between promotions and reported food waste across eight perishables. Multibuy purchasers sometimes reported less waste, alongside greater consumption and freezing. Experiments support the proposed mechanism: buying unusually large quantities activates concern about waste and encourages preventive behavior.

**Tags:** Price Promotions, Consumer welfare

## Why it matters

**1. HubSpot lets customers repurpose a commitment without reducing it** My interpretation is that this addresses the frustration of paying for unused capacity while buying replacement capabilities. It makes committed spending feel recoverable without offering a refund. The one-way conversion also encourages AI adoption. Retention effects remain undisclosed. Practitioners should distinguish flexibility in allocating expenditure from flexibility in reducing expenditure.

**2. Substack’s tax settings can change the bill without changing the price** Readers experience the total charge, not the publisher’s unchanged list price. A community feature can therefore affect the perceived price of an otherwise written newsletter. My inference: publishers should explain renewal totals and review benefit classifications before customers encounter the charge.

**3. Mystery discounts appeal when the certain saving feels trivial** The mechanism is not simply overweighting small probabilities. The certain alternative matters. Before replacing a discount with a prize draw, test whether customers regard the forgone saving as negligible.

**4. Tiny price increases deserve their own audit** Consumer inattention is a plausible explanation, not a demonstrated cause. For practitioners, the useful diagnostic is cumulative exposure. An audit focused only on conspicuous increases can miss repeated small adjustments. Historical observational data do not establish current retailer intent.

**5. Buying more on promotion does not necessarily mean wasting more** Purchase quantity alone is an inadequate welfare measure. Promotions can change how households manage food after checkout. “Buy one, freeze one” is a useful communication hypothesis. Self-reported waste and greater consumption also require separate scrutiny.

## Ideas to apply

- Distinguish customer value from revenue flexibility when evaluating a credit conversion.
- Show readers the expected renewal total when taxes change the eventual charge.
- Audit repeated small increases cumulatively. Compare that result with an audit that only flags conspicuous changes.

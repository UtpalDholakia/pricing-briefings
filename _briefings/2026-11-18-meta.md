## 1. [India caps cancer-drug trade margins at 30% — prices fall up to 70%](https://biospectrumindia.com/article/pdf/28746)

**Source:** BioSpectrum India, India, Nov 1, 2026: [biospectrumindia.com](https://biospectrumindia.com/article/pdf/28746)

India's National Pharmaceutical Pricing Authority notified a 30% cap on trade margins for 137 non-scheduled anti-cancer drugs on October 17, 2026, with revised maximum retail prices taking effect on November 9; the list includes immunotherapies such as pembrolizumab, durvalumab and osimertinib, and the government expects prices to fall by up to 70% and patients to save about Rs 2,500 crore a year. The cap targets the distribution chain, not the manufacturer's price: it limits what distributors and retailers can add, and manufacturers must file revised prices, advertise the cuts, and keep producing the covered drugs. The Supreme Court pushed the government here — on October 12 it asked why the intervention stopped at cancer drugs, citing an Indian-made cardiac stent supplied to retailers for around Rs 5,000 but carrying a maximum retail price of Rs 25,000, and asked the government to examine a broader 16% retail-margin framework. A separate set of gazette orders on November 2 fixed retail prices for 34 newly introduced formulations, including a gemcitabine injection at Rs 5,623.07 a vial.

## 2. [Google One's cheapest plan jumps 32% in Pakistan — the third rise in under two years](https://www.techjuice.pk/google-one-30gb-price-increase-pakistan-january-2027/)

**Source:** TechJuice, Pakistan, Nov 16, 2026: [techjuice.pk](https://www.techjuice.pk/google-one-30gb-price-increase-pakistan-january-2027/)

Google will raise the annual price of its Google One 30GB plan in Pakistan from PKR 1,899 to PKR 2,500 from January 12, 2027, a rise of PKR 601 or about 32%, taking the monthly equivalent from roughly PKR 158 to PKR 208. This is the third Google One revision for Pakistani users in under two years: Google doubled the 100GB Basic plan from PKR 219 to PKR 419 a month in March 2025, then raised prices again across Algeria, Egypt, Nigeria, Pakistan and Türkiye in July 2026. Google attributes the rises to higher infrastructure and service costs, though the report notes Pakistan's exchange rate stayed relatively stable, so currency does not explain the full jump. The increase is announced now but takes effect in January, so subscribers who check their renewal date before January 12 can still lock in the old rate for a year — and anyone whose stored data exceeds the free 15GB limit cannot simply cancel without losing uploads, backups and incoming mail.

## 3. [On Threads, small sellers are opting out of Black Friday — and shoppers say the deals are fake anyway](https://www.threads.com/@libragirl1016/post/DfyIuAXFZRv)

**Source:** Threads, Nov 14–18, 2026: [threads.com](https://www.threads.com/@libragirl1016/post/DfyIuAXFZRv)

The small-seller opt-out that ran through last week's Threads posts is still growing, and it now has a consumer echo. One small-business owner posted on November 15 pushing back at complaints that 30% off is not a real sale, arguing that a deeper cut would be a loss on her margins and inviting critics to compare with the full price. Separately, a November 16 post dismissing typical Black Friday discounts drew about 27,000 likes and 140 comments, with commenters sharing examples of token cuts and items marked up before the sale. And a November 18 post from a small-business owner warned that steep panic discounts — a 70% sale is the example given — train customers to reject the normal price once the sale ends, so the seller has to keep discounting to sell at all. Three different mechanisms in one thread of posts: the margin floor that makes discounting impossible, the reference-price inflation shoppers now assume, and the learned waiting that discounts create. ## 6. [Swiggy and Zomato's platform fee went from ₹2 to ₹17.58 in three years — and competition just froze it](https://longpress.news/swiggy-zomato-platform-fee-creep)

**Source:** Long Press, India, Nov 1, 2026; TradingView, Nov 17, 2026: [longpress.news](https://longpress.news/swiggy-zomato-platform-fee-creep)

Zomato introduced its platform fee at ₹2 an order in August 2023 and called it a test; it is now ₹17.58 including GST on every order, including orders from paying Gold and One subscribers, after steps of ₹4 in January 2024, ₹6 in mid-2024, ₹10 as a "festive season fee" that October, ₹12.50 through 2025, and ₹14.90 before GST in March 2026, which Swiggy matched within the week. The fee is now 5–6% of average order value in Indian cities, on top of delivery, packaging and a ₹25 rain fee added in September 2025; in July 2026 more than 1,000 Bengaluru restaurant owners threatened to delist over the fee stack. What changed this month is the direction: Zomato is holding back platform-fee increases during the festive season for the first time in three years, deploying deeper checkout discounts instead, as Swiggy's Toing, Rapido's zero-fee Ownly and Flipkart's Eat-In pilot challenge the duopoly. The next pressure is already visible — India's new Labour Code, effective November 21, may add about ₹3.2 a food-delivery order in social-security contributions, according to a Kotak estimate, which the platforms will have to absorb or pass on.

## 7. [ServiceNow renewals now carry 8–12% unit rises plus a 3% compounding uplift — and AI is bundled in whether you use it or not](https://www.npifinancial.com/knowledge-center/how-to-beat-servicenow-at-the-negotiation-table-in-2026/)

**Source:** NPI Financial, US, published Apr 6, 2026, updated Nov 9, 2026: [npifinancial.com](https://www.npifinancial.com/knowledge-center/how-to-beat-servicenow-at-the-negotiation-table-in-2026/)

Procurement advisory NPI's analysis of enterprise ServiceNow contracts finds unit prices rising 8–12% even for customers with growing licence volumes, and renewal contracts increasingly carrying 3% compounding annual uplift clauses — about 9% more over a standard three-year term on the exact same licence footprint. The AI layer is the bigger lever: Now Assist is gated behind Pro Plus and Enterprise Plus tiers, and upgrading to those tiers represents a 30–60% increase in per-user cost, while AI usage grew ninefold in the first half of 2025 and quickly outpaced the bundled Assist allotments, with overage costs not transparently priced. Support is being repriced too — Impact Advanced is no longer offered in 2026, pushing customers toward the costlier Impact Total. These are advisory estimates from NPI's contract work, not list prices: ServiceNow publishes no rate card for any of its products, so there is no published list price to compare against — list price not disclosed, net price not disclosed, and the figures above are what advisers see buyers actually being asked to pay. Yesterday's run found no fresh vendor announcement from ServiceNow; this is the mechanism behind the renewals rather than a new list change.

## 8. [Salad raised GPU rates up to 33% on November 1 — and gave six days' notice against a 14-day promise](https://www.usagepricing.com/blueprint/salad)

**Source:** UsagePricing, checked Nov 5, 2026, citing Salad's own price-change post of Oct 26: [usagepricing.com](https://www.usagepricing.com/blueprint/salad)

Salad, which rents out idle consumer GPUs by the hour, raised its High, Medium and Low priority rates on 21 GPU classes on November 1, 2026, while leaving its cheapest Lowest tier unchanged; High rose 10–20% on most classes, with an RTX 4090 at High priority going from $0.330 to $0.440 an hour, a 33% rise. The increase applied to instances already running at 00:00 UTC on November 1, so existing workloads got dearer with no redeploy. Salad's stated reason is supply — demand has outpaced supply on its most popular GPUs, and it is raising provider payouts to attract more — and the tiering is deliberate: raising High most and Lowest not at all widens the gap so High stays available. The notice is the story as much as the rise: Salad's blog post went up on October 26, six days before the change, while its own terms promise at least 14 days' notice. This is Salad's second rate-card change in 2026, after a September 12 repricing that cut most classes.

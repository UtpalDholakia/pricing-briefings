## Key takeaways

- An all-in ticket price can make checkout more predictable while a change in the fee formula makes a group order more expensive.
- Runna raised both annual prices in the UK. The extra amount a shopper pays to choose the Strava bundle instead of Runna alone went down.
- A historical low-price claim can be less convincing when it refers to a more distant period.

## What happened

### 1. Tixtree moves the booking fee from the order to the ticket

**Source:** [Tixtree organizer help center](https://www.tixtree.com/help/books/organizers-faq/page/why-all-in-pricing) · Change effective November 1, 2026

Tixtree announced two changes for purchases starting November 1. Buyers would see prices including applicable fees and taxes upfront. Organizers could also replace an order-level processing fee with a booking fee on each item. Tixtree says its own platform fee did not rise. If an organizer keeps the same fixed fee but applies it to each ticket, five tickets carry five fixed charges instead of one. That is an implication of the terms, not a measurement of actual orders.

**Tags:** All-inclusive pricing, Partitioned Pricing

### 2. Runna’s price increase changes the choice between two plans

**Sources:** [Runna UK subscription notice](https://support.runna.com/en/articles/18298149-changes-to-our-uk-subscription) · New rates from November 10, 2026 · [QuietlyChanged](https://quietlychanged.com/changes/2026-11-14-runna-uk-prices-rise-from-first-renewal-on-or-after-dec-10/) · November 14, 2026

Runna’s UK annual plan went from £99.99 to £109.99. The annual Strava bundle went from £119.99 to £124.99. Existing subscribers move at their first renewal on or after December 10. Runna attributes the bundle increase to its own service and says the Strava component stayed the same. But the gap between the two published annual prices fell from £20 to £15.

**Tags:** Price Bundling, Subscription Renewals

### 3. A Tokyo burger chain cuts the price of a staple

**Sources:** [United & Collective announcement](https://prtimes.jp/main/html/rd/p/000000036.000055277.html) · November 12, 2026 · [Nihon Interview Shimbun](https://www.media-ir.com/news/?p=188255) · November 14, 2026

Starting November 13, the 3rd Burger cut its regular chicken burger from ¥450 to ¥390 at all five Tokyo locations. A chili bowl lunch set fell from ¥940 to ¥840. The chain also introduced a ¥540 chicken teritama burger. Management said changes to chicken specifications helped reduce ingredient costs. The announcement did not report how shoppers responded.

**Tags:** QSR Pricing, Price Communications

### 4. A plastic bottle bought admission to a Houston garden

**Source:** [Houston Botanic Garden, Texas Recycles Day](https://hbg.org/event/texas-recycles-day-celebration-3/) · Offer ran November 13–15, 2026

For three days, Houston-area residents could bring one plastic bag or bottle per person for free daytime admission. H-E-B sponsored the offer. The garden advertised a saving of up to $12 per adult. It did not report attendance attributable to the promotion.

**Tags:** Incentives, Accessibility

### 5. A more distant low-price claim can be less persuasive

**Source:** [Jin and colleagues, Psychology & Marketing](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mar.70135) · March 18, 2026 · Research discovery

Across five studies that combined secondary data with controlled experiments, more distant historical price cues produced weaker purchase responses than more recent cues. The researchers linked the effect to greater difficulty retrieving the comparison and lower credibility. The difference was smaller among consumers who were more aware of persuasive tactics. The study does not establish a sales penalty that applies across retailers.

**Tags:** Academic research, Price Promotions, Informational Value

## Why it matters

For Tixtree, disclosing the whole price and deciding how to calculate it are separate choices. For Runna, the comparison a shopper sees can move in a different direction from the component price the company describes. Both are reasons to audit the choice the customer actually faces.

A price cut on a familiar burger may influence how people judge the rest of the menu. The Houston offer poses a different question: whether bringing a recyclable makes the visit feel more purposeful or creates one more reason not to go. The historical-price study shows why an impressive comparison period is worth little if buyers don’t trust it.

## Ideas to apply

- Compare old and new ticket totals for one ticket, a pair, and a family order.
- Measure whether a smaller bundle premium changes the choice between plans among new buyers and renewing customers.
- When a promotion uses a noncash requirement, compare attendance with unconditional free admission. Measure participation by group size, not just total visits.

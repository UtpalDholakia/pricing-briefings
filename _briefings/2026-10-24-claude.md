## 1. Nine-ending prices absorbed tariff shocks: left-digit bias slowed pass-through — Hirshman & Ignatenko, CESifo Working Paper 13056 (SSRN, posted Oct 19)

**Source:** [papers.ssrn.com](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7622838)

Using retail price data around recent tariffs, the authors find that cost shocks passed through mostly to prices that did not end in 9, while 9-ending prices showed much lower pass-through, consistent with firms fearing to cross a left-digit threshold. Inflation after the tariffs was noticeably higher for non-9-ending products, even untariffed ones. A rare macro-scale demonstration that a consumer bias becomes a firm-side pricing friction that shapes aggregate inflation.

## 2. LLM pricing agents learn to set a high "anchor" and then undercut it — Bichler, Hierhager, Köck & Pieroth, arXiv 2610.21711 (Oct 15)

**Source:** [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21711)

In repeated Bertrand simulations, whether LLM agents reach competitive or supracompetitive prices depends on deployment: agents fed raw market histories with rivals' prices sustain high prices in self-play, while agents reasoning over summarized own-profit histories drift toward competition. In mixed markets the LLM cycles between posting a high-price anchor and undercutting it to win demand. Interesting for anyone thinking about how consumers will read reference prices when the sellers setting them are agents.

**Type:** Research · **Tags:** Academic research, Dynamic pricing, Algorithmic Pricing, AI in Pricing, Collusion, Behavioral Economics

## 3. A "moral harm" account of price fairness — Campbell, Pomerance & Percival Carter, Journal of Consumer Research 53(3), October 2026 issue

**Source:** [academic.oup.com](https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article/53/3/444/8195730)

Now in the print issue: eight studies argue that fairness judgments rest partly on inferred harm to people, driven by the firm's strategy, buyer vulnerability, and the product's welfare impact. Holding a price constant after costs fall is seen as unfair even with no reference-price violation, and the harm-fairness link is stronger for liberals and weaker when the firm appears to be acting in self-defense. A useful extension beyond dual entitlement for explaining why price cuts and differential prices can also be judged unfair.

**Type:** Research · **Tags:** Academic research, Fairness, Consumer welfare, Behavioral Economics, Costs

## 4. Court lets FTC drip-pricing case against Ticketmaster proceed; "+Fees" label called inadequate — All About Advertising Law (Venable), Oct 14

**Source:** [allaboutadvertisinglaw.com](https://www.allaboutadvertisinglaw.com/2026/10/court-allows-ftc-drip-pricing-lawsuit-against-ticketmaster-to-proceed.html)

A California federal court denied dismissal, accepting that showing the all-in price before checkout does not cure deception that "induces the first contact," and that a reasonable consumer would not read "+Fees" as a 44% markup. It also treated Ticketmaster's claim that it avoided all-in pricing to stay competitive as supporting the FTC's theory. In effect this is a legal endorsement of the anchoring mechanism behind partitioned pricing research; note the contrast with a Maryland ruling requiring reliance under state law.

**Type:** Regulation · **Tags:** Price transparency, Partitioned Pricing, Regulation, Pricing Laws, Legal issues in pricing, concert ticket pricing, Price labels

## 5. Carnival brings dynamic pricing to onboard packages — Global News, Oct 21

**Source:** [globalnews.ca](https://globalnews.ca/news/12110936/carnival-cruise-lines-dynamic-pricing/)

Starting December 1, some Carnival onboard bundles will vary by season, sailing length, and itinerary, framed as 35–55% savings versus à la carte, while the company declined to explain how prices are calculated. This is a good test case: demand-based pricing of add-ons sold to a captive audience after the main purchase, where fairness norms around exploiting lack of alternatives should be especially strong.

## 6. Surveillance pricing, argued both ways — Penn Gazette (Oct 22) and CNN Business (Oct 19)

**Source:** [thepenngazette.com](https://thepenngazette.com/high-price-low-price-your-price-my-price/) · [cnn.com](https://www.cnn.com/2026/10/19/business/surveillance-prices-government-bans)

Read together, they show the policy fight hinges on whether consumers can tell personalization from dynamic pricing, an open empirical question.

## 7. EU's shrinkflation patchwork: France, Austria, Romania require shelf warnings; Brussels resists — Euronews, Oct 23 (with Mouseprint's fall 2026 shrink list, Oct 17)

**Source:** [euronews.com](https://www.euronews.com/2026/10/23/less-product-same-price-what-is-supermarket-shrinkflation) · [mouseprint.org](https://www.mouseprint.org/2026/10/17/here-we-shrink-again-fall-2026-part-1/)

Euronews summarizes the national rules (France since 2024; Austria since April 2026 for unit-price increases of 3% or more), a Hamburg court treating same-package downsizing as misleading, and the Commission's pushback on Italy's labels. Mouseprint's new US list (Hershey's Kisses, Ziploc, Dixie, a store-brand oatmeal) shows private labels following brands down. The cross-country variation in disclosure regimes looks like a natural experiment for testing whether shelf notices change consumer detection and response.

## 8. EU consumer authorities target in-game currency pricing at nine game firms — Lewis Silkin, Oct 15

**Source:** [lewissilkin.com](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/10/15/eu-consumer-protection-network-launches-action-involving-nine-video-games-compani-102o5do)

The CPC Network has opened action against firms including Supercell, Riot, Mojang, King, and Ubisoft under its principles on virtual currencies: showing prices in real money, avoiding mismatched currency bundles that force leftover balances, and making exchange rates clear. Regulators are treating token currencies explicitly as a mechanism that dampens the pain of paying and drives overbuying, which brings mental-accounting research directly into enforcement.

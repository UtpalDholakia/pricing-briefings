## Key takeaways

- Hyatt’s new rules make some awards usable together. That can change a reward’s value without changing its stated amount.
- Alaska replaces a company fee plus employee-card fees with one annual charge. The comparison depends on team size.
- Hy-Vee is ending annual auto-renewal as it prepares to close its paid program. Monthly billing here is a transition arrangement.

## What happened

### 1. Hyatt removes a choice between some rewards
**Source:** [Hyatt’s 2027 loyalty announcement](https://newsroom.hyatt.com/worldofhyatt2027), November 18, 2026.

From January 2027, members can combine eligible suite upgrades, Guest of Honor and free-parking awards on the same reservation. Hyatt also says awards will be valid for 365 days after issue. The important change is not just earning more rewards. Removing a restriction on using rewards together can increase their usefulness. That is a practitioner interpretation of the terms, not evidence that members will redeem more or stay more often.

**Tags:** Loyalty Program, Rewards Program, Customer loyalty.

### 2. Alaska’s business card changes who benefits from the fee
**Source:** [Alaska Air Group’s card announcement](https://news.alaskaair.com/loyalty/alaska-air-group-refreshes-atmos-rewards-visa-business-card-with-bank-of-america/), November 18, 2026.

The Atmos Rewards Visa Business Card replaces $70 per company plus $25 per employee card with a flat $195 annual charge and no additional employee-card fee. Five employee cards put the old fee at $195. Smaller teams paid less under the old structure, while larger ones paid more. New benefits also accompany the change, so the fee calculation is not a complete value comparison. The seller has changed the unit of charging, not merely the number.

**Tags:** Pricing models, Price Bundling, Membership.

### 3. Hy-Vee stops selling a year it plans not to renew
**Source:** [Hy-Vee membership terms](https://www.hy-vee.com/corporate/policy/membership-terms/), changes effective October 29 and November 5, 2026; checked November 19.

Hy-Vee stopped selling annual Perks Plus memberships on October 29 and stopped annual auto-renewal from November 5. Monthly membership is $8.25 plus tax, billed every 30 days. It plans to phase out the program in early 2027. Existing annual members keep benefits through their terms. A former annual member must actively sign up for monthly service to continue. Do not treat the resulting lapse rate as ordinary price-driven churn.

**Tags:** Membership, subscription pricing, Subscription Renewals.

### 4. Dossier’s fourth bottle changes the kind of reward
**Source:** [Dossier’s promotion terms](https://dossier.co/pages/terms-conditions/), sale period November 18–24, 2026.

For guests, three eligible items earn 20% off, four earn a free perfume, and five earn 40% off. The tiers are not cumulative. The fourth-item reward therefore changes from a percentage saving to a gift. The gift is preselected and has no cash value. This menu invites shoppers to compare unlike rewards while deciding whether to add a bottle. Its sales effect is unknown. A test should measure paid basket value after discounts, not item count alone.

**Tags:** Price Promotions, Price Bundling, Consumer decision making.

### 5. A grocery coupon can win a basket from another store
**Source:** [Gabel, Simester and Timoshenko, In-Store Coupons](https://artemtimoshenko.com/assets/papers/In%20Store%20Coupons%20February%202026.pdf), February 2026 working paper.

An older discovery: in a German supermarket experiment spanning 71 categories, store-entry coupons increased the promoted brand’s purchase incidence on the current visit from 0.33% to 1.61%. Separate household-panel analysis suggests some expansion came from competing stores, rather than purchases simply brought forward. The randomized evidence concerns coupon offers, not coupon clipping versus automatic savings. Retailer gains need not equal manufacturer gains when sales move between stores selling the same brand.

**Tags:** Academic research, Price Promotions, Retailer Pricing Strategy.

## Why it matters

Hyatt changes redemption compatibility. Alaska changes the charging unit. Dossier changes the reward at the next threshold. Each makes a simple comparison of advertised amounts incomplete. The grocery experiment adds another question: whose incremental sale is being counted? A store can gain a purchase without creating another purchase for the brand across all stores.

## Ideas to apply

- Compare reward redemption and repeat purchasing before and after removing a use restriction. Keep earning rates separate.
- Show a fee comparison for the buyer’s actual quantity, including the break-even point. List changed benefits alongside it.
- For a basket promotion, compare contribution after discount and gift cost across thresholds. Check returns as well as checkout baskets.

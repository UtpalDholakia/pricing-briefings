## Key takeaways

- How people read a price ending may depend on who they believe set it. The experiments did not establish a sales effect.
- Precise prices can seem cheaper without reliably raising purchase intention.
- Removing a card surcharge removes a price component. It does not guarantee a lower total payment.

## What happened

### 1. An AI-set price may make “.99” look manipulative

**Source:** [Journal of Consumer Affairs: “When AI Sets the Price”](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/joca.70080) · October 16, 2026

Chacon and Limongi tested price-setting agent and price format with Chilean students and U.S. consumers. Responses were less favorable when prices were attributed to AI. Evidence favoring round over just-below AI prices was less consistent across experiments. The larger study also found evidence consistent with perceived manipulation as a mechanism.

**Tags:** AI in Pricing, just-below pricing

### 2. Precise prices looked cheaper without increasing purchase intention

**Source:** [Frontiers in Behavioral Economics: precise, just-below, and round prices](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/behavioral-economics/articles/10.3389/frbhe.2026.1828446/full) · June 12, 2026 · Research discovery

Two preregistered experiments involving 729 participants compared round, just-below, and precise prices. Precise prices produced the most favorable price image and were most often underestimated in recall. Yet price endings did not reliably change purchase intentions or perceived quality. These were student samples evaluating imagined purchases.

**Tags:** Price presentation, Processing Prices

### 3. Why eight beers might need only three prices

**Source:** [Marketing Science: “Consumer-Driven Class Pricing”](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mksc.2023.0133) · March 12, 2026 · Research discovery

Zuhui Xiao offers a consumer-side explanation for assigning a few price points across many differentiated products. In his analytical model, reference dependence and loss aversion can make finer price differentiation unprofitable. The resulting demand shifts reduce willingness to pay for lower-cost products more than they increase it for higher-cost alternatives. This is a theoretical result, not a field experiment.

**Tags:** Pricing models, pricing structures

### 4. Australia removes card surcharges, but not necessarily their cost

**Source:** [Business.gov.au: new card-payment surcharge rules](https://business.gov.au/news/card-payment-surcharge-changes) · October 1, 2026

New card-network rules took effect on October 1, removing surcharges for major card payments in Australia. Businesses still incur processing costs and may adjust base prices. Other charges, including permitted weekend surcharges, remain separate.

**Tags:** Partitioned Pricing, Regulation

### 5. The subscription renewal decision moves into the banking app

**Sources:** [Subscription Insider: ScribeUp raises $13 million](https://www.subscriptioninsider.com/blog/scribeup-raises-13m-as-subscription-control-moves-beyond-brands) · October 22, 2026 · [businesswire.com](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20261019537229/en/ScribeUp-Raises-%2413-Million-Series-A-to-Bring-Modern-Bill-Management-Solutions-to-Financial-Institutions)

ScribeUp announced $13 million in financing, comprising $10 million in equity and $3 million in debt. Its technology lets participating banks surface recurring bills and help customers manage or cancel them.

**Tags:** Subscription Renewals, Price transparency

## Why it matters

**1. An AI-set price may make “.99” look manipulative** The implication is worth testing: consumers may interpret the same price ending differently depending on who supposedly chose it. This is evidence about judgments and intentions, not demonstrated sales losses.

**2. Precise prices looked cheaper without increasing purchase intention** The useful distinction is between changing a price judgment and changing demand. For practitioners, a favorable “looks inexpensive” score should remain an intermediate measure, not substitute for a consequential purchase test.

**3. Why eight beers might need only three prices** Its practical challenge is useful: more granular pricing is not automatically better, even when setting and maintaining individual prices becomes inexpensive.

**4. Australia removes card surcharges, but not necessarily their cost** The behavioral opportunity is clearer price expectations: a displayed price need not acquire a card fee at checkout. That is an interpretation, not evidence of improved trust or lower spending. Practitioners should distinguish the benefit of removing an irritating price component from the separate question of whether customers pay less overall.

**5. The subscription renewal decision moves into the banking app** The pricing implication is contextual: a subscription can be evaluated beside household expenses, away from the product experience that originally justified its price. Greater visibility and easier cancellation could weaken inertia-based retention. That remains an inference. Neither the financing nor company-reported capabilities establish a measured effect on churn.

## Ideas to apply

- Cross price-ending format with a truthful description of how the price was set. Measure perceived fairness and consequential choice separately.
- Compare pricing many items at a few common points with setting each one individually.
- Evaluate renewal behavior after customers get clearer visibility into recurring charges, rather than inferring a churn effect from the feature alone.

## 1. OpenAI sells the same tokens at five speeds, with a premium tier priced to double in March

**Source:** OpenAI API pricing and GPT-6.2 launch pages (Nov 17, 2026): [developers.openai.com](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing); also [openai.com](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-6-2-astra-sol/)

GPT-6.2 Astra lists at $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens. That is 20% below GPT-6 Astra, which stays on sale at $10/$50, and exactly matches the list price of Anthropic's Fable 5.2. The bigger change is the speed ladder. Background requests are 30% cheaper with a two-hour completion window and 40% cheaper with an eight-hour window. Batch is half price, Fast is 2x Standard, and Ultrafast is 3x Standard until February 28 and 6x from March 1. Selling waiting time is textbook second-degree price discrimination: buyers who can wait sort themselves into the cheap tier. The new part is that the top tier launches at a stated, temporary multiple. Ultrafast buyers will form their reference price at 3x and then face a 100% rise, which the seller announced in advance. Whether pre-announcing a rise makes it feel fair, or just schedules the backlash, is an open question, and I have filed it as one.

## 2. BellRing raises Premier Protein prices and tells investors it expects to lose more volume than it gains in price

**Source:** BellRing Brands Q4 FY2026 release and call (Nov 18, 2026): [sec.gov](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001772016/000162828026074645/brbrexh991-q42026earningsr.htm); call transcript: [fool.com](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/11/18/bellring-brbr-q4-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)

BellRing's adjusted gross margin fell to 20.1% in the quarter from 29.7% a year earlier. Management blamed high-single-digit protein inflation, tariffs included, and said Premier Protein shakes need low-double-digit price increases. Its fiscal 2027 guidance assumes price elasticity greater than one, so volume will fall proportionally more than price rises. Management also said more of the consumer response is still to come. It is rare for a firm to state this out loud. A firm that expects elastic demand and raises prices anyway is betting on margin over volume, which is the opposite of the usual "we're absorbing costs" story. For the reader's research, the interesting part is the gap between investor talk and shopper talk. What investors hear is a price rise expected to shrink volume; shoppers will see a cost explanation on the shelf. Club-channel promotions will show whether Premier's buyers measure the new price against the old shelf price or against competitors' shakes.

## 3. ClickUp turns dollar-priced AI add-ons into pooled credits inside every plan

**Source:** ClickUp Help Center (Nov 2026): [help.clickup.com](https://help.clickup.com/hc/en-us/articles/44297094474391-AI-Credits-availability-and-limits); change log: [pricingsaas.com](https://pricingsaas.com/companies/clickup/diffs/2026W45)

Around November 2, ClickUp retired its $9 and $28 per-user AI add-ons. Each plan now comes with a monthly pool of AI credits: 1,000 per seat on Core, 1,500 on Business and 10,000 on a new $100 MAX tier. Credits are shared across the workspace, reset each month and don't roll over. Extra credits are sold in $10 blocks. Existing workspaces without an add-on get a single grant of 1,000 credits per user, which never resets. The move turns a visible line-item price into a bundle, and the AI cost now sits in an allowance whose value per action ClickUp doesn't publish. That is the vendor-set exchange rate the credits claim is about. Use-it-or-lose-it pools also add a mental-accounting pressure that dollar pricing lacks: buyers may spend credits before they expire, or ration them for fear of running out. Filed as Context on `credits-obscure-true-price`.

## 4. Kenya debates a law that would put every home internet connection on a meter

**Source:** Al Jazeera (Nov 22, 2026): [aljazeera.com](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/22/kenyas-internet-metering-bill-raises-privacy-and-affordability-concerns); also The Standard: [standardmedia.co.ke](https://www.standardmedia.co.ke/business/article/2001562098/telkom-opposes-proposed-internet-metering-rules)

A private member's bill before Kenya's Parliament would require internet providers to give each customer a meter number and bill by data consumed. Supporters present this as fairer billing. The Communications Authority told the committee on November 3 that it opposes a compulsory mandate and proposed itemised bills, audited meters and cheap packages for low-income users instead. On November 10 Telkom Kenya asked lawmakers to protect unlimited and speed-based plans. It noted that mobile data, about 96% of subscriptions, is already billed by volume. What makes this unusual is that the state, not the seller, would choose the tariff structure. The research on flat-rate bias and the "taximeter effect" predicts that many households would pay more and enjoy less under metering. A running meter makes each use feel like a cost, and people tend to overestimate their usage, so they also overvalue the security of a flat rate. In this bill, "fair" means paying for what you use. The evidence suggests many consumers would prefer not to think about the meter at all.

## 5. A 46,000-store audit of Black Friday 'was' prices is under way, and a vendor puts a number on not shopping around

**Source:** OSF preprint (Nov 21, 2026): [osf.io](https://osf.io/7fxzk/overview); Deal.ai press release (Nov 17, 2026): [fortune.com](https://fortune.com/press-releases/deal-ai-single-store-shoppers-pay-more-black-friday-2026-11-17/)

The OSF project asks how often the 'was' price shown on Black Friday exceeds what a store actually charged beforehand. It also asks whether stores under the EU's 30-day prior-price rule behave differently. It scrapes the public product feeds of 46,306 Shopify and WooCommerce stores in 49 countries every six hours, from November 14 to January 31. The design is public before any results exist, which is unusual for this literature. Its stated limit is that a Black Friday reduction can be checked against at most 13 days of prior prices, not 30. Separately, Deal.ai's scrape of 24,394 identical products at 16 US retailers found that one randomly chosen store averaged 15.6% above the lowest listed price. Checking three stores cut the gap to 3.7%. Deal.ai earns commissions from retailers, so treat the figures as company-reported. Read together, the two pieces say something about Black Friday shoppers. The inflated reference price is one cost they face, but stopping after one store may be the larger one. Both are unreviewed, and the OSF study has no results yet.

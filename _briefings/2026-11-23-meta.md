## 1. [11.11 delivered demand but no new people — and the ads chasing them cost up to 88% more per click](https://ecommercesea.substack.com/p/you-cant-bid-your-way-to-a-bigger)

**Source:** EcommerceSEA (Stan Chew), Nov 22, 2026, on day-by-day trackers for ten brands across Malaysia, Singapore, the Philippines and Vietnam; Lazada's own 11.11 report, Nov 20, via Ebrun (machine-translated): [ecommercesea.substack.com](https://ecommercesea.substack.com/p/you-cant-bid-your-way-to-a-bigger) and [english.ebrun.com](https://english.ebrun.com/20261120/721717.shtml)

The platform announcement of Shopee's record 11.11 was covered here on Nov 18; what is new is the sellers' own post-mortem, and it says the opposite of the press release. Across ten brands, visitors came in at 13–65% of target while conversion ran at 130–234% of target — on one electronics brand's Shopee store, 11 November brought 1.6 times a normal day's visitors and an 18.3% conversion rate against about 7% normally. The sale also stretched instead of spiking: daily sales across the window ran at +4% and −11% versus 10.10, against plans of +63% and +18%, spread over 13 days instead of four. Paid traffic came in at 48–76% of target at clicks costing 45–88% more than planned, and the week after the sale was worse — one brand's sales fell to half of October, and lowering its return-on-ad-spend target from 5.8 to 5.5 made the algorithm spend less, not more, because there were not enough people worth bidding on. The official scoreboard tells a different story: Lazada said on Nov 20 that its LazMall brand mall contributed nearly 80% of regional GMV at 7 times the daily level, cross-border GMV grew over 200% year on year, and average order value rose more than 30% across its six markets. Read together, the two accounts say the festival worked for brands shoppers already trusted and for the platforms' own malls, and that the discount did the selling — the new customers the ads were supposed to find mostly never arrived.

## 2. [Alibaba's Qwen4-Max lists at $1.50/$4.50 — 25% below its predecessor, and the flagship is closed](https://www.datastudios.org/post/alibaba-qwen4-max-launch-api-pricing-1-50-input-4-50-output)

**Source:** Data Studios, Nov 2026, on Alibaba Cloud's Model Studio pricing page, updated Nov 16, 2026: [datastudios.org](https://www.datastudios.org/post/alibaba-qwen4-max-launch-api-pricing-1-50-input-4-50-output)

Alibaba made Qwen4-Max generally available on November 16, five days after a limited preview, at $1.50 per million input tokens and $4.50 per million output tokens on its international (Singapore) list, with cache reads at $0.075. That is 25% below Qwen3.8-Max, the model it succeeds, still listed at $2.00/$6.00 in the same region, and on output it costs less than half of Claude Sonnet 5.5 ($2.00/$10.00) and less than a quarter of Claude Opus 5.5 ($4.00/$20.00). Two things about the price are as notable as its level: the same rate applies across the whole 1-million-token context and to thinking and non-thinking modes, so a 900,000-token prompt costs the same per token as a short one; and Alibaba has published no batch price, while Anthropic and Moonshot both discount batch jobs, so the gap narrows for offline work. The release also changes Alibaba's distribution policy at the top of the range: Qwen4-Max is a closed model with no downloadable weights, and Alibaba has said only that smaller Qwen4 models will be released with open weights, without giving dates. These are published list prices from Alibaba's own pricing page; what any buyer actually pays is not disclosed. Alibaba's own claim that the average cost per task fell from $4.43 to $1.67 on its Work360 evaluation is self-reported on an unpublished task set and has not been reproduced independently.

## 3. [Korea's Black Friday came three weeks early — and by November 27 most of it is already over](https://www.epickor.com/blog/573)

**Source:** EpicKor, updated Nov 23, 2026, checked against Korean announcements and press reports: [epickor.com](https://www.epickor.com/blog/573)

Korea did not adopt the American calendar; it put a government festival in that slot instead. The K-Sale Festa, part of the Korea Grand Festival, ran October 29 to November 15 with about 40,000 companies taking part, and the retailers scheduled their own sales inside it: Olive Young's Black Friday ran November 1–7, Coupang's WOW Members Day November 2–8 (more than 370,000 products, up to 70% off, with ₩10,000 and ₩30,000 coupons released twice daily), 11st's Grand 11th Day November 1–11, G Market's Big Smile Day November 2–13, and Shinsegae's SSG Day October 30 to November 8. All of them ended before the US Black Friday on November 27, so a search for "Korea Black Friday" this week mostly returns finished sales. What is actually on in late November is fashion: Musinsa's winter sale runs November 22 to December 2, with the "up to 80%" headline applying only to lucky-draw coupons and second-hand items while countdown coupons go up to 30%; Topten, Spao, Uniqlo, Zara and H&M hold winter sales between November 18 and December 1; and Lotte, Shinsegae and Hyundai department stores run winter sales to November 29 at up to 50–60%. One benefit works at every sale: the instant tax refund, which takes the VAT off at the till on receipts from ₩15,000. One new piece this year is aimed at buyers abroad: the Trade Ministry ran a reverse-direct-purchase sale, Korean shops selling to overseas consumers through a K-Online Shopping Week with Olive Young, Musinsa and Kurly.

## 4. [Dangote cut its petrol price ₦140 in five days — Abuja pumps moved ₦25 to ₦35](https://theradar.ng/business/dangotes-depot-petrol-price-fell-n140-in-five-days-abuja-pumps-moved-n25-to-n35)

**Source:** TheRadar, Nov 21, 2026, on market checks reported by PM News, Legit.ng, BusinessDay and The ICIR: [theradar.ng](https://theradar.ng/business/dangotes-depot-petrol-price-fell-n140-in-five-days-abuja-pumps-moved-n25-to-n35)

Dangote Petroleum Refinery's petrol price for marketers fell from ₦1,210 a litre on Monday, November 16 to ₦1,070 on Friday, November 20 — ₦140 in five days, the lowest since July, after climbing to ₦1,300 on November 3 under a fixed-naira arrangement before the refinery returned to pricing linked to international gasoline benchmarks. The pump barely moved. A Legit.ng survey of Abuja stations on Thursday, November 19 found NNPC selling at ₦1,300 (down from ₦1,325), MRS at ₦1,365 (from ₦1,399), and Empire Energy and Ranoil at ₦1,385 (from ₦1,420) — cuts of ₦25 to ₦35 a litre, so a 50-litre fill-up is now ₦1,250 to ₦1,750 cheaper. Set against the ₦1,070 gantry price, the gap at NNPC's pump is ₦230 a litre. Part of that gap is cost — trucking from Lekki to Abuja, depot charges and the station's margin — and part is timing: stations holding stock bought earlier in the week at ₦1,159 or ₦1,130 have little reason to sell below what that stock cost. Nigeria's downstream market is deregulated, so each marketer sets its own pump price and there is no date by which a depot cut must reach the pump; with December travel coming up, the gap between ₦1,070 at the refinery and about ₦1,300 at Abuja pumps is the number to watch.

## 5. [Argentina's minimum wage buys 36 pan dulce this Christmas — it bought 60 in 2023](https://adalytica.com/news/argentina-minimum-wage-sweet-bread-prices-54d07899)

**Source:** Adalytica, Nov 22, 2026: [adalytica.com](https://adalytica.com/news/argentina-minimum-wage-sweet-bread-prices-54d07899)

Pan dulce, the sweet bread on Argentina's Christmas table, has become a one-number gauge of what disinflation has not done for living standards. In November 2023 a minimum wage bought 60 sweet breads across five major brands; by 2026 it buys 36, 40% fewer, and measured by weight the basket has shrunk 44.4%, from 32.97 kilos to 18.34 kilos. The minimum wage rose 173% over the period, to 398,800 pesos from 146,000, but the bread rose far faster: Fantoche's 500-gram loaf is up 844% to about 8,492.44 pesos, so the same wage buys 46 Fantoche loaves where it bought 162 two years ago, and Don Satur's kilo price is up 254%, Havanna's 227%, Steinhauser's 201% and Bonafide Premium's 203%. The official figures point the same way: Argentina's consumer prices rose 2.2% in October, the highest monthly increase since April, with year-on-year inflation at 33.1% and food inflation at 34.6%. Adalytica is a small analytics outlet and these are its own compilations, not official statistics, so read the brand-level figures as directional — but the direction is the story: nominal pay rose sharply and still lost to the price of a festive staple, and with the holidays approaching the gap between the official wage and the lived price is on the table.

## 6. [Half of America's small businesses raised prices this year — and half plan to do it again](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/small-business-owners-are-more-confident-in-their-own-companies-than-in-the-economy-and-two-thirds-use-ai-gusto-survey-finds-302936587.html)

**Source:** Gusto, 2026 State of Small Business report, Nov 12, 2026, via PR Newswire; survey of 1,731 owners of US businesses with 24 or fewer employees, Aug 17–Sep 7, 2026: [prnewswire.com](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/small-business-owners-are-more-confident-in-their-own-companies-than-in-the-economy-and-two-thirds-use-ai-gusto-survey-finds-302936587.html)

Fifty-two percent of small businesses raised their prices over the past 12 months, most often by up to 10%, while 42% held prices steady and just 3% cut them — and 50% plan to raise prices again next year. The reason is costs, not confidence: 59% of owners say changing input prices hurt their business over the past year, against 3% who say the effect was positive, and 40% say rising interest rates hurt. The same owners are oddly split about the future: 61% are optimistic about their own businesses but only 18% about the US economy, down from 29% a year ago. The finding lines up with the NFIB October survey covered here on Nov 16, which found a net 33% of owners had raised selling prices and a net 37% planned increases, the highest reading since July 2022. Two caveats: the respondents are Gusto customers, so read the numbers as directional rather than a census, and the survey was weighted to the national mix of industries and firm age with a margin of error of ±3.4 points. For Utpal's research the interesting part is the asymmetry — a majority raising prices while a majority also says input prices hurt them is cost-push pricing by firms too small to absorb it, and half of them expect to do it again before their customers have adjusted to the first round.

## 7. [USPS wants its 8% fuel surcharge permanent at 11% — and small sellers are redrawing free shipping](https://waybillwire.com/articles/usps-locks-in-11-fuel-surcharge-and-small-sellers-redraw-the-math-on-free-shippi)

**Source:** Waybill Wire, Nov 16, 2026, on the USPS filing of Nov 12: [waybillwire.com](https://waybillwire.com/articles/usps-locks-in-11-fuel-surcharge-and-small-sellers-redraw-the-math-on-free-shippi)

USPS filed notice on November 12 to make its fuel surcharge permanent at 11% from January 17, 2027, up from the 8% temporary charge it added in April — and on the same day it plans an average 5.9% increase across Priority Mail, Priority Mail Express, Ground Advantage and Parcel Select. The Postal Regulatory Commission reviews the changes before they take effect, and USPS says it will reevaluate in July 2027 based on first-half fuel prices. For small online sellers the date is a deadline, and the responses show how a shipping cost becomes a price decision: TickWise 3 Moms Organics lifted its free-shipping minimum to $100 from $75 after its cost to ship a package climbed to about $8.42 from $5.36, while Joe & Bella tried the same $75-to-$100 move and reverted after conversion dropped by more than half. Others are rewriting checkout itself — Lori's Designer Shoes dropped its flat $10 fee under $150 for calculated shipping, and Todd Shelton now subtracts return shipping from refunds for the first time. The pressure is measurable: in ShipStation's Q4 2026 survey of 1,600 decision-makers at US businesses with fewer than 500 employees, 52% named rising carrier fees and fuel surcharges as a major difficulty, up from 31% in the previous survey, and 72% said shipping and freight costs climbed in the past three months. The context is a market where ground parcel rates already sit at a record 43.3% above the January 2018 baseline, and UPS raises rates an average of 5.9% on December 21 with FedEx following on January 4.

## 8. [Thanksgiving dinner now takes 1 hour 47 minutes of work to pay for — in 1986 it took 3 hours 11](https://www.omnicalculator.com/insights/everyday-life/calculated-thanksgiving-dinner-in-hours-of-work)

**Source:** Omni Calculator, Nov 2026, on the American Farm Bureau Federation survey released Nov 18, 2026 and Bureau of Labor Statistics wage data: [omnicalculator.com](https://www.omnicalculator.com/insights/everyday-life/calculated-thanksgiving-dinner-in-hours-of-work)

The Farm Bureau's $58.63 dinner and the Groundwork $137.12 figure were covered here on Nov 22; what is new is the 41-year series measured in hours of work instead of dollars. The same classic dinner for 10 cost $28.74 in 1986 and $58.63 this year — more than twice as much in dollars — but the average hourly wage of production and nonsupervisory workers rose from $9.01 to $32.75 over the same period, so the dinner now takes 1 hour and 47 minutes of work to pay for, about 44% less than the 3 hours and 11 minutes it took in 1986. The pattern is not a steady fall: after 2000 the work time held almost flat for more than two decades — about 2 hours and 16 to 17 minutes in 2000, 2010 and even in the record-price year of 2022 — then dropped again after 2022, when wages kept rising while the dinner's price came down from its $64.05 peak. The average hides the other half of the story: at the federal minimum wage of $7.25 an hour, this year's dinner takes about 8 hours and 5 minutes of work, a full working day. The Farm Bureau survey is a volunteer price check of one fixed menu, not an official price index, and wages are before tax — but as a long-run measure it makes the point that a price rise and a price burden are different things, and which one a household feels depends on whose wage you divide by.

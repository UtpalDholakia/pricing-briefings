## Key takeaways

- A price-history audit needs a comparison window, not just a percentage of disappointing deals. See item 1.
- Showing price history can itself change expectations. See item 2.
- Separate the purchase deadline from the product's useful life, its components and its usage date. See items 3–5.

## What happened

### 1. A Canadian audit asks whether Black Friday improves the odds
**Source:** [Gabriel Tassé, November 2026; data collected November 19](https://gabrieltasse.com/en/resources/black-friday-best-time-to-buy).

**Tags:** "Amazon", "Discounting", "Consumer decision making".

Across 10,521 product-year observations on Amazon.ca in 2020–2025, Tassé reports a good price in 41% of Black Friday windows versus 31% of comparable eleven-day windows. Good means among the year's cheapest quarter of days, with ties adjusted. This is an independent historical audit, not 2026 sales evidence. Popular-product selection and one marketplace limit generalization. The practical distinction: a useful buying window need not deliver a unique annual low. Audit the benchmark before judging the promotion.

### 2. A price-history chart is not a neutral container
**Source:** [Wenzel Drechsler and Martin Natter, Journal of Interactive Marketing, May 1, 2011](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1016/j.intmar.2011.02.001). Older research discovery.

**Tags:** "Academic research", "Price presentation", "consumer psychology".

The authors report that historical price trends, variation and range influence price expectations and purchase-timing decisions. Their abstract also attributes part of the response to visualization itself. I read the publisher's abstract and appendix, not the full methods. I would not translate the result into a sales lift. The useful question is whether a falling line teaches shoppers to wait. Test the display, not merely whether historical information is present.

### 3. Patagonia rejects urgency without rejecting markdowns
**Sources:** [Patagonia's Black Friday page](https://www.patagonia.com/black-friday.html); [Web Specials](https://www.patagonia.com/shop/web-specials). Undated company pages, checked November 28, 2026.

**Tags:** "Branding", "Discounting", "customer value".

Patagonia's Black Friday page stresses lasting gear and an unlimited-time offer. Its Web Specials page separately explains discounts as surplus inventory from spring 2026 or earlier. These are different promises. Refusing event pressure does not mean refusing clearance. My interpretation: separating the reasons to buy may protect the regular range from a blanket expectation of holiday markdowns. The pages provide no causal evidence of that benefit. Test whether shoppers understand the distinction and whether full-price conversion changes.

### 4. Fairphone discounts the cost of keeping the phone
**Source:** [Fairphone's promotion instructions, updated November 16, 2026](https://support.fairphone.com/hc/en-us/articles/40145188947346-How-to-claim-your-Black-Friday-and-Cyber-Monday-2026-promotions).

**Tags:** "Price Bundling", "Discounting", "customer value".

Fairphone's November 16–30 offer includes a free case and screen protector with eligible Gen. 6 phones, plus 20% off spare parts and accessories. Buyers select the protective items through a pop-up and must check that the cart shows them as free. This packages protection with acquisition while also discounting repair inputs. Whether it improves perceived lifetime value remains untested. Compare the bundle with an equal-cost handset discount. Count redemption failures and later repair purchases, not just initial orders.

### 5. An early-bird ticket need not require an early calendar decision
**Sources:** [Northeast Times, November 27, 2026](https://northeasttimes.com/2026/11/27/harrisburg-outdoor-show-2027-tickets-black-friday/); [organizer's attendee FAQ, undated, checked November 28](https://www.greatamericanoutdoorshow.org/plan-your-trip/attendee-faq/).

**Tags:** "Discounting", "Consumer decision making", "customer value".

The Great American Outdoor Show sells early-bird tickets through December 31. Regular rates begin January 1. Yet a daily ticket works on any one show day, and a two-day ticket need not be used consecutively. The FAQ does not quantify the early-bird saving. The interesting design separates paying early from committing to specific dates. That may reduce the perceived risk of advancing payment. Compare flexible and date-specific offers at the same price before crediting the discount for advance bookings.

## Why it matters

These examples build on the [SALVE framework of price image](https://thepricingconundrum.substack.com/p/pricing-concept-the-salve-framework-51a), especially stability, value for money and evaluability. The extension here is a testing question: can the same price work differently when its history, rationale, included protection or usage flexibility changes? The audit measures prices. The older study measures expectations and timing decisions. The company examples document terms, not customer outcomes. Keep those evidence types separate.

## Ideas to apply

- Define the counterfactual before reporting promotional success: another buying window, another display or another offer structure.
- Measure contribution across promotion and recovery periods. Treat a shifted purchase as different from an additional purchase.
- For early payment, test whether usage flexibility matters more than another percentage point off.

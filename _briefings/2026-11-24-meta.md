## 1. [At Ignite, Microsoft turned Copilot into a seat plus a meter — and governance into a $15 add-on](https://ecorpit.com/microsoft-ignite-2026-recap-azure-copilot-agents/)

**Source:** eCorpIT, Nov 21, 2026, checked against Microsoft's Book of News (updated Nov 19) and Microsoft's pricing pages; seeded-credit change via Ryse Technologies, Nov 2026, on Microsoft's licensing overview: [ecorpit.com](https://ecorpit.com/microsoft-ignite-2026-recap-azure-copilot-agents/) and [erpsoftwareblog.com](https://erpsoftwareblog.com/2026/11/reduce-microsoft-licensing-costs-2027/)

Microsoft Ignite ran November 17–20 in San Francisco, and the pricing news is not a price rise but a change in what a price buys. No list price changed: Microsoft 365 Copilot stays at $30 per user per month, Agent 365 at $15 and the new Microsoft 365 E7 bundle at $99, all paid yearly — these are published list prices, and what any buyer actually pays is not disclosed. What changed is the shape of the bill: the Copilot seat now covers chat and Office apps, while Copilot Cowork is metered, the new Today Feed needs Copilot Credits, and Windows 365 for Agents is eligible for Copilot Credits — and the spending limits that would cap that meter are still in preview. Agent governance itself is now a priced line: Agent 365, launched in May 2026 and already counting more than 50 million registered agents, costs $15 a seat for third-party agent insights, Defender risk signals and Purview protections, while basic usage insights for Microsoft-built agents are now free with a qualifying subscription. The quietest change lands at renewal: from November 1, 2026, new and renewed Power Platform and Dynamics 365 licences no longer include seeded AI Builder credits, so AI work that ran on included capacity now draws on metered Copilot Credits — licences bought before that date keep their credits to the end of the term. And from October 1, Microsoft began applying a 5% cost-of-capital uplift to annual-term CSP software subscriptions billed monthly (server products first; per-user subscriptions have carried the same 5% premium since April 1, 2025), which is a charge for paying monthly, not a price change — but one many estates pay without having chosen it.

## 2. [GitHub made the Copilot seat free — and bills from the first use](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/get-started/whats-changing-with-github-enterprise-and-copilot?utm_source=ghec-changelog-enterprise-access-docs&utm_medium=changelog&utm_campaign=universe-2026)

**Source:** GitHub Docs, announced October 28, 2026 at GitHub Universe; plan comparison verified against GitHub's pricing pages on November 17, 2026: [docs.github.com](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/get-started/whats-changing-with-github-enterprise-and-copilot?utm_source=ghec-changelog-enterprise-access-docs&utm_medium=changelog&utm_campaign=universe-2026) and [datastudios.org](https://www.datastudios.org/post/github-copilot-business-vs-github-copilot-enterprise-comparison)

On October 28, GitHub added a third way to buy Copilot for large customers, called Copilot access, and it inverts the model. Users with a GitHub Enterprise Cloud licence pay a $0 seat charge; users without one pay $10 a month for Copilot Standalone — and in both cases metered usage is billed from the first request, with no included allowance. The features are the same as Copilot Enterprise, and the transition is voluntary: existing Business and Enterprise seats stay as they are. The arithmetic underneath is the reveal. A Copilot Business seat costs $19 a month and includes 1,900 pooled AI credits; Enterprise costs $39 and includes 3,900 — and at $0.01 a credit, each seat price is exactly the dollar value of the credits it includes. So the seat was never really an access charge; it was a prepaid usage commitment with features attached, and the new model simply removes the prepayment floor. The catch is scope and control: Copilot access is available today only to Enterprise Cloud customers working with a GitHub or Microsoft account team, self-serve opens in stages, and an enterprise cannot mix the new access with Business or Enterprise seats — so a buyer cannot move only its light users. The renewal question changes with it: from how many seats to buy to how much AI usage to budget, with no prepaid cushion underneath.

## 3. [Meta's ads cost 23% more in the US and Canada — and a new setting may make the rise look smaller](https://falia.co/en/insights/paid-advertising/facebook-instagram-ads-cost/)

**Source:** Falia, checked November 13, 2026, on Meta's third-quarter results (October 21, 2026) and PPC Land reporting: [falia.co](https://falia.co/en/insights/paid-advertising/facebook-instagram-ads-cost/)

The average price of a Meta ad rose 23% year over year in the United States and Canada in the third quarter of 2026, against 14% worldwide, while impressions rose only 8% in the region and 10% worldwide — so in North America the price rose almost three times faster than the number of ads delivered. Meta's chief financial officer, Susan Li, attributed the rise on the October 21 analyst call to ads performing better and to better economic conditions: when an ad converts better, an advertiser can pay more for the same impression and keep the same cost per sale, and in an auction everyone does this at once. The number that matters for a small advertiser is not the average but their own cost per sale, which can stay flat while the average rises. The fresh twist is measurement: since November 12, 2026, Meta has been turning on by default a setting that measures some conversions on the customer's device, and reported conversions could rise — making cost per result look lower — without any change in actual sales. Falia found no Meta help page on the setting as of November 13. The figures are from Meta's October 21 earnings, so they are a month old; the measurement change is the November event, and it arrives days before Black Friday, when ad prices peak.

## 4. [Britain's competition watchdog is investigating what Meta charges businesses — in data, not money](https://laweconcenter.org/resources/the-watchdog-and-the-web-crawler/)

**Source:** International Center for Law & Economics, Nov 18, 2026, on the CMA announcement of October 30, 2026: [laweconcenter.org](https://laweconcenter.org/resources/the-watchdog-and-the-web-crawler/)

Earlier this year Meta proposed new commercial terms for businesses on Facebook and Instagram: to keep doing business on the platforms, companies would have had to let Meta's software scan their websites and use what it found to develop its own products. German publishers called the proposal "blatant theft"; their British counterparts called it "an outrageous assault on journalism". On October 30, the UK Competition and Markets Authority announced it was investigating whether Meta's commercial terms amount to an abuse of dominance under the UK Competition Act. Meta has since shelved the update, but the CMA is pressing ahead and says it will examine Meta's existing terms and any revisions. The economics here is the holdup problem: a business that spent years building an audience on a platform loses much of that investment if it leaves, and the platform can use that leverage to demand more — which is why the price Meta charges is not always a number on an invoice. The piece argues the holdup theory is a poor fit here, since businesses that read the fine print got Meta to rewrite it and the clause would have left publishers free to license the same material to rivals — but it also notes what remains is a dispute over how much Meta can ask in return for its services, and settling that would put Britain's competition watchdog in the business of setting prices.

## 5. [A $3 pizza became $27.11 at checkout — and Reddit split over who was fooled](https://www.thecooldown.com/green-business/uber-eats-pizza-delivery-hidden-fees/)

**Source:** The Cool Down, Nov 9, 2026, on a Reddit r/MildlyInfuriating thread: [thecooldown.com](https://www.thecooldown.com/green-business/uber-eats-pizza-delivery-hidden-fees/)

A shopper found an Uber Eats promotion for a pizza normally priced at $25 for just $3 — then discovered the restaurant's $14 minimum meant adding breadsticks, and the checkout total came to $27.11 before tip. The poster deleted the cart and ordered from a different app, writing that it felt shady to offer a $3 pizza that would actually cost at least $17. The thread then split in a way that matters for pricing research: some commenters called the fees predatory and said the apps were unaffordable, while others said expecting a $3 pizza delivered was unrealistic and the problem was the shopper's own arithmetic. The same pattern shows up elsewhere in the piece: Flashfood users questioned a 99-cent service fee on orders under $12, and other customers reported delivery fees nearly doubling a $22 McDonald's order. The pricing point is the partition: the $3 headline, the $14 minimum, the fees and the tip are each priced separately, so the buyer discovers the real price only at checkout — and the disagreement in the thread is itself the finding, since one group reads the same partitioned price as deception and the other as a deal they should have understood.

## 6. [East Carolina set 2027-28 tuition: undergraduates up 3%, medical school up 8.7%](https://news.ecu.edu/2026/11/13/trustees-approve-degree-conferral-for-fall-graduates-set-2027-28-tuition/)

**Source:** East Carolina University news, Nov 13, 2026 (Board of Trustees meeting): [news.ecu.edu](https://news.ecu.edu/2026/11/13/trustees-approve-degree-conferral-for-fall-graduates-set-2027-28-tuition/)

East Carolina University's Board of Trustees approved tuition increases for the 2027-28 academic year on November 13, and the pattern is a tiered one. Undergraduate tuition rises 3%, about $138 a year, but only for incoming students in fall 2027 — current in-state undergraduates see no rise under the UNC system's fixed tuition policy. Graduate tuition rises 5%, most of the revenue earmarked for new graduate assistantships, while the Brody School of Medicine rises 8.7% and the School of Dental Medicine 10.1%. No mandatory fees increase, but on-campus housing rises 4%. The proposal now goes to the UNC System Board of Governors for approval, so it is a board vote, not yet a final price. The pricing point is the segmentation: the university charges the increase to the students with the least information about what the degree is worth — incoming undergraduates — while protecting the ones already enrolled, and charges the steepest rises to the professional schools where the degree is a licence to a high-paying job.

## 7. [Cash GLP-1 prices split in two: a $59 compounded floor and a $299 brand floor](https://glpchart.com/news/)

**Source:** GLP Chart, November 2026 Cash Price Index (published Nov 2, covering October 2026) and weekly moves to Nov 15: [glpchart.com](https://glpchart.com/news/)

The November cash-price index for GLP-1 drugs, published November 2, shows two markets where there used to be one. The median all-in cash price across 32 priced programs was $299 a month, and the brand-name floor held at $299 — but the lowest verified compounded semaglutide price fell to $59 a month, from $198 in September, after a new program, Collective, entered the chart at that price. The brand makers are meanwhile moving their own self-pay prices in both directions: NovoCare sells the Wegovy pill for $149 a month at 1.5 mg, and its $199 offer on a new patient's first two Wegovy shot fills ends December 31, after which the shot is $349 a month; Lilly's Foundayo rises from $149 to $199 a month at 2.5 mg on January 1 (and $199 to $299 at 5.5 mg). The gap between list and cash is the other story: a retail pharmacy pays about $1,302 for a month of Wegovy, according to the federal acquisition-cost file, while the lowest verified cash price is $299. And the public backstop is expanding: eligible Medicare Part D members pay a $50 monthly copay for Wegovy, Foundayo or the Zepbound KwikPen under the Medicare GLP-1 Bridge, which runs through December 31, 2027. GLP Chart is an independent price tracker that checks each program's price daily; the compounded figures are cash-pay prices for compounded drugs, not list prices for the branded products.

## 8. [Regulators approved 2027 health insurance rises of 14.8% on the marketplace and 12.7% for employers](https://www.savewithbenefits.com/2027-health-plan-rates)

**Source:** Save With Benefits compilation of CMS rate-review filings, pulled November 1, 2026; corroborated by KFF via Marketplace, Nov 18, 2026: [savewithbenefits.com](https://www.savewithbenefits.com/2027-health-plan-rates) and [marketplace.org](https://www.marketplace.org/story/2026/11/18/why-your-health-plan-will-cost-more-in-2027)

Regulators have now decided 613 of the 615 rate filings for 2027, and the approved numbers are close to what insurers asked for: a median increase of 14.8% on marketplace plans and 12.7% on employer group plans, against requests of 15.3% and 14.5%. The national median hides a 25-point spread: approved marketplace increases run from 3.0% in Vermont to 28.0% in Arizona, and group increases from 3.6% in Vermont to 24.3% in Ohio. Regulators did cut some asks sharply — New York from 20.3% requested to 8.0% approved, Rhode Island from 17.8% to 10.6%, Connecticut from 17.8% to 12.4% — but in 18 states they approved more than was asked, led by Oregon (12.3% to 19.9%) and Minnesota (12.3% to 17.3%). The ceiling on what a household can be exposed to is rising too: the 2027 out-of-pocket maximum is $12,000 self-only and $24,000 for a family, up 13% from $10,600 and $21,200. KFF's separate analysis puts the benchmark silver plan up 13% for 2027 on top of 25% last year, with employer plans up 8–10%, and notes the enhanced subsidies that expired at the end of 2025 are the reason the sticker price now lands harder. One caveat: the state table is a benefits broker's compilation of CMS filings, not a CMS publication itself, so treat the medians as compiled, not official.

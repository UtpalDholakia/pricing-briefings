## Key takeaways

- Evaluate a discount together with the rights the customer gives up.
- Promotion length, billing frequency and commitment length are different things.
- Separating a fee can communicate who is responsible for a cost, not just make the base price look smaller.

## What happened

### 1. Marriott prices the booking channel as well as the room
**Source:** [Marriott Cyber Sale terms](https://www.marriott.com/offers/last-minute-hotel-deals/terms-and-conditions.mi), booking window November 24–December 1, 2026; checked November 24.

Marriott's sale offers most public bookings 15% off the standard room rate, versus 25% for members booking through the app's offer landing page. Both require prepayment and are non-refundable, non-changeable and non-cancellable. Participating properties, dates and brands are restricted. The deeper discount rewards a specific booking route, not membership alone. It also comes with a commitment cost. My interpretation is that this sorts customers by both channel willingness and confidence in their travel plans. A percentage saving against the standard rate does not establish a saving against the best flexible alternative.

### 2. cPanel advertises a sale beside a future renewal-price notice
**Sources:** [cPanel pricing](https://www.cpanel.net/pricing/), checked November 24, 2026; [2027 licensing guide](https://www.cpanel.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/cPanel_Store_Licensing_Guide_2027.pdf), valid December 16, 2026.

cPanel advertises 50% off new licenses until December 7. Solo displays $13.73 per month with annual billing, excluding VAT. The same page warns that a December 16 adjustment affects new orders and subsequent renewals. Its linked guide lists Solo at $32.99 monthly or $362.89 annually from that date. These are different billing bases, not numbers to compare directly. The sale can establish a low reference price before the renewal. Put first-term cost and the applicable renewal schedule together. The exact future charge for an individual promotional purchase was not verified at checkout.

### 3. HBO Max separates a twelve-month discount from annual billing
**Source:** [HBO Max offer FAQ](https://help.hbomax.com/US-en/answer/Detail/000002567), November 23–30, 2026 offer; checked November 24.

Eligible new and lapsed US subscribers can get Basic with Ads for $5.49 monthly plus tax for twelve months. Billing renews monthly, and cancellation leaves access through the current billing cycle. This is not a prepaid annual plan. After the promotion, renewal uses the then-current monthly rate, currently $10.99, not a guaranteed future price. Changing plans forfeits the offer. The design provides a long price incentive without collecting a year upfront. Whether that creates a habit or simply delays cancellation requires cohort data. Initial signup counts cannot settle it.

**Tags:** "subscription pricing", "Price Promotions", "Subscription Renewals"

### 4. Thanksgiving does not command a campground premium everywhere
**Sources:** [Modern Campground on Insider Perks' November index](https://moderncampground.com/usa/u-s-campground-rate-increases-cool-ahead-of-thanksgiving-state-pricing-remains-uneven/), November 23, 2026; [index publisher](https://insiderperks.com/ohpi/).

Insider Perks reports Thanksgiving campground rates 2.7% above nearby ordinary weekends nationally. Arizona's premium is 8.7%, while four states show lower holiday rates. The comparison uses each site type's Thanksgiving Friday rate against ordinary Fridays within three weeks on either side. These are advertised rates, not paid prices or measured willingness to pay. The report comes from a commercial industry provider, not an independent audit. Its useful lesson is to question the automatic holiday markup. Test local booking pace and displacement before applying the same premium across a portfolio.

**Tags:** "Dynamic pricing", "Hospitality", "High demand"

### 5. A carbon-offset fee and a shipping fee send different signals
**Sources:** [Goenka and Bagchi, Journal of Consumer Research](https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucae026), first published April 8, 2024, February 2025 issue; [public paper](https://mkwu.people.ust.hk/assignment/2026_06.pdf), study 3C.

Older discovery: in a preregistered, incentivized meal-kit choice experiment, the target brand's choice share was 40.1% with an all-inclusive price and 27.6% when carbon-offset was separated. Separating shipping left choice at 40.1%. The wider paper links socio-moral surcharges to buyers perceiving the firm as avoiding responsibility. The result distinguishes the fee's purpose from partitioning alone. This was an online choice with a prize incentive, not observed subscription retention. Do not infer that every shipping surcharge is harmless or that every environmental charge will backfire.

## Why it matters

My reading is that buyers evaluate more than the amount paid. They also evaluate restrictions, timing and the division of responsibility. A discount or surcharge can change those judgments even when its arithmetic is clear.

## Ideas to apply

- Show first payment, renewal basis and lost flexibility beside the promotional price.
- Compare holiday rates with nearby equivalent dates, then check conversion and contribution, not posted premiums alone.
- Test whether customers understand a fee as a service cost or a responsibility the seller is shifting to them.

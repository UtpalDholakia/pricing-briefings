## 1. [Meta now charges Facebook Pages to link out — $50 a month for eight links](https://awespirestudios.com/meta-link-limits-organic-posts/)

**Source:** Awespire Studios, US, Oct 31, 2026, updated Nov 10, citing Engadget, TechCrunch, Social Media Today and Meta's help pages: [awespirestudios.com](https://awespirestudios.com/meta-link-limits-organic-posts/)

Facebook Pages are now limited to two posts with links per month unless they pay for Meta One. The limit spread to far more Pages when Meta One launched on September 15, 2026, after a test that began in December 2025. The cheapest business tier, Essential at $14.99 a month, buys no extra links at all; Advanced at $49.99 buys eight a month, Expert at $149.99 buys twenty, and Max at $499.99 is unlimited. Links in comments count toward the limit, a bare domain with no "https://" counts, and the count resets on the first of the month — while links in ads and links to Meta's own apps are unlimited, and personal profiles and publisher Pages are exempt. Nonprofits are caught too: Engadget reported a city library and a pet food pantry hitting the limit, and one nonprofit marketer called $50 for eight links "crazy." The pricing point is the exit toll: Meta's own figures say 98.7% of US post views in early 2026 had no outside link, down from about 1 in 8 feed views five years ago to about 1 in 77 — the platform is now charging for the right to send your own followers to your own site.

## 2. [One in three Amazon Black Friday "deals" was no cheaper than the month before](https://groundworkcollaborative.org/work/amazon/)

**Source:** Groundwork Collaborative, US, Nov 16, 2026, prices tracked via Keepa; Amazon's response as reported by USA Today, Nov 16: [groundworkcollaborative.org](https://groundworkcollaborative.org/work/amazon/)

Groundwork examined 351 products Amazon itself promoted as its best Black Friday and Cyber Monday deals in 2024 and 2025, comparing each sale price with the lowest price in the run-up. Nearly one in three, 29.1%, was priced the same or higher during the sale than the lowest price in the 30 days before it; against a 90-day window the share rises to 57.3%. Nearly one in ten deals, 9.4%, was actually priced higher than the month before, and 27% were higher than the 90-day low. The examples are mundane, which is the point: Beats Solo 4 headphones "on sale" for $129.95 had already cost $129.95 twelve days earlier, and a Fitbit Charge 6 at $99.95 had cost the same twenty days before Cyber Monday. Amazon called the report "deeply flawed", saying it cherry-picked from more than 10 million deals and that its own research found 98% of the products at or below pre-sale prices. Groundwork is a progressive think tank, so read the framing accordingly — but the method is checkable, and it pairs with the Brazil finding this briefing covered on Nov 17, where only 30.3% of Black Friday offers were real discounts: the same pattern, on two continents, in the same week.

## 3. [A $13.32 Harvest bill became $133.98 — and the price page cannot explain why](https://www.stateofindiehacking.com/stories/harvest-price-increase-bending-spoons/)

**Source:** State of Indie Hacking, Nov 2026, drawing on aphyr's Nov 8 post, the BBC (Aug 20) and a Hacker News thread (Nov 9): [stateofindiehacking.com](https://www.stateofindiehacking.com/stories/harvest-price-increase-bending-spoons/)

Kyle Kingsbury, a self-employed contractor who has billed through Harvest for about a decade, wrote on November 8 that his monthly bill rose from $13.32 to $133.98 after Bending Spoons, which acquired Harvest in July 2025, moved customers onto a new "enterprise" plan with "unlimited" usage billing — for, as far as he can tell, exactly what he had before. The BBC's August report found the same pattern at larger scale: a UK consultancy's bill rose from $130 to $2,110 a month, a US customer's annual charge from $2,800 to $23,000, with rises of as much as 1,500%. The public pricing page does not resolve the argument: it lists "starting from" prices of $9 a seat a month for Teams and $14 for Enterprise, while usage fees for projects, clients, tasks and invoicing sit on top and the Unlimited usage fee is not published. Commenters on Hacker News disputed the $133.98 figure itself, pointing out that no such plan exists on the page, while others posted their own renewal emails — one Basic plan at $40 a month renewing at $586.50. Harvest's spokesperson told the BBC the largest increases hit outdated legacy plans, with 30 days' notice by email. The figures are reported customer bills, not a published rate card, and Harvest has not said how many customers were moved — which is itself the pricing story: a "starting from" seat price plus an unpublished usage meter is a price you cannot compute until the bill arrives.

## 4. [The "AI tax" at renewal: vendors ask 20–37%, buyers who push back land near 12%](https://www.mysterydemo.com/blog/saas-price-increase-statistics)

**Source:** Mystery Demo's roundup of published 2026 procurement research, citing Tropic's renewal data and Vertice's October 2026 data: [mysterydemo.com](https://www.mysterydemo.com/blog/saas-price-increase-statistics)

Tropic, which negotiates software contracts from its own deal data, puts AI-driven renewal increases at 20% to 37%, against a typical annual uplift of 3% to 9% — and where buyers pushed back, the ask fell by about 55% on average, landing near 12%. The increase arrives through four tactics Tropic names: forced migration into new AI-inclusive packages with no option to renew at the old price, unbundling then rebundling AI add-ons, credit-based pricing that resists benchmarking, and conditional discounts that only apply if the buyer takes the AI add-ons. Vertice's data adds the quiet version: 27% of vendors cut features or service levels while holding or raising price in the second quarter of 2026, and 70% of tail-spend contracts auto-renewed in the third quarter without any review. Zylo's 2026 index found 78% of IT leaders reported unexpected charges tied to AI features or consumption pricing in the past year. These are reported net outcomes from procurement datasets, not list prices — no vendor publishes the uplift, which is why the renewal quote, not the price page, is where enterprise SaaS pricing now happens.

## 5. [Oracle cut Java prices by up to 94% — and still charges for people who never touch Java](https://medium.com/licenseware/oracle-cut-java-prices-by-up-to-94-it-still-counts-every-employee-10271b0199a3)

**Source:** LICENSEWARE, Nov 16, 2026, reading Oracle's Java SE Universal Subscription Global Price List dated Oct 8, 2026: [medium.com](https://medium.com/licenseware/oracle-cut-java-prices-by-up-to-94-it-still-counts-every-employee-10271b0199a3)

Oracle's October price list rewrote the bottom half of its Java pricing: a 10-person organisation now pays $100 a year instead of $1,800, down 94%, and a 500-person one pays $10,000 instead of $90,000, down 89%. The cut runs from 12.5% to 94% depending on headcount below 3,000 employees; above 3,000, nothing changed, with enterprise bands starting at $10.50 per employee per month. What did not change is the metric that makes Java bills hurt: the price is set by whole headcount — full-time, part-time and temporary staff, plus the staff of agents, contractors and outsourcers supporting internal operations — "without regard to the number of Employees that use the Programs." One licensable Oracle JDK install is enough to put the entire headcount on the meter, and the new small-business bands are direct-only, so a reseller cannot quote them. These are published list prices, and the cut is real — but it changes the rate, not the logic: a 94% cut on a metric that counts people who never open Java.

## 6. [Bengaluru's cheapest bus ticket rose 67% overnight — the shortest trips rose most](https://thecsrjournal.in/karnataka-increases-bus-fares-by-up-to-rs-10-effective-november-4-citing-rising-diesel-prices-and-financial-challenges)

**Source:** The CSR Journal, India, Nov 4, 2026: [thecsrjournal.in](https://thecsrjournal.in/karnataka-increases-bus-fares-by-up-to-rs-10-effective-november-4-citing-rising-diesel-prices-and-financial-challenges)

Karnataka raised fares across all four of its state-run bus corporations from midnight on November 4, announced by Transport Minister Byrathi Suresh. Bengaluru's city buses (BMTC) rose 33% to 35% on average, and the other three corporations about 23%. The minimum BMTC fare went from ₹6 to ₹10, a 67% rise, while the maximum ordinary fare went from ₹35 to ₹45; the air-conditioned Vajra maximum rose from ₹75 to ₹90 and the airport Vayu Vajra maximum from ₹405 to ₹425. A Bengaluru-to-Nelamangala ordinary fare rose from ₹35 to ₹44, and Majestic to Shivajinagar from ₹12 to ₹18. The minister cited diesel prices and the corporations' finances, saying "profit is not our motive" and that the aim was to save the institutions — while free travel for eligible women under the Shakti scheme continues on non-luxury buses. The pricing point is the shape of the rise: fares are set stage by stage, so the shortest trips, the ones the poorest riders take, carry the largest percentage increase.

## 7. [Three tuition boards in one week gave three different answers to the same cost pressure](https://news.ecu.edu/2026/11/13/trustees-approve-degree-conferral-for-fall-graduates-set-2027-28-tuition/)

**Source:** East Carolina University News, Nov 13, 2026; West Virginia University Today, Nov 13; University of Hawaiʻi News, Nov 19: [news.ecu.edu](https://news.ecu.edu/2026/11/13/trustees-approve-degree-conferral-for-fall-graduates-set-2027-28-tuition/)

East Carolina's trustees approved a 3% undergraduate tuition rise for incoming students in fall 2027, about $138 a year, while current in-state students pay nothing more under the system's fixed-tuition policy; graduate tuition rises 5%, medical school 8.7% and dental 10.1%, and on-campus housing rises 4%. West Virginia set its 2027-28 rates nine months early: resident undergraduates pay $216 more per semester and non-residents $636 more, with graduate tuition up $252 and $666 respectively — explicitly so that acceptance letters can carry real numbers instead of a price decided the following summer. Hawaiʻi went the other way: its regents voted 7–2 on November 19 to freeze tuition across all ten campuses for two years, then raise it 5% in 2029-30, with only the pharmacy college rising 5% a year from 2027; tuition there is lower in real terms than a decade ago, and all 35 members of the public who testified opposed any increase. Same cost pressure, three answers: raise now and protect current students, raise now but announce early so the price is certain, or freeze now and raise later.

## 8. [Washington approved a 16.7% small-business health premium rise — slightly more than insurers asked for](https://www.piawest.com/news-releases-and-bulletins/nine-insurers-approved-to-sell-2027-small-group-health-plans-in-washington)

**Source:** Professional Insurance Agents Western Alliance, Nov 4, 2026, on the Washington Office of the Insurance Commissioner: [piawest.com](https://www.piawest.com/news-releases-and-bulletins/nine-insurers-approved-to-sell-2027-small-group-health-plans-in-washington)

Washington approved nine insurers to sell 2027 small-group health plans, for businesses with fewer than 50 employees that are not legally required to offer benefits. The insurers requested an average increase of 16.5%; the Office of the Insurance Commissioner approved an average of 16.7%, after an actuarial review of each company's financial projections. The commissioner called the nine-way choice a positive sign while adding that "as a country we need a more large-scale solution to address healthcare costs." The national context, from KFF's annual survey as cited by PIA: family coverage now averages $28,303 a year, up 5% from 2025, and what a worker pays for single coverage averages $1,672, up 15.8%. The approval is the story, not just the number: the regulator is required by law to approve increases the insurer can justify, so the 16.7% is a floor of justification, not a ceiling on the rise — and it still came in above the ask.

## 9. [The average wedding costs $34,200 — a number that describes no actual wedding](https://www.theknot.com/content/average-wedding-cost)

**Source:** The Knot 2026 Real Weddings Study, surveying 10,474 US couples married in 2025: [theknot.com](https://www.theknot.com/content/average-wedding-cost)

The Knot's 2026 study puts the average US wedding at $34,200, for the ceremony and reception, excluding the ring and honeymoon. But the study's own brackets show the average is a blend of different purchases: couples with budgets up to $15,000 spent $8,900 on average, those in the $15,000–$40,000 bracket spent $26,400, and those over $40,000 spent $70,300. Geography does the same work: New York City averages $88,000 and New Jersey $57,000, against $17,000 in Wyoming and $18,000 in Idaho and Utah. The venue alone averages $12,900, and the average guest count is 117 at $292 a head. The pricing point is that an average across a bimodal distribution is the least informative number on the page: a couple budgeting $34,200 is budgeting for a wedding almost nobody actually has.

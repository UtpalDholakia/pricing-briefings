## 1. Nine-ending prices slowed tariff pass-through, so inflation showed up in round-ish prices instead

**Source:** SSRN (CESifo WP 13056), Hirshman (Cornell) & Ignatenko (York), posted Oct 19 · [papers.ssrn.com](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7622838)

The 2025 tariffs reached shelves mostly through prices that did not end in 9. Prices ending in 9 had much lower pass-through, and annual inflation ran higher for non-9 items, including products that were never tariffed. This is left-digit bias working as a macro friction: firms seem to protect price thresholds and push cost shocks onto other SKUs. That opens a question about cross-subsidy inside the assortment and about which consumers end up paying for threshold protection.

## 2. LLM pricing agents can hold prices up by setting a high anchor and undercutting it now and then

**Source:** arXiv 2610.21711, Bichler, Hierhager, Köck & Pieroth, Oct 15 · [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21711)

In repeated Bertrand games, LLM agents that see raw price histories (rivals' prices included) can keep prices above competitive levels in self-play. Agents fed summarized profit histories drift toward competitive prices. When the two designs compete, one sets a high reference price and periodically cuts below it, so both sellers stay above marginal cost. The finding is that the interface the agent is given, not only the algorithm, decides market outcomes. Reference-price dynamics come out of agent design rather than consumer memory.

**Type:** Research · **Tags:** Academic research, Algorithmic Pricing, AI in Pricing, Behavioral Economics, Marginal Costs

## 3. AI booking agents differ more than tenfold in price sensitivity, and weaker ones can be steered by listing order

**Source:** arXiv 2609.31468 (PriceBench), Pavel Kireyev, Sept 25 (older, but relevant to item 2) · [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31468)

Across 28 LLMs given the same hotel-booking tasks, price sensitivity varied by more than an order of magnitude, even within a single model family. Average booked nightly rates ranged from $247 to $393. Less capable models either fixated on one listing position or chose almost at random. If agents start doing the buying, much of what we know about consumer price response gets handed to model choice, and position effects become something sellers can exploit.

**Type:** Research · **Tags:** Academic research, AI in Pricing, Hospitality, Consumer decision making

## 4. Second Circuit hears retailers' challenge to New York's "your personal data" price warning

**Source:** Courthouse News, Oct 19 · [courthousenews.com](https://www.courthousenews.com/retailers-slam-surveillance-pricing-warning-label-in-second-circuit-appeal/)

The NRF argues that the mandated all-caps notice ("THIS PRICE WAS SET BY AN ALGORITHM USING YOUR PERSONAL DATA") is accurate but misleading: shoppers may assume sensitive identifiers are involved, and may not realize personalized prices can be lower. Judge Chin seemed skeptical and asked whether merchants could simply add explanatory context. For consumer researchers, the case turns on an empirical question nobody has answered well: what inferences does a disclosure like this trigger about fairness and price level? That is a ready-made experiment.

## 5. Web-scraped data: about half of US dynamic price changes in 2025 were cuts

**Source:** Fortune (AP), Oct 18 · [fortune.com](https://fortune.com/2026/10/18/dynamic-pricing-surveillance-prices-consumer-data-digital-labels-algorithmic/)

Decodo, a web-data firm, tracked 2025 price changes in electronics, apparel and home improvement and found roughly half were decreases. That cuts against the public belief that dynamic pricing only goes up. The methodology is barely described, so treat it as a prompt rather than evidence. Still, the gap between how often prices actually fall and how strongly people believe "algorithm means higher price" fits asymmetric attention to price increases and hostile attributions.

## 6. Court lets FTC's Ticketmaster drip-pricing case proceed: deceiving at "first contact" is enough, and "+Fees" is too vague

**Source:** All About Advertising Law (Manatt), Oct 14 · [allaboutadvertisinglaw.com](https://www.allaboutadvertisinglaw.com/2026/10/court-allows-ftc-drip-pricing-lawsuit-against-ticketmaster-to-proceed.html)

The court held that a misleading upfront price can violate the FTC Act even when the full total appears before purchase, partly because shoppers filter and compare on the fee-free number. Fees could add up to 44%. A Maryland court came out the other way under state law, finding no reliance once the total was shown. The ruling puts into law what behavioral work on partitioned pricing has long argued: the anchor does its harm at search, not at checkout. Related evidence: Jin, Wagman & Xing (SSRN, Aug) find that California's all-in rule for short-term rentals made guests more price-sensitive, yet average nightly rates rose 4.8% as fee-dependent listings left the market. ## 7. Olo launches a delivery app built on in-store menu prices plus a flat 5% fee

**Source:** Mezha, Oct 20 · [mezha.net](https://mezha.net/eng/news/mezha-b3a56ecd10988e0716be7d84_olo_launches_nationwide)

Olo's new consumer app (about 250 brands at launch) charges the same menu prices as in-store and adds a visible 5% service fee plus delivery. Incumbent platforms tend to bury their 20-30% commissions in higher menu prices. This is a live test of whether consumers prefer an explicit surcharge on an honest base price or a hidden markup with smaller visible fees. It pits partitioned-price transparency against the pain of seeing a fee line.

## 8. Fall shrinkflation roundup: store brands shrink too, and one oatmeal raised calories per serving after cutting packets

**Source:** Mouseprint (Edgar Dworsky), Oct 17 · [mouseprint.org](https://www.mouseprint.org/2026/10/17/here-we-shrink-again-fall-2026-part-1/)

New downsizes include Hershey's Kisses (10 to 8.7 oz), Ziploc freezer bags (16 fewer per box), Dixie Ultra plates (35 to 30), and Stop & Shop's private-label oatmeal (10 to 8 packets). Readers noticed the oatmeal's per-serving calories went up, which may be a reformulation that makes per-serving comparisons harder. Private labels following national brands complicates the usual "value refuge" view of store brands, and it fits recent scanner evidence that size elasticity is close to zero.

## 9. Credits as a currency with an exchange rate the vendor controls: how AI is reshaping B2B software pricing

**Source:** Security Boulevard (Deepak Gupta, syndicated), Oct 17 · [securityboulevard.com](https://securityboulevard.com/2026/10/how-to-buy-software-when-ai-breaks-pricing/)

Gupta argues that seat pricing breaks once agents do the work, and he ranks credits, consumption meters and outcome fees by how closely the billing unit tracks value. He warns that credits let vendors quietly change exchange rates and let unused balances expire. The author runs a vendor, so read it with that in mind. The useful point is that SaaS credits are rebuilding the psychology of in-game currencies in enterprise software: decoupling from money, breakage, and opaque conversion rates. Consumer research on tokens should apply directly.

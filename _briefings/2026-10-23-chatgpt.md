## Key takeaways

- Walmart gives customers a high comparison total and a smaller monthly equivalent for the same annual charge.
- A per-subscriber fee takes a smaller share of creator revenue as a creator’s subscription price rises.
- A historical price can change whether an advertised saving looks like a deal.

## What happened

### 1. Walmart creates a remarkably aggressive bundle anchor

**Source:** [Walmart Corporate Newsroom](https://corporate.walmart.com/news/2026/10/meet-your-120-life-hack), October 20, 2026.

Walmart is offering Walmart+ and Sam’s Club Plus together for $120 annually, almost $100 below their previous combined cost. The package spans warehouse access, delivery, streaming, fuel savings, and rewards.

**Tags:** Price Bundling, Membership, Price presentation

### 2. Supercast prices its platform to encourage expensive creator tiers

**Sources:** [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/red-seat-ventures-chris-balfe-aims-substack-for-audio-creators-2026-10), October 23, 2026; [Supercast pricing](https://www.supercast.com/pricing).

Supercast is adding paid and free newsletters to its podcast subscription platform. Its base charge remains $0.59 per subscriber monthly, plus payment-processing fees, rather than a percentage of creator revenue.

**Tags:** Platform pricing, Tiered Pricing

### 3. Surcharges depend on what kind of relationship customers think they have

**Source:** [Psychology & Marketing](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mar.70152), April 22, 2026.

Across three experiments, partitioned pricing produced lower purchase intentions than an all-inclusive price when participants perceived an exchange-oriented brand relationship. The difference diminished under a communal relationship. Perceived surcharge fairness mediated the interaction.

**Tags:** Partitioned Pricing, Social processes

### 4. A truthful historical price can puncture the sale-price anchor

**Source:** [Journal of Consumer Policy](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10603-025-09607-z), December 26, 2025.

In a preregistered experiment with 500 UK participants, researchers added the lowest price from the preceding 30 days to eight advertised discounts. When that historical price was below the current offer, purchase intentions fell. The estimated decrease was larger for utilitarian goods than hedonic ones. Financial-information avoidance did not significantly moderate the effect.

**Tags:** Price Promotions, Disclosure

### 5. Even benevolent price discrimination can trigger costly defection

**Source:** [“Consumer Behavior under Benevolent Price Discrimination”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.03581), April 4, 2024. Older discovery; working paper.

Six incentivized studies involving 3,951 participants examined discounts reserved for low-income consumers. A substantial group paid a switching cost to avoid the discounting seller, despite suffering no absolute loss themselves. Explaining that income differences arose from luck or that the discount improved equality did not remove the aversion.

**Tags:** Price discrimination, Fairness

## Why it matters

**1. Walmart creates a remarkably aggressive bundle anchor** The behavioral move is broader than bundling. Walmart supplies a nearly $400 comparison basket, then reframes $120 as “$10 a month.” That creates both a high external anchor and a digestible periodic price. Practitioners should still model utilization. Minimum-order rules and ad-supported streaming make the experienced discount household-specific.

**2. Supercast prices its platform to encourage expensive creator tiers** That architecture makes the platform’s effective take rate fall as creators raise prices. It therefore encourages broad price ladders, including scarce superfan experiences. A proposed $10,000 celebrity-roast tier is only an idea, not evidence of demand. Still, such tiers can segment extreme willingness to pay and make ordinary premium plans look comparatively modest.

**3. Surcharges depend on what kind of relationship customers think they have** The proposed mechanism is concrete processing plus lower trust: customers scrutinize each component and ask whether the fee is justified. The practical implication is conditional. Loyal or community-oriented customers may tolerate partitioning, but transactional customers are more likely to treat a separately visible fee as evidence about the seller’s motives.

**4. A truthful historical price can puncture the sale-price anchor** The disclosure supplies a credible competing reference point and converts “saving” into perceived overpayment. The outcome was stated intention, however, not observed purchasing.

**5. Even benevolent price discrimination can trigger costly defection** The authors attribute much of the response to feeling unfairly passed over. Warm glow increased active support only when purchases could influence future discounts. For practitioners, benevolent intent may need participatory framing, not merely transparent eligibility rules.

## Ideas to apply

- Compare the annual bundle price with the benefits households are likely to use rather than the sum of available benefits.
- Test the same total price with a separate fee and an all-inclusive display. Check which customers see the fee as justified.
- Ask buyers to assess a sale with and without its recent price history. Measure completed purchases if you can.

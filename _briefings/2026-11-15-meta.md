## 1. Diwali phone sales fell 20% as festive discounts failed to beat earlier hikes

**Source:** My Mobile India, India, Nov 12, 2026: [mymobileindia.com](https://www.mymobileindia.com/diwali-smartphone-sales-fall-20-as-rising-prices-hurt-demand/)

Offline smartphone sales in India fell 18-20% year on year during Diwali, according to the All India Mobile Retailers Association, even though footfall held up. Memory-chip costs had pushed prices up through the year, so budget buyers under Rs 15,000 mostly stayed away or turned to refurbished phones, while demand concentrated in the Rs 30,000-60,000 range. Almost 60% of festive purchases were financed on EMI, and the association warned sales could fall by as much as 50% over the next three months.

## 2. Mexico's consumer watchdog built a price-history tool for Buen Fin

**Source:** Particle News, Mexico, Nov 9, 2026: [particle.news](https://particle.news/story/profeco-launches-pricecomparison-microsite-as-shoppers-rely-on-ai-ahead-of-el-buen-fin)

Profeco, Mexico's federal consumer agency, published a dedicated Buen Fin microsite tracking weekly prices for 490 products across about 400 stores, flagging differences as large as MXN 9,309 for a single item. The tool is aimed at the classic festival trick the other briefings have covered in the US and Europe but not in Latin America: raising a price before the sale so the discount looks bigger. Shoppers are also leaning on AI comparison tools, with search interest up roughly 400% in three months, according to a joint Ipsos-Google study cited in the report.

## 3. Japan raised food prices on 1,477 items in November, and labour is now the story

**Source:** Mynavi News, Japan (Japanese, translated), Nov 6, 2026: [news.mynavi.jp](https://news.mynavi.jp/article/20261106-5268845/)

Teikoku Databank counted 1,477 food and drink items raised in November by 195 major makers, about ten times the 150 items in November last year, at an average rise of 17% per item. Seasonings led with 644 items, followed by alcohol and drinks at 417. Raw-material costs were still cited in 91.7% of cases, but labour costs were cited in a record 51.5%, up from 26.5% in 2024. For the year as a whole, 20,327 items have been raised, the second straight year above 20,000.

## 4. South Africa asks who should pay for the wires when solar homes buy less power

**Source:** EnergyBee, South Africa, Nov 9, 2026: [energybee.co.za](https://energybee.co.za/news/nersa-municipal-tariff-review-solar-homes-2026)

On November 6, regulator NERSA published a consultation paper and draft framework to standardise more than 500 kinds of municipal electricity charges and to separate network charges from retail charges. It asks directly how rooftop solar, wheeling and customers leaving the grid should be reflected in tariffs so remaining customers are not unduly burdened. The live number to watch is Eskom's proposed 2027/28 tariff, an average 8.83% rise, with the Homepower 4 fixed charge rising from R535.80 to R660.90 a month; NERSA decides on November 26.

## 5. Florida State doubled one master's tuition, with no grandfathering for current students

**Source:** FSU News, US, Nov 15, 2026: [fsunews.com](https://www.fsunews.com/story/news/2026/11/15/fl-bog-approves-tuition-increases-for-3-fsu-masters-programs/92772638007/)

Florida's Board of Governors approved Florida State's proposal on November 13 to raise market-rate tuition for three online graduate business programs from fall 2027. The risk management and insurance master's rises 100%, from $600 to $1,200 per credit hour, taking the total from $19,800 to $39,600 before fees, while the management information systems master's and the online MBA each rise 60% to the same $1,200 per credit hour. The increase applies to current students as well as new ones, with no grandfathering at the original rates, and the university says it is working on tuition assistance to offset the change.

## 6. Medicare drug-plan premiums are set to rise 50% in 2027

**Source:** KFF Health News, US, Oct 30, 2026, updated Nov 4, 2026: [kffhealthnews.org](https://kffhealthnews.org/medicare/medicare-drug-plans-premium-hikes-affordability-trump-cms/)

A KFF analysis found average monthly premiums for stand-alone Medicare prescription drug plans will rise from $34 in 2026 to $51 in 2027, a 50% increase, with the average deductible rising from $540 to $643. About 10.4 million enrollees, or 76% of those in stand-alone plans without low-income subsidies, will pay more if they stay on the same plan, including 4.1 million now on zero-premium plans, for which no zero-premium plans will exist in 2027. One possible cause is the decision in July to end early a pilot that subsidised stand-alone premiums in 2025 and 2026. ## 7. A crochet seller raised her prices, then cut them back for her community

**Source:** Instagram, US, Nov 10, 2026: [instagram.com](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DfisKGdNShB/)

A crochet handbag seller on Instagram said she had first priced her bags at $175, then raised them to $175-225 after followers urged her to charge more for high-demand work, and has now permanently returned the price to $175. She said community support mattered more to her than the extra money and that she did not want to price people out of something she created. It is a small, public reversal of the usual creator-economy script, in which higher prices are treated as the only direction of travel, and it shows loyalty to early buyers acting as a real constraint on a rise.

## 8. A South African Black Friday speaker was R2,999, then R6,499, then R2,999 again

**Source:** Facebook, South Africa, Nov 13, 2026: [facebook.com](https://www.facebook.com/reel/2249822262255515/)

A South African news segment shared on Facebook tracked a speaker system priced at R2,999 on September 28, raised to R6,499 three days later, and then advertised on Black Friday at R2,999 with a claimed saving of R3,500, meaning the real saving against the earlier price was zero. Commenters largely called the practice misleading and said they now wishlist items months ahead to check true discounts, naming major local chains among the offenders. It is the same inflated-reference-price pattern the other briefings track in the US and Europe, documented here by a consumer-facing video rather than a regulator or a paper.

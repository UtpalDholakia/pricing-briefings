## 1. [Only 30.3% of Brazil's Black Friday deals are real discounts — and 20.8% cost more than usual](https://www.shoprime.com.br/black-friday/indice-de-precos)

**Source:** Shoprime, Brazil, Portuguese, updated Nov 17, 2026: [shoprime.com.br](https://www.shoprime.com.br/black-friday/indice-de-precos)

Shoprime compared 5,812 advertised Black Friday offers with each product's own price history, and only 30.3% were more than 5% below the product's typical price; 48.9% sat within 5% of it, 20.8% cost more than usual, and only 18.6% were at the lowest price ever recorded. Electronics (27.6% real discounts, 21.0% above typical) and fashion (22.3% real, 26.5% above typical) were worse than average. A fixed panel of 13,695 products checked against October medians found the median change was +0.0% — no broad pre-Black Friday inflation, but no broad fall either — and the real-discount share has fallen from 39.1% on Nov 12 to 30.3% on Nov 17 as the day approaches. The history is Shoprime's own record since August 2026, not the stores' official history, and coupons and freight are excluded.

## 2. [China's 11.11 ends at RMB 1,776.4 billion — and the platforms still won't say their own totals](https://longbridge.com/zh-HK/quote/JD.US/news/305290322)

**Source:** Syntun via PR Newswire, China, Chinese, Nov 12, 2026: [longbridge.com](https://longbridge.com/zh-HK/quote/JD.US/news/305290322)

Third-party monitor Syntun put total GMV across all online platforms for the October 7–November 11 festival at RMB 1,776.4 billion (about US$264.4 billion): e-commerce platforms accounted for RMB 1,679.0 billion, with Tmall ranked first, instant retail RMB 89.1 billion and community group buying RMB 8.3 billion. The platforms competed this year by simplifying promotions — Tmall replacing cross-store thresholds with official direct discounts, JD leading with spot goods and direct price cuts — and by pushing AI through the shopping journey and instant delivery, rather than by trumpeting a headline total, which the big platforms have not published since 2022.

## 4. [ACA benchmark premiums rise 13% for 2027 — the second double-digit jump in a row](https://www.kff.org/affordable-care-act/9-things-to-watch-for-the-2027-aca-open-enrollment-period/)

**Source:** KFF, US, Oct 30, 2026, corrected Nov 6, 2026: [kff.org](https://www.kff.org/affordable-care-act/9-things-to-watch-for-the-2027-aca-open-enrollment-period/)

KFF's analysis of finalized rates finds average benchmark silver premiums rising 13% for 2027 — 11% in states using HealthCare.gov and 16% in state-run marketplaces — after a 26% rise from 2025 to 2026, a cumulative 42% increase over two years. Insurers had proposed a median 15% increase, blaming higher underlying care costs and a worsening risk pool after the enhanced premium tax credits expired at the end of 2025; KFF estimates risk-pool changes added about four percentage points to premiums in 2026 and a similar amount again in 2027. The sting falls hardest above 400% of the federal poverty level, where buyers no longer qualify for any premium tax credit, and enrollment is already shrinking — plan selections fell by 1.2 million in 2026 and effectuated enrollment was down 12% (2.6 million people) to 19.2 million, with the CBO projecting marketplace enrollment falling from 22.4 million in 2025 to 13.1 million in 2027. This is distinct from yesterday's Medicare Part D item: a different program (ACA marketplaces, not Medicare) and a different mechanism (subsidy expiry plus risk-pool deterioration, not a premium schedule). Note: this item is 11 days old, included as strong research with finalized numbers.

## 5. [West Virginia University sets 2027-28 tuition early — resident undergraduates up $216 a semester](https://wvutoday.wvu.edu/stories/2026/11/13/to-provide-certainty-wvu-sets-tuition-housing-dining-rates-for-next-academic-year)

**Source:** WVU Today (West Virginia University), US, Nov 13, 2026: [wvutoday.wvu.edu](https://wvutoday.wvu.edu/stories/2026/11/13/to-provide-certainty-wvu-sets-tuition-housing-dining-rates-for-next-academic-year)

WVU's Board of Governors approved tuition, fees, housing and dining rates for 2027-28 on Nov 13 — months earlier than usual, explicitly so families see real numbers in acceptance letters instead of deciding blind. On the Morgantown campus, baseline undergraduate tuition rises $216 per semester for West Virginia residents and $636 for non-residents from fall 2027; graduate tuition rises $252 per semester for residents and $666 for non-residents, with university fees up $24 (undergraduate) and $27 (graduate). The regional campuses rise less in dollars — $144 and $180 for residents at Potomac State and WVU Tech respectively — while housing gets a new six-tier structure in which the cheapest tier actually falls $182 per semester and most other rooms rise about $266 per semester. The pricing story is the timing as much as the level: WVU is selling certainty itself, turning an early rate announcement into a recruiting pitch at a moment when applications and deposits are running ahead of prior years.

## 6. [Pakistan's regulator adds Rs2.37 a unit to November power bills — and its own members call the industrial rate "baseless"](https://customstoday.media/nepra-hikes-electricity-tariff-by-rs2-37-for-november-bills/)

**Source:** Customs Today, Pakistan, Nov 7, 2026: [customstoday.media](https://customstoday.media/nepra-hikes-electricity-tariff-by-rs2-37-for-november-bills/)

Pakistan's National Electric Power Regulatory Authority (NEPRA) approved a Rs2.37-per-unit increase under the September fuel price adjustment, to appear in consumers' November bills nationwide including Karachi's K-Electric customers; the Central Power Purchasing Agency had asked for Rs2.56. Separately, NEPRA raised the concessional rate for additional electricity use by industrial and agricultural consumers from Rs22.98 to Rs25.30 per unit (up Rs2.32), under the three-year Roshan Economy Power Package that prices only incremental consumption at the discounted rate. The decision passed only on the chairman's casting vote, with two members dissenting and calling the Rs25.30 rate baseless — a regulator publicly split over its own price. This is non-Western energy pricing of the kind Claude and ChatGPT have not covered, and it is distinct from yesterday's NERSA/Eskom item: a different country and a different mechanism, a monthly fuel adjustment rather than a tariff review.

## 7. [Patreon forced every remaining per-creation creator onto monthly billing on Nov 1 — and set their new prices for them](https://support.patreon.com/hc/en-us/articles/27992352573069-How-to-move-from-per-creation-to-subscription-billing)

**Source:** Patreon Help Center, US, switch effective Nov 1, 2026 (page re-checked Nov 17): [support.patreon.com](https://support.patreon.com/hc/en-us/articles/27992352573069-How-to-move-from-per-creation-to-subscription-billing)

Driven by Apple's requirement that all creators use subscription billing for iOS in-app purchases, Patreon automatically switched every creator still on legacy per-creation billing on November 1, 2026. By default each tier's per-creation price was multiplied by the highest number of paid posts the creator charged for in any single month over the previous three months. Billing stays paused, with no charges at all, until the creator logs in, reviews the new tier prices and resumes billing; tier prices cannot even be edited while billing is paused. One creator's Nov 10 post shows what that repricing looks like in practice: tiers moved from $3, $6, $25 and $100 per creation to $10, $15, $45 and $120 a month, with iOS-app subscribers charged more again to cover Apple's fee. Creators can apply a price change to new members only (grandfathering existing members) or to everyone, with one month's notice.

## 8. [An AI newsletter doubled its price in one day, $200 to $400 a year, and sold the old price as a deadline](https://ruben.substack.com/p/grapefruit)

**Source:** "How to AI" newsletter, Substack, Nov 11, 2026: [ruben.substack.com](https://ruben.substack.com/p/grapefruit)

The author of the "How to AI" Substack doubled the annual paid price from $200 to $400 a year on November 11, framing it as 55 cents a day becoming $1.10 a day — "still less than your coffee" — and offered the old $200 price only until Sunday, November 15 at 11:59pm ET, grandfathered for as long as the subscriber stays. The justification given is scope growth, not costs: the paid tier started as articles plus a Slack group, then added live workshops, hired two staff in September to teach Claude and Claude Code, added a Friday build-along session, and now has a 5,300+ member community. The author also cites $19,467 in negotiated discounts on AI tools passed to members at no cut, and offers an expense template for readers to send to their manager. It is a clean creator-economy case of a 100% rise sold on added value plus a grandfathered-price deadline.

A book-selling author on Threads pushed back sarcastically at Shopify's seasonal Black Friday preparation prompts, saying her margin is about two dollars per book, which makes any meaningful sale impossible, and telling the platform to stop urging her to discount. ## 10. [Salesforce's $50 Agentforce Coworker seat covers searches — but work it hands off bills separately](https://ctol.digital/news/salesforce-agentforce-coworker-pricing-tier/)

**Source:** CTOL Digital, Nov 5, 2026, citing Salesforce's pricing page and billing guide: [ctol.digital](https://ctol.digital/news/salesforce-agentforce-coworker-pricing-tier/)

Salesforce reported more than 185,000 Agentforce Coworker users on November 4, 2026, and its pricing page lists a dedicated Coworker licence at $50 per user per month, including all Coworker searches plus a pooled 30 MB indexing allowance per user per month that does not roll over. The catch is in the billing guide: when Coworker delegates execution to another agent, that work follows the other agent's billing terms, so a seat's searches are covered but downstream actions are not. The underlying meter is Flex Credits at $500 per 100,000 credits ($0.005 each), with a standard action at 20 credits ($0.10) and a voice action at 30 credits ($0.15); the page also lists an Agentforce User licence at $5 per user per month (Flex Credits still required), add-ons at $125, industry pricing at $150, and Max from $550. These are published list prices, read from Salesforce's own pricing page; net price not disclosed — and the 185,000-user figure does not show how many customers bought new standalone $50 seats, since Coworker access is also bundled into some editions.

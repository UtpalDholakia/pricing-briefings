## 1. [Google's $100 AI plan now buys ad-free YouTube for the whole household — at no extra cost](https://9to5google.com/2026/11/18/google-ai-ultra-youtube-premium-family/)

**Source:** 9to5Google, Nov 18, 2026; Google One community announcement: [9to5google.com](https://9to5google.com/2026/11/18/google-ai-ultra-youtube-premium-family/) and [support.google.com](https://support.google.com/googleone/thread/478234603/update-youtube-premium-family-is-now-included-with-google-ai-ultra?hl=en&sjid=4025927620881355695-NC)

On November 18, Google upgraded the YouTube benefit bundled into Google AI Ultra from a single-person Premium plan to YouTube Premium Family, at no extra cost, on both Ultra tiers — 5x at $99.99 a month and 20x at $199.99 a month. The subscriber plus up to five household members now get ad-free YouTube and YouTube Music, background play and downloads; a standalone Family plan costs $26.99 a month in the US, against $15.99 for the Individual plan Ultra included until now, so the bundled benefit gains $11 a month of list value, $132 a year, with no price change. Existing Ultra members are upgraded automatically, though the Ultra subscriber must be the Google family group manager to share it. The cheaper AI Pro tier at $19.99 a month keeps only Premium Lite, which excludes YouTube Music. The pricing point is the shape of competition at the top of the AI market: Google is not cutting Ultra's $100 price to match ChatGPT and Claude, it is widening the bundle around it, and the widening is aimed at the household, not the individual user — six ad-free accounts for one subscription.

## 2. [11.11 did not fail to deliver demand. It failed to deliver new people.](https://ecommercesea.substack.com/p/you-cant-bid-your-way-to-a-bigger)

**Source:** EcommerceSEA (Stan Chew), Nov 22, 2026, on day-by-day trackers for ten brands across Malaysia, Singapore, the Philippines and Vietnam: [ecommercesea.substack.com](https://ecommercesea.substack.com/p/you-cant-bid-your-way-to-a-bigger)

A Southeast Asian ecommerce consultancy went through the 11.11 trackers its teams filled in daily for ten brands — batteries, tyres, footwear, coffee, home and pet care, men's hair care, wellness, Korean beauty and two smaller local brands — and the short version is the headline. Traffic was the constraint, not conversion: visitors came in at 13–65% of target on most brands while conversion ran at 130–234% of target. On one electronics brand's Shopee store, November 11 brought 1.6 times a normal day's visitors and an 18.3% conversion rate, against about 7% normally — more than half the lift came from people who were already there buying faster. The sale also stretched instead of spiking: on the two largest accounts, daily sales across the 11.11 window came in at +4% and −11% versus 10.10, against plans of +63% and +18%, the same daily rate spread over 13 days instead of four, with Shopee Singapore running October 30 to November 17. And the week after, the usual levers failed: lowering the ROAS target from 5.8 to 5.5 on a fashion brand's TikTok account cut daily spend from $306–587 to $185–223, because the algorithm could not find enough people worth bidding on, and delivered ROI had already slipped to about 4.5, the brand's break-even. The public data leans the same way: Criteo found conversion up 20% year on year in Southeast Asia while retail site traffic stayed broadly flat. The pricing lesson is that a festival discount converts a crowd; it does not create one, and the crowd has to be built before the night, not bought on it.

## 3. [Lazada's 11.11 scoreboard: brand malls took nearly 80% of GMV, and affiliates drove the rest](https://english.ebrun.com/20261120/721717.shtml)

**Source:** Ebrun, Nov 20, 2026, on Lazada's 11.11 release; Shopee Indonesia figures via SocioToday: [english.ebrun.com](https://english.ebrun.com/20261120/721717.shtml) and [sociotoday.com](https://sociotoday.com/realme-secures-top-spot-in-volume-while-xiaomi-dominates-gmv-during-shopee-indonesia-11-11-big-sale-2026/)

Lazada released its 11.11 numbers for Southeast Asia on November 20, and the concentration is the story. LazMall, the brand mall, contributed nearly 80% of the region's total GMV, running at seven times its daily level, while the "One-Click Easy Cross-Border" business grew GMV over 200% year on year with order volume at nearly four times the daily average. Average order value rose year on year in all six major Southeast Asian markets, up more than 30% for the region. On November 11, affiliate marketing drove over 40% of GMV for Lazada's Chinese merchant business, and over half in Vietnam. Shopee Indonesia's own 11.11 release shows the same split between volume and value: realme was the best-selling smartphone brand by units, while Xiaomi took the top spot by GMV, with prices from Rp2,899,000 for an entry Redmi to Rp17,999,000 for the Xiaomi 17 Ultra. The pricing point is where festival value lands: the discount is the draw, but the money concentrates in brand malls and affiliate-driven stores, and the platform's own scoreboard measures that concentration, not the depth of any single discount. One caveat: the Ebrun piece is an AI-translated write-up of Lazada's release, so treat the figures as the company's own claims, not an audit.

## 4. [Shopee now takes 15 cents of every GMV dollar — up from 13 a year ago](https://businessbearings.com/articles/sea-s-revenue-jumps-43-4-to-8-6-billion-in-q3-as-shopee-sets-records)

**Source:** Sea Limited Q3 2026 results, Nov 10, 2026, via Business Bearings; take rate from UOB Kay Hian's note on the results: [businessbearings.com](https://businessbearings.com/articles/sea-s-revenue-jumps-43-4-to-8-6-billion-in-q3-as-shopee-sets-records) and [uobkh.com.sg](https://uobkh.com.sg/strapi-uploads/sd261111_26779b3634.pdf)

Shopee's third quarter shows the platform monetising each transaction more aggressively than a year ago. GMV reached $39.7 billion, up 23.3% year on year, but Shopee's revenue grew 41.3% to $6.1 billion — the gap is the take rate, which UOB Kay Hian puts at 15%, up from 13% in the third quarter of 2025. ShopeeVIP membership passed 21 million and now contributes nearly a quarter of GMV. The take rate is the broker's calculation from Sea's figures, not a number Sea states, but the direction is in Sea's own release: revenue growing nearly twice as fast as the value of goods sold. For a seller, that is the other side of the festival story in items 2 and 3: the platform's cut rises even as discounts deepen, so a seller's 11.11 "growth" can be real in GMV and thinner in margin at the same time. Sea's group revenue rose 43.4% to $8.6 billion and net income 34% to $502.5 million, with all three of its businesses profitable.

## 5. [Japan's rice fell 36% — and 1,477 other food items rose in the same month](https://thebostonreport.com/2026/11/01/japan-price-hikes-november-2026-food-power-bills/)

**Source:** The Boston Report, Nov 1, 2026, on Teikoku Databank's survey of 195 major food makers (Oct 30) and Nikkei/Fuji News Network reporting; rice prices from the agriculture ministry's weekly supermarket survey: [thebostonreport.com](https://thebostonreport.com/2026/11/01/japan-price-hikes-november-2026-food-power-bills/) and [maff.go.jp](https://www.maff.go.jp/j/nousan/kokumotu/ksppos.pdf)

Japan's food prices tell two stories at once. The staple that defined the last two years has collapsed: a 5 kg bag of rice that cost ¥4,416 in the New Year week now costs ¥2,747, down 36.4% year on year in the week of November 2–8, according to the agriculture ministry's survey of about 1,000 supermarkets. But almost everything else went up in November: manufacturers repriced 1,477 food and drink items, by an average of 17%, led by condiments (644 items, mostly sauces and mayonnaise) and drinks (417 items). The 2026 total has now passed 20,000 repriced items for a second straight year, and the reasons companies give have shifted — raw materials were cited for 91.7% of increases, the lowest share since Teikoku Databank started tabulating in 2023, while labour costs were cited for 51.5%, a record. Energy rose with it: seven of the ten major utilities set record household rates for November use, with TEPCO's standard bill at ¥9,738 and Tokyo Gas at ¥6,718. The government response is a subsidy, not a price control: Prime Minister Takaichi announced winter electricity and gas subsidies for December to February worth about ¥7,000 per standard household, while the gasoline subsidy tapers, with the target pump price rising about ¥5 a litre a month from December. The pricing point is the split: the most visible price fell by a third and food inflation stayed high anyway, because the fall was one item and the rises were 1,477 of them.

## 6. [Brazil's consumer watchdog gave Amazon 72 hours to prove its Black Friday is safe](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/change/22672-national-consumer-secretariat-investigation-into-amazon-over-consumer-protection-measures-for-black-friday-2026)

**Source:** Digital Policy Alert, on Brazil's National Consumer Secretariat (Senacon), Nov 16, 2026: [digitalpolicyalert.org](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/change/22672-national-consumer-secretariat-investigation-into-amazon-over-consumer-protection-measures-for-black-friday-2026)

On November 16, Brazil's National Consumer Secretariat, Senacon, notified Amazon to provide information within 72 hours on the measures it has adopted to protect consumers during Black Friday 2026. The investigation is still under deliberation, and it is the federal enforcement step behind the warnings state Procon agencies have been issuing all month about artificial price inflation and fake promotions. It lands on ground Meta covered on November 17: Shoprime's price-history index found only 30.3% of advertised Black Friday offers were real discounts, and 20.8% cost more than the product's usual price. Brazilian shoppers are saying the same thing in their own words: posts on Threads from November 22–24 note prices looking higher than they were 30 days ago and ask whether the "discount" is real. The pricing point is the shift from measurement to enforcement — the index showed the discounts were mostly not discounts, and now the regulator is asking the platform to show its working before the sale, not after it.

## 7. [From December 1, Substack handles your sales tax — and the real price of "free" is 13.6% plus 30 cents](https://substack.com/pricing)

**Source:** Substack pricing page, checked November 2026: [substack.com](https://substack.com/pricing)

From December 1, 2026, Substack will calculate, collect and remit sales tax and VAT on paid subscriptions for its writers, at no extra cost — no registration with tax authorities, nothing to turn on, though some countries are excluded. It is a small change with a clear pricing logic: Substack absorbs a compliance cost that, on rival platforms, is one more reason to pay for a plan. The page's own comparison table makes the contrast explicit: beehiiv's Lite plan starts at $49 a month billed yearly and Ghost(Pro)'s Publisher plan at $29, both rising with list size, while Substack charges nothing to publish at any list size and takes 10% of paid revenue. But the "free" platform is not free at the point of payment: Stripe takes 2.9% plus 30 cents per payment and 0.7% for recurring billing on top of Substack's 10%, so the total deduction is 13.6% plus 30 cents — and the fixed 30 cents punishes small prices hardest. At $5 a month a writer keeps $4.02, or 80.4%; at $8 a month, $6.61, or 82.6%; annual billing keeps more. The pricing point is the shape of the fee: a percentage that scales with success and a fixed charge that punishes the cheapest subscriptions, which is exactly where new creators price.

## 8. [One creator's Black Friday plan: no sale, no countdown, same prices — and last year it worked](https://nerminyusufi.com/checkout-without-a-clock-holding-prices-black-friday-week/)

**Source:** Nermin Yusufi, Nov 19, 2026: [nerminyusufi.com](https://nerminyusufi.com/checkout-without-a-clock-holding-prices-black-friday-week/)

A one-person creative brand selling digital guides, a small zine, a template pack and consulting calls has announced she will hold every price exactly where it is from Thanksgiving morning through Cyber Monday night — no banners, no "last chance" stories, no fake clock, just a note saying prices are the same as yesterday. She tried the same thing last year and kept the receipts. The $9 zine PDF sold 14 copies against about six in a normal week; the $24 field guide sold two; the template pack sold zero; she booked two consulting calls at her regular rate. Traffic rose about 30% on a normal week, mostly from people searching for Black Friday creative tools and landing on a page with no sale. Her read is that low-friction items moved because they were easy yeses, not because they were cheap, and that without a discount people had to actually want the thing. "Urgency creates transactions. Clarity creates relationships." It is one shop and one week, not a study — but it is the only priced experiment in this week's Black Friday coverage that reports what happened when the discount was zero, and it pairs with the surveys: MoneySuperMarket found 35% of UK small businesses sitting out Black Friday this year, up from 22% last year, and Xero found 31% of New Zealand small firms doing the same.

## 9. [A paid app is a five-dollar decision — and on Google Play the chart sometimes has only four apps in it](https://dev.to/pb_z_a8cbb692add4fb2da733/the-top-paid-chart-is-a-five-dollar-shelf-and-on-google-play-it-is-often-barely-a-chart-p92)

**Source:** dev.to, Nov 24, 2026, snapshot of Nov 23 Top Paid charts in ten markets: [dev.to](https://dev.to/pb_z_a8cbb692add4fb2da733/the-top-paid-chart-is-a-five-dollar-shelf-and-on-google-play-it-is-often-barely-a-chart-p92)

Someone pulled the Top Paid chart of every storefront daily for a few months, and the November 23 snapshot says paid apps are priced in a narrow band almost everywhere. On the App Store the median Top Paid app costs $4.99 in the US, £4.99 in the UK, €4.99 in France, ¥1,440 in Japan, ₹199 in India, R$11.90 in Brazil and ₩4,150 in South Korea — in nine of ten markets the median lands between roughly $5 and $8 in local terms, and above about $25 you are an outlier on the shelf whatever you believe your product is worth. The surprise is on Google Play: the chart is not always 30 apps. Japan returns six entries, Australia four, Canada five, France six, Germany 18 — the storefront's paid chart really has that few rows on that day. Play's paid chart is dense only in the US and India. The pricing point is that "Top 5 Paid" in a market where the chart has five apps tells you almost nothing about demand, and that the two stores are not comparable shelves: a paid price that looks normal on one can be invisible on the other. Caveats, stated by the author: one day, list prices only, no currency conversion, and the post is AI-assisted with its data and code published for reproduction.

## Pick of the day

Item 2, the 11.11 post-mortem. Everyone reports festival GMV; almost nobody reports what the festival cost to buy. Ten brands' own trackers show the discount did its job — conversion more than doubled — and still missed target, because the people were not there to convert. Then the post-sale test proved it: when the team lowered the ROAS target, the algorithm spent less, not more. A sale that cannot create its crowd is a tax on the crowd you already have.

## 1. [Gemini 4 Neon launched at $3/$15 — 25% below what Carbon will cost from December 1](https://www.datastudios.org/post/google-gemini-4-neon-launch-api-pricing-3-input-15-output)

**Source:** DataStudios, Nov 18, 2026, on Google DeepMind's launch post; prices verified against Google's own Gemini API pricing page, read Nov 21: [datastudios.org](https://www.datastudios.org/post/google-gemini-4-neon-launch-api-pricing-3-input-15-output)

Google released Gemini 4 Neon on November 18, priced at $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens, with cached input at $0.15. The pricing page now also lists Batch and Flex at $1.50/$7.50 and Priority at $5.40/$27.00; there is no free tier. The price matters because of the calendar: Carbon and Argon cost $2/$10 only until November 30, then double to $4/$20 on December 1, so from that date Neon is 25% cheaper than both flagships on input and output. Hydrogen, at $1.50/$7.50 with a free tier, stays the cheapest paid option. This briefing covered Argon's doubling on Nov 16 and Carbon's on Nov 18; Neon is the new fact, and it changes the read on the doubling — Google is not just raising its flagship price, it is slotting a mid-priced model underneath it, so a team that built on Carbon's launch price has a cheaper downgrade path that did not exist a week ago. These are published list prices; what any buyer actually pays under a commitment is not disclosed.

## 2. [Singles Day's fastest-growing product was AI tokens, not phones](https://www.emarketer.com/content/price-wars-cautious-subdued-singles-day-2026)

**Source:** eMarketer, Nov 2026, on Syntun data and company reports: [emarketer.com](https://www.emarketer.com/content/price-wars-cautious-subdued-singles-day-2026)

This briefing covered the Singles Day total on Nov 17 (RMB 1,776.4 billion, up 4.8% against 14.2% in 2025); what is new is what sold. Sales of AI tokens on Tmall jumped over 700% between October 15 and November 5 compared with the previous four weeks, and Tmall launched an AI Token Center selling consumer token packages from Alibaba Cloud, MiniMax, Moonshot AI and Z.AI. JD.com said searches for "token" rose more than 13-fold and sales of large-language-model token packages rose more than tenfold; it sold more than 50 million AI-related products, and the value of AI PCs, workstations, cameras, glasses and recording devices more than doubled. Top livestreamer Austin Li sold 100,000 monthly memberships of Alibaba's QwenWork office AI service within half an hour. The pricing point is the product itself: AI tokens are now sold to consumers the way phone credit used to be, in small prepaid packages through a livestream, which makes the token — a metered input — a retail good with a shelf price, a discount and a scarcity deadline. Alibaba and JD.com still do not publish their own headline totals, so Syntun's estimate remains the only overall figure.

## 3. [Singapore and Malaysia broke Grab's cross-border taxi monopoly — but the new apps have no prices yet](https://www.misslobang.com/article/cross-border-taxi-quota-500-cdg-zig-kumpool-nov-2026)

**Source:** MissLobang, Singapore, Nov 6–8, 2026, on the two transport ministries' joint statement: [misslobang.com](https://www.misslobang.com/article/cross-border-taxi-quota-500-cdg-zig-kumpool-nov-2026)

From November 16, Singapore and Malaysia each license 500 cross-border taxis, up from 300, and two more ride-hail operators — Singapore's CDG Zig and Malaysia's Kumpool — join Grab as licensed platforms under the Cross-Border Taxi Scheme. Street-hail fares stay fixed: S$80 for a standard 4-seater from Ban San Street Terminal to Larkin Sentral or within 35 km (S$120 for a 6-seater, S$180 premium), and RM240, RM360 and RM540 in the other direction, with surcharges beyond 35 km. The interesting part is what is missing: neither CDG Zig nor Kumpool had published an app fare when the scheme started, so the competition exists on paper before it exists in prices, and Grab says its app fares are fixed upfront with reference to the street-hail fares. Private-hire cars still cannot carry paying passengers across the border. A quota rise plus a second and third booking platform should mean shorter waits and something to compare against Grab's upfront price — but until the new apps publish fares, there is no way to know whether they compete on price or just on availability.

Samsung's own Black Friday offers run until November 30. The pricing point is the asymmetry: Flipkart's week-long sale sets the reference price first, and Amazon, arriving later, will have to beat a price its rival has already published rather than set one.

## 5. [Japan's rice price fell by a third in a year — while everything else kept rising](https://map-news.com/japan-rice-prices/en/)

**Source:** Map News, updated Nov 21, 2026, on Japan's Ministry of Agriculture weekly supermarket data, published Nov 20: [map-news.com](https://map-news.com/japan-rice-prices/en/)

A 5 kg bag of rice cost ¥2,773 on average in Japanese supermarkets in the week of November 9–15, up ¥26 on the week but down ¥1,487, or 34.9%, on a year earlier, according to the farm ministry's weekly POS data from about 1,000 stores. Brand rice averaged ¥2,824 and blended or store-brand rice ¥2,541. The fall is the unwind of a spike: the same bag cost about ¥2,000 in early 2024, peaked at ¥4,416 in the New Year week of 2026, and has fallen by more than a third since. October's prefectural survey tells the same story at the other end of the shelf — Koshihikari cost most in Sapporo at ¥4,150 for 5 kg, and in all 47 prefectural capitals it was cheaper than a year earlier. The contrast is the point: this briefing covered Teikoku Databank's November wave of 1,477 repriced food and drink items on Nov 15, and rice is the staple that broke the pattern — a supply-driven price fall inside a year when food makers announced more than 20,000 increases.

## 6. [South Africa's petrol is set to fall R2.65 a litre in December — but diesel will rise, because a tax holiday is ending](https://www.sowetan.co.za/motoring/2026-11-17-heres-what-you-could-pay-for-petrol-and-diesel-in-december/)

**Source:** Sowetan, South Africa, Nov 17, 2026, on Central Energy Fund mid-month data (Oct 30–Nov 16): [sowetan.co.za](https://www.sowetan.co.za/motoring/2026-11-17-heres-what-you-could-pay-for-petrol-and-diesel-in-december/)

South African petrol looks set for a large December cut, while diesel is set to rise slightly — and the difference is tax, not oil. The Central Energy Fund's mid-month data points to 93-octane petrol falling R2.65 a litre, from R29.87 to about R27.22 inland, and 95-octane falling R2.90, from R30.24 to R27.34, when prices adjust on December 2. Diesel, on the same data, rises: 500ppm by R0.28 a litre and 50ppm falls only R0.12. The reason is the National Treasury's November fuel-levy relief — R1.50 a litre off petrol and R3.00 off diesel — which is partially reversed in December, adding R0.50 back to petrol and R1.50 back to diesel. Diesel's underlying over-recovery of about R1.34 a litre is more than wiped out by the returning tax; petrol's larger over-recovery survives it. These are projections, not final prices, and they move daily with oil and the rand — but the mechanism is already fixed: a temporary tax cut ending is a price rise, even in a month when the fuel itself gets cheaper.

## 7. [A third of Britain's small firms are skipping Black Friday — and in New Zealand, half of those who join borrow to do it](https://www.business-money.com/announcements/black-friday-boycott-grows-as-nearly-2-million-small-businesses-refuse-to-take-part/)

**Source:** Business Money, Nov 16, 2026, on MoneySuperMarket research; Xero New Zealand media release, Nov 19, 2026: [business-money.com](https://www.business-money.com/announcements/black-friday-boycott-grows-as-nearly-2-million-small-businesses-refuse-to-take-part/)

Two new surveys sharpen the opt-out story this briefing first reported on Nov 19. MoneySuperMarket now says 35% of UK small businesses will sit out Black Friday this year, up from 22% in 2025 — roughly 1.97 million firms — and the figures are now reported by two outlets, where a week ago they could not be confirmed. Among firms that do take part, 53% say it helps attract new customers, while 14% say the discounts devalue their products or brand. Xero's New Zealand survey of 517 small business owners, released November 19, finds 31% do not plan to take part, rising to 44% of sole traders, and that around half of previous participants used credit cards, supplier terms, loans or delayed bill payments to fund extra stock and staff. Two caveats: the UK poll covered 300 owners, and neither survey measures margins, so this is directionally on point for the low-margin opt-out hypothesis, not a direct test. The split inside the numbers is the story: the firms that join mostly say it works, the firms that sit out mostly say it would cost them — and the ones in the middle are borrowing to afford the discount.

## 8. [Amazon told sellers its 2027 FBA fees rise $0.19 a unit — and the small items lose their discount rate](https://ppc.land/amazon-sellers-face-average-0-19-fba-fee-rise-from-january-15-2027/)

**Source:** Amazon Seller Central announcement, Nov 5, 2026, via PPC Land, Nov 6: [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/amazon-sellers-face-average-0-19-fba-fee-rise-from-january-15-2027/)

Amazon announced on November 5 that US Fulfillment by Amazon fees will rise by an average of $0.19 per unit from January 15, 2027, about 0.8% of the average item's selling price. The average hides the spread: small standard-size items priced $10–$50 rise $0.40 on average, and those over $50 rise $0.70, while items under $10 rise $0.20 — and five fee categories lose their discounted low-price FBA rates entirely, paying the $10–$50 rates instead. From February 1, a single age-based storage fee replaces the storage utilisation and aged-inventory surcharges, and the low-inventory-level fee is recalculated on 28 days of supply. The 3.5% fuel and logistics surcharge introduced in April 2026 stays, and the published rate cards exclude it. One rate did fall: 3D printers get a lower referral fee, 12% on the first $200 and 5% above, effective November 5. Amazon publishes these fee changes once a year, normally effective mid-January; the 2027 schedule is a bigger structural change than 2026's, and it rewards fast-moving, well-measured inventory while charging more for small items above $10, aged stock and awkward shapes.

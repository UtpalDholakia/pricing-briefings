## 1. [HubSpot won't charge for Claude or ChatGPT inside its CRM — but agents working at scale will pay in credits](https://ppc.land/hubspot-wont-charge-extra-when-claude-or-chatgpt-works-its-crm/)

**Source:** PPC Land, Nov 2026, on HubSpot's newsroom post of Nov 17, 2026, "HubSpot is headless. Here's how we'll price it": [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/hubspot-wont-charge-extra-when-claude-or-chatgpt-works-its-crm/)

HubSpot said on November 17 that everyday work done from Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini or Microsoft Copilot through its connectors is included in the seat a customer already pays for, and that third-party agents using its existing APIs count against the plan's API allowance rather than its credits. The chargeable route is a third one: a governed environment for agents that HubSpot is still building, where work done at scale — enriching records, personalising outreach — will be paid for with HubSpot Credits. The post gives no rates, limits or start date for that environment, and no ceiling for what counts as "everyday" connector use. The context is a pricing model already moving: on the November 4 earnings call HubSpot said its hybrid seats-and-credits pilot now covers all new customers in Europe, the Middle East and Africa, and that customers created more than 22 million CRM objects through connectors in the quarter, up 157% on the second quarter. Credits already have published prices — $0.01 each pay-as-you-go, $10 per 1,000 in a capacity pack — and HubSpot's own agents are priced in them, a resolved Customer Agent conversation at 50 credits ($0.50) and a Prospecting Agent recommendation at 100 ($1.00). These are published list prices; net price is not disclosed. The open question is the contradiction the post leaves standing: under the pilot, API usage sits inside credits, while the November 17 post says API calls from third-party agents will not consume credits — HubSpot reports what it learned in EMEA in February 2027.

## 2. [Slack caps its AI agent at 15 messages a week on Business+ — then sells the overage in Salesforce credits](https://slack.com/help/articles/53579676130195-Slackbot-limits-and-credit-usage)

**Source:** Slack Help Center, "Slackbot limits and credit usage", read Nov 22, 2026; pricing-page trackers recorded the limits row appearing on Slack's pricing page in mid-November: [slack.com](https://slack.com/help/articles/53579676130195-Slackbot-limits-and-credit-usage)

Slack's own help centre states the meter inside its seat plans: each member of a Business+ workspace can send 15 messages to Slackbot per week, resetting at midnight on Monday, while Free and Pro customers get Slackbot only as a trial or preview that does not reset. Only the top Enterprise+ tier removes the cap entirely. Once a Business+ member hits 15, the choices are to wait for Monday or to pay in Salesforce Flex Credits — if a Salesforce organisation is connected and both admins switch it on. Salesforce's rate card prices a Slackbot standard action at 40 Flex Credits and an advanced action at 200, which at Salesforce's list price of $500 per 100,000 credits is $0.20 and $1.00 per action. The pattern is the same one HubSpot is building toward in item 1: the seat buys a small allowance of agent work, and the meter starts where the allowance ends, with the expensive actions — deep research, creating slides and documents — priced five times the standard ones. These are published list prices; what any buyer actually pays is not disclosed.

## 3. [Britain's energy bills are forecast to rise 26% in January — to their highest since 2023](https://www.cornwall-insight.com/press-releases/energy-bills-set-for-steep-rise-in-january/)

**Source:** Cornwall Insight press release, Nov 18, 2026: [cornwall-insight.com](https://www.cornwall-insight.com/press-releases/energy-bills-set-for-steep-rise-in-january/)

Cornwall Insight's final forecast for the January to March 2027 price cap puts a typical dual-fuel household paying by direct debit at £2,166 a year, up £443 — about 26% — on the current £1,723 cap, and the highest level since 2023. The unit rates behind it are electricity at 32.54p per kWh, up from 26.32p, and gas at 10.90p, up from 7.97p. The cause named is wholesale gas: disruption to exports and record-low European storage heading into winter, with the conflict in the Middle East pushing prices up. The forecast already includes the zero rate of VAT on household electricity, extended to July 2027, but not the one-off £100 Household Energy Payment announced in the Budget, which sits outside the cap. This is a forecast, not the confirmed cap — Ofgem announces the official figure on November 25 and it takes effect January 1 — and Cornwall Insight expects a small fall in April. The timing is the point: the rise lands in the coldest quarter, on households already dealing with the cost-of-living crisis, and the cap limits unit rates rather than total bills, so a cold January costs more than the headline suggests.

## 4. [US rent growth turned positive for the first time in over three years — by one dollar](https://www.apartmentlist.com/research/national-rent-data)

**Source:** Apartment List National Rent Report, November 2026 (October data): [apartmentlist.com](https://www.apartmentlist.com/research/national-rent-data)

Year-over-year rent growth in the US flipped positive in October for the first time since mid-2023 — by 0.1%, with the national median rent at $1,384, exactly $1 more than a year earlier. The milestone is real but thin: rents are still falling in inflation-adjusted terms, month-over-month rents fell 0.3% in October, and the market is only just absorbing the construction boom that peaked in 2024 with more than 600,000 new multifamily units. The softness is still visible underneath the headline: the vacancy rate is 7%, down from a 7.3% peak in February but above its long-run average, 32% of properties are offering concessions worth at least a month of free rent (up from 28% a year ago), and units take 36 days to lease, the longest October since tracking began in 2019. The geography tells the story: San Francisco city rents are up 24% year over year and San Jose 9%, as the AI boom creates high-paying tech jobs, while San Antonio is down 3.8% and Austin — after the fastest apartment-building spree of any major market — has moderated from a 6.2% decline in 2025 to 1.2%. Apartment List expects 2026 to be the first year since 2022 with positive full-year rent growth.

## 5. [Thanksgiving dinner costs 11% more this year — but the Farm Bureau says 6%](https://groundworkcollaborative.org/work/thanksgiving-staples-up-11-as-soaring-prices-gobble-up-paychecks/)

**Source:** Groundwork Collaborative and The Century Foundation, Nov 17, 2026, on NielsenIQ data; American Farm Bureau Federation survey via Now Georgia: [groundworkcollaborative.org](https://groundworkcollaborative.org/work/thanksgiving-staples-up-11-as-soaring-prices-gobble-up-paychecks/)

A new analysis from Groundwork Collaborative and The Century Foundation, using NielsenIQ store prices for the weeks ending October 31, 2026 and October 30, 2025, puts a 26-item Thanksgiving grocery list at $137.12, up 11% from $123.52 — roughly three times overall inflation. The turkey is the centrepiece of the rise: a 15-pound frozen bird is up 26% to $19.65, with wholesale prices around $1.80 a pound, up 30% on last year and nearly double 2024, while green beans are up 36.7%, elbow macaroni 40.2%, cream of mushroom soup 22.5% and aluminium foil 22.3%. The counterpoint matters: the American Farm Bureau Federation's survey puts a classic dinner for 10 at $58.63, up about 6% from 2025 — ending a three-year streak of declines — with a 16-pound turkey at $23.68, up 10%. The two measures count different baskets by different methods, so the gap is a warning about the number as much as the price. Either way, the direction is the same, and the coping is visible in the same reports: a LendingTree survey finds 53% of people say inflation is affecting their Thanksgiving plans, and Groundwork cites one in five Americans covering holiday costs with buy-now-pay-later loans.

## 6. [The closing-down sale on your Facebook feed may be from a shop that never existed](https://www.thinkmoney.co.uk/blog/black-friday-scams-2026-how-to-spot-ai-fake-shops-and-closing-down-sales/)

**Source:** thinkmoney, Nov 2026, on Which? investigations, Barclays research and BBC Wales reporting: [thinkmoney.co.uk](https://www.thinkmoney.co.uk/blog/black-friday-scams-2026-how-to-spot-ai-fake-shops-and-closing-down-sales/)

The Black Friday discount doing the most damage this year may not be a real discount at all: adverts on Facebook and Instagram for independent shops holding heartbreaking closing-down sales, everything up to 80% off, from businesses that never existed. In May 2026 consumer group Which? investigated 20 suspicious online shops presenting themselves as long-established independent businesses, some selling cheap imported clothing as higher-quality goods, and seven suspicious social media accounts featuring what appeared to be AI-generated influencers promoting supposedly handmade products, some videos drawing hundreds of thousands of views. Barclays found 76% of UK adults believe AI has made shopping scams more convincing, yet only 36% check whether a website is genuine, and NordVPN researchers said on November 4 they had found more than 10,000 fake stores impersonating 166 fashion brands. One case shows how the pricing works as bait: BBC Wales reported in July on Seren Cardiff Jewellery, a supposed Cardiff business closing down with discounts of up to 80%, whose customers paid for jewellery that never arrived before the site was taken down. The pricing point is that the discount is not the price of a product but the price of trust — an 80% markdown plus a sad story is what makes a shopper skip the check they would otherwise make. The FTC's April 2026 figures put losses from scams starting on social media at $2.1 billion in 2025.

## 8. [A discount that is hard to claim can cut sales of the product it promotes](https://marketbusinessnews.com/when-a-discount-is-hard-to-claim-it-can-reduce-sales-of-the-product/457159/)

**Source:** Market Business News, Nov 21, 2026, on Cheng and Blank, Journal of Marketing Research, 2025, and an American Marketing Association discussion, Aug 2026: [marketbusinessnews.com](https://marketbusinessnews.com/when-a-discount-is-hard-to-claim-it-can-reduce-sales-of-the-product/457159/)

A large discount with a demanding condition — a minimum purchase, a membership, a long contract — can reduce total sales of the promoted product compared with offering no promotion at all, according to five studies by Andong Cheng and Ashley Stadler Blank using secondary data, field data and laboratory experiments. The mechanism is the reference price working backwards: shoppers who cannot meet the condition see the discounted price, judge the regular price a worse deal by comparison, and become less willing to buy at full price. The lesson for anyone running a conditional offer is to measure total sales of the promoted product, not redemptions — counting only the people who claimed the deal misses the people who saw it, decided they could not qualify, and bought something else or left. The result is not a verdict against conditional offers: easier qualification produced different outcomes, and the authors suggest testing a simpler rule against the existing offer and a no-promotion group, or showing the offer only to shoppers likely to qualify. The limits are stated plainly: product value, the burden of the condition and the audience all matter, and a subscription that appeals to someone already intending to sign up for a year can put off someone who wants flexibility.

## 9. [Don't wait for Black Friday to buy RAM or a GPU — the chips cost more than they did a year ago](https://www.nexus-compute.com/blog/black-friday-2026-ram-ssd-gpu-prices)

**Source:** Nexus Compute, Nov 6, 2026, on The Elec/DRAMeXchange, TrendForce and Tom's Hardware price tracking: [nexus-compute.com](https://www.nexus-compute.com/blog/black-friday-2026-ram-ssd-gpu-prices)

Black Friday is five days away and the year's most-watched tech categories are moving the wrong way for buyers. The average contract price of 8Gb DDR4 memory rose 5.77% in October to $27.50, a record and its 19th straight monthly gain, and TrendForce forecasts PC DRAM contract prices up 13% to 18% in the fourth quarter. At retail, a 32GB DDR5-6000 kit's best US price was $459 on November 3 against a lowest-ever $72, and the cheapest in-stock RTX 5080 was $2,199.99 on October 30 against a $999 list price, with Tom's Hardware saying the card is unlikely to be available by Black Friday at all. SSDs are the one category with genuine downward pressure: a benchmark NAND contract price was flat in October after a rally since January 2025, and spot wafer prices fell — though a Samsung 990 EVO Plus 2TB still cost $365.99 on Amazon on October 27, or $183 per terabyte, against $129 a year earlier. The guide's warning applies beyond components: Which? studied 227 products from the 2023 sale and found 92% of the deals were the same price or cheaper at other times of the year. A discount measured against an inflated was-price is not a discount, and this year the was-price is doing most of the work.

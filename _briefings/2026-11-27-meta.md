## 1. [Only one in five Black Friday deals was the year's cheapest — and barely half of shoppers still believe the discounts](https://www.retailgazette.co.uk/blog/2026/11/four-in-five-black-friday-deals-arent-at-their-cheapest-which-finds/)

**Source:** Which? investigation, published Nov 24, 2026, via Retail Gazette, Nov 25; BCG sixth annual Black Friday study, Nov 4, 2026, and European Commission sweep, March 2026, via Solid Labs, Nov 24: [retailgazette.co.uk](https://www.retailgazette.co.uk/blog/2026/11/four-in-five-black-friday-deals-arent-at-their-cheapest-which-finds/) and [solidlabs.com](https://www.solidlabs.com/blog/are-black-friday-deals-real)

Which? tracked more than 250,000 daily prices on 756 home, tech and health products at eight UK retailers over twelve months, and found only 22% reached their lowest price of the year during the four-week Black Friday period; another 44% only matched a price available elsewhere in the year, and 62% were cheaper or the same price in the six months after the sale. The spread between retailers is wide: Very had 33% of its deals at their lowest price, Amazon 27%, and Richer Sounds 6%. The examples show the reference price doing the work: an LG 75-inch TV at AO was advertised at £1,800 with a claimed £899 saving against a £2,699 recommended price Which? could not find evidence it ever sold at — it fell to £1,499 in December and £1,208 in May — and Bose QuietComfort Ultra headphones advertised at £399 fell to £299.95 in March. Shoppers have noticed: BCG's survey of 10,220 consumers in ten countries finds only 54% trust that Black Friday prices are genuinely lower than usual, one in five actively distrust them, and 22% plan to spend less or skip the sales because discounts feel available all the time; Ireland's consumer commission puts trust at 23%, its lowest since 2022. The pricing point is that the discount is no longer believed by default — BCG's trust scores put retailer reputation and money-back guarantees at a net +65%, verified reviews at +59% and a clear price comparison at +58%, while real-time scarcity messages score +8% — so proof now sells and pressure does not.

## 2. [Mexico's Buen Fin closed its best edition in 15 years — on card payments and in-store shopping](https://antropico.mx/la-semana-en-marketing-16-23-noviembre-2026/?lang=es)

**Source:** CANACO CDMX / CEESP estimates via La Jornada Estado de México; Concanaco Servytur survey via Publimetro and El País, Nov 18, 2026; Profeco final tally via Vanguardia, Nov 18: [antropico.mx](https://antropico.mx/la-semana-en-marketing-16-23-noviembre-2026/?lang=es)

Mexico's Buen Fin ran November 13–17, and the Mexico City chamber of commerce, using estimates from the private-sector economics centre CEESP, puts national sales at about MXN 260,000 million, above Concanaco's official target of MXN 235,000 million and the best result in the festival's 15 years — a sector estimate, not the official final balance, and Concanaco's survey measures business owners' perceptions, not a census of sales. What is measured shop by shop: 82% of businesses sold more than on a normal weekend, 64% rated their results good or excellent, and 40% of consumers spent more than MXN 5,000; electronics took 31% of main purchases and white goods 22%, 85% of shoppers paid by card against 12% in cash, and 63% bought in store only while just 18% bought online only. Profeco's final complaint tally — already filed in the trackers on Nov 24 — was 243 complaints, 98.8% settled by conciliation, with MXN 1,571,779 recovered, mostly for promotions not honoured and advertised prices not respected. The pricing point is that the festival is won before the weekend: shoppers arrived with prices compared and cards ready, and a promotion that is not honoured costs the seller more than no promotion at all.

## 3. [In Brazil, the same Black Friday product cost 230% more from one store to the next](https://seg.es.gov.br/black-friday-pesquisa-do-procon-es-identifica-variacao-de-ate-230-nos-precos-de-produtos)

**Source:** Procon-ES price survey, Nov 5–10, 2026; Procon-SP monitoring, via Jornal de Indaiatuba, Nov 26, 2026: [seg.es.gov.br](https://seg.es.gov.br/black-friday-pesquisa-do-procon-es-identifica-variacao-de-ate-230-nos-precos-de-produtos) and [jornaldeindaiatuba.com.br](https://www.jornaldeindaiatuba.com.br/procon-sp-alerta-consumidores-sobre-ofertas-enganosas-e-golpes-na-black-friday)

The consumer protection agency of Espírito Santo checked more than 40 products online and in stores in the run-up to Black Friday and found the same item varying by up to 230% between sellers: a Mondial CH 05 electric grill cost R$140.76 in one store and R$464.61 in another for cash payment, while in physical stores a Três Corações Passione espresso machine ranged from R$179.90 to R$429.00, a 138% gap. The payment method is a second price: a Redmi Watch 5 Active varied 167% for cash and 206% on instalments, and a ten-piece Eirilar pan set varied 145% cash and 178% on instalments. Meanwhile Procon-SP, the São Paulo agency, has monitored a sample of products on the main sales sites since September to catch "maquiagem de preços" — raising the price before the sale so the discount looks bigger — and surveyed 585 consumers between October 8 and November 10. This is the state-level measurement behind the federal step covered on Nov 25, when Senacon gave Amazon 72 hours to prove its Black Friday consumer protections. The pricing point is that with the same product varying threefold, the discount percentage is meaningless without a price history — and the instalment price can be a different product from the cash price.

## 4. [Japan's inflation hit 2.3% in October — lettuce nearly doubled while rice fell by a record 30%](https://fxrecap.com/forex-news/top-stories/japan-cpi-october-2026-2-3-percent)

**Source:** Statistics Bureau of Japan, Consumer Price Index for October 2026, released Nov 20, 2026, via FX Recap: [fxrecap.com](https://fxrecap.com/forex-news/top-stories/japan-cpi-october-2026-2-3-percent)

Japan's headline consumer prices rose 2.3% year on year in October, up from 2.1% in September; core inflation excluding fresh food edged up to 2.2%, while the core-core measure that also strips out energy slowed to 2.2% from 2.4%. The rise came from the volatile parts: fresh food rose 6.3%, with lettuce prices up 95.6%, and energy turned positive at 2.2% after falling 0.6% in September — electricity up 1.6%, city gas up 5.3%, petrol still 2.3% cheaper — while the bureau estimates policy measures, including abolishing the provisional petrol tax rate, are holding the headline down by about 0.97 percentage points. The offset came from processed food, which slowed to 2.2%, and from ordinary rice falling 29.8%, the largest drop since records began in 1971, as well as private high school tuition falling 73.2% as free-tuition policy widened. What is new against the Nov 25 item, which covered the Teikoku Databank wave of 1,477 November price rises and the rice fall: this is the official October release, and it shows the split — fresh food and energy pushing the headline up while underlying domestic pressure eases — ahead of the Bank of Japan's December 18 decision, with its policy rate at about 1.25%.

## 5. [Singapore's "temporary" taxi fuel surcharges now run to February 2027 — and the card fee can cost more than the fare add-ons](https://pricehip.com/sg/blog/singapore-headlines/taxi-fare-singapore-surcharges-extended-28-february-2027)

**Source:** PriceHip, updated Nov 21, 2026, checked against ComfortDelGro and TransCab fare pages; MissLobang ride-hail comparison, updated Nov 22, 2026: [pricehip.com](https://pricehip.com/sg/blog/singapore-headlines/taxi-fare-singapore-surcharges-extended-28-february-2027) and [misslobang.com](https://www.misslobang.com/article/best-ride-hailing-apps-singapore-2026)

Temporary taxi and private-hire fuel surcharges in Singapore that were due to end on November 30 now run to February 28, 2027: metered taxis stay one cent higher per distance or waiting unit at S$0.27, Grab private-hire rides keep a S$0.90 surcharge per trip, TADA keeps S$0.90 on trips under S$18 and S$1.20 above, and ComfortDelGro's Zig fixed fares keep a S$0.50–0.80 driver fee. The surcharges were due to lapse three times and were extended each time, and the operators say the money goes to drivers in full to cover fuel costs. The stacked charges are the story: Grab's real fixed cost is S$2.10 a trip — the S$1.20 platform fee plus the S$0.90 surcharge — and Gojek's runs up to S$2.60, while paying a metered taxi fare by card adds 10% plus GST, about S$2.18 on a S$20 meter, which is more than any platform fee on the page; cash costs nothing and NETS costs S$0.30. Public bus and MRT fares are a separate matter and are set to rise on December 26, 2026. The pricing point is that the fare is the smallest part of the price: the fixed charges and the payment method decide which app is cheapest, and the "temporary" surcharge is now in its fourth extension.

## 6. [88% of Americans say they will shop small this weekend — but last year they spent less doing it](https://www.americanexpress.com/en-us/newsroom/amex-for-business/2026-shop-small-impact-study-results/)

**Source:** American Express 2026 Shop Small Impact Study, Nov 12, 2026; NRF / Prosper Insights Thanksgiving weekend survey, Nov 19, 2026, via Kurums, Nov 26: [americanexpress.com](https://www.americanexpress.com/en-us/newsroom/amex-for-business/2026-shop-small-impact-study-results/) and [kurums.com](https://kurums.com/small-business-saturday-2026-sales-playbook/)

Ahead of Small Business Saturday on November 28, American Express reports that 88% of US consumers say they are likely to shop at small businesses this holiday season, the highest level since 2012; 96% of owners say they are using new strategies to prepare, 90% expect to use AI tools for marketing, operations and personalization, and 70% say holiday spending will affect their ability to grow in 2027. But intent is not revenue: Amex estimated last year's Small Business Saturday spending at $18 billion, about 19% below the $22 billion reported for 2024, and the National Retail Federation expects 182.7 million people to shop over Thanksgiving weekend, slightly below last year's 186.9 million, with 82% taking steps to manage costs. The useful detail for sellers is who shows up and why: one in five holiday shoppers plan to buy from small or local businesses, and their reasons are supporting community businesses (71%), unique gifts (56%), handmade work (37%) and quality (33%) — none of them is price. The margin pressure is real on the other side too: the NFIB's October report has a net 33% of owners reporting higher selling prices and a net 37% planning increases in the next three months. One caveat: Amex created Small Business Saturday in 2010 and processes the card payments, so it has a stake in strong turnout. The pricing point is that small shops win on reasons that are not price, which makes discounting into Black Friday the wrong contest — bundles, gift-with-purchase and January bounce-back vouchers protect the margin the markdown gives away.

## 7. [On TikTok Shop, a 30% Black Friday discount can turn a $50 order from break-even into an $11 loss](https://www.595.agency/blog/tiktok-shop-black-friday-2026-seller-guide)

**Source:** 595 Agency seller guide, updated Oct 30, 2026, rates from Webgility's October 2026 guide citing TikTok's program documentation; TikTok Shop campaign price rules via Mediovsky: [595.agency](https://www.595.agency/blog/tiktok-shop-black-friday-2026-seller-guide) and [mediovsky.com](https://mediovsky.com/tiktok-shop-seller-guide/)

TikTok Shop's US Black Friday campaign runs November 11–30, and joining it requires Smart Promotion enrollment and a Shop Performance Score of at least 3.5. The fee stack is what decides whether the sale makes money: a 6% referral fee for most categories — some trade reports cite 8% since August 4, though TikTok's public rate table still lists 6% — plus Smart Promotion at 3.5% normally and 4.5% during campaigns, charged on all store GMV whether the order was discounted or not, plus creator commissions the seller sets and GMV Max ad spend. The worked example is the agency's own assumptions, not TikTok's numbers, but the shape is the point: a $50 product with a $30 cost, a 15% creator commission, $4 of fulfilment and $3 of ads earns $0.25 at full price and loses $10.93 at 30% off, so 1,000 units at that discount lose about $10,900 before overhead. And every discount sets the next price: campaign prices cannot exceed the lowest price customers paid in the past 90 days under the Standard tier, with the Advanced tier taking another 3% off that, and co-funded coupons are funded one part TikTok to nine parts seller in the biggest events. The pricing point is that the platform enforces a reference price on sellers while charging a percentage of everything they sell — the discount is priced twice, once at the till and once in the fee.

## 8. [Salesforce expects AI to touch a fifth of Cyber Week sales — but three in four shoppers won't let an agent pay](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/cyber-week-ai-sales-2026/?bc=OTH)

**Source:** Salesforce Cyber Week 2026 predictions, Nov 19, 2026: [salesforce.com](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/cyber-week-ai-sales-2026/?bc=OTH)

This is a forecast, not results: Salesforce predicts $357 billion in global online sales for Cyber Week, November 24–30, up 6% year over year, with $84 billion in the US, and $75.8 billion of the global figure — 21% — influenced by AI and agents, up from $67 billion in 2025. AI-driven traffic to retailer sites is up 104% year over year, a fifth of holiday shopping traffic will be nonhuman, and shoppers arriving via AI referrals are twice as likely to convert as typical ecommerce shoppers. The adoption split is the story: 31% of consumers have already tried a personal agent such as Meta's Muse or OpenAI's Dots, and nearly 20% use AI tools as the first stop in their holiday shopping — but only 8% have had an agent buy something for them and 73% are not ready to hand over their card details, while 74% say they trust the recommendations AI gives them. Social is the other channel to watch: 23% of ecommerce traffic now comes from social media, up 13% year over year, led by TikTok and Instagram, and discount rates are up 5% globally and 8% in the US in November while average selling prices are down 2% globally. Two caveats: Salesforce sells the agent tools it is measuring, and the figures are predictions built on its Shopping Index of 1.5 billion shoppers. Read against Claude's Visa survey today — only 23% would trust generative AI to make the payment — the pricing point is that AI is winning the comparison and losing the checkout: sellers will compete for the agent's shortlist while still having to win the human's approval to pay.

## 9. [From January, Amazon will count 63% fewer ad impressions — and your CPM will nearly triple without spending a cent more](https://antropico.mx/la-semana-en-marketing-16-23-noviembre-2026/?lang=es)

**Source:** Amazon advertiser notice, Nov 3, 2026, confirmed by Digiday Nov 16, via Antrópico; PPC Land example: [antropico.mx](https://antropico.mx/la-semana-en-marketing-16-23-noviembre-2026/?lang=es)

From January 1, 2027, display impressions bought through Amazon's DVA platform inside its own store will be counted only when the ad appears on screen, not when it starts downloading — and Amazon itself estimates 63% fewer impressions on average in those spaces. The money does not change and the sales do not change, only the yardstick: PPC Land's example has a $5 CPM becoming $13.51 and click-through rising from 0.10% to 0.27% with the same clicks, and Amazon has not clarified whether invoices will be calculated on the new count. Google makes a similar change on February 17, 2027 — Ad Manager and AdSense will count display impressions when they start rendering, and its buying tools will bill on the same basis — calling it a "slight decrease" with no percentage attached, while YouTube's public view counts now include co-view estimates with no effect on revenue. Between January 1 and February 17 the two platforms will count differently, so CPM comparisons across platforms break for six weeks. It is the same theme as the Nov 26 item on Meta's on-device conversion measurement, which went on by default on November 12: the measurement changes first and the price comparison second. The pricing point is practical: before January, download the rendered-impression report alongside the usual one, because your own ratio between the two counts is worth more than Amazon's 63% average.

Item 1, the Which? study.

## Key takeaways

- In the pharmacy experiments, passing through a price increase in the cart hurt new-customer conversion and later purchasing.
- A pooled subscription allowance can be worth different amounts to customers who use different models.
- Rules for advertised discounts can change sellers’ promotion behavior as well as what shoppers see.

## What happened

### 1. A price increase inside the cart causes damage beyond that purchase

**Sources:** [“The Effects of Within-Cart Price Increases on Consumer Behavior”](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7508619) · September 25, 2026 preprint · [papers.ssrn.com](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/7508619.pdf?abstractid=7508619&mirid=1&type=2)

Three randomized field experiments at an Italian pharmacy marketplace compared passing through an unexpected cart-stage increase with honoring the earlier price. Pass-through reduced new-customer conversion by 9.95 percentage points. It also reduced spending and subsequent purchasing over 90 days. Customers commonly removed the affected product, then abandoned the cart minutes later.

**Tags:** price increases, Fairness

### 2. Cursor’s bundle makes “more usage” customer-dependent

**Sources:** [Cursor announcement and discussion](https://forum.cursor.com/t/introducing-the-x-subscription/174450) · October 12, 2026 · [Current pricing](https://x.ai/pricing)

The X Subscription combines Grok, Cursor, Grok Bot, and X features. Paid usage is pooled across products and refreshes weekly. Premium costs $30 monthly, Super $100, and Ultra $200. Third-party models in Cursor are available but billed separately at API prices. Existing Cursor customers retain their included third-party usage until they switch.

**Tags:** Price Bundling, Usage-based pricing

### 3. Apple turns individual subscriptions into adjustable seat purchases

**Source:** [Apple Developer announcement](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=yfo21auu) · October 22, 2026

Organizations and schools can now purchase multiple App Store subscription seats in one transaction, assign them through device-management systems, and add or remove seats as needs change. Multiseat purchasing is enabled by default for developers. Group purchasing for smaller teams and households is planned for winter. Apple did not announce automatic volume discounts.

**Tags:** per-seat pricing, subscription pricing

### 4. Restricting misleading discounts may also reduce genuine promotions

**Sources:** [“Discounting Discounts”](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7064981) · Revised July 31, 2026 · [papers.ssrn.com](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/7064981.pdf?abstractid=7064981&mirid=1&type=2)

This working paper compares Amazon marketplaces inside and outside the EU after sellers were required to base advertised discounts on the lowest preceding 30-day price. Displayed discounts fell substantially. The authors estimate that average prices rose roughly 1–2%, while the frequency of price reductions fell about 10%. Effects were strongest in France and discount-intensive categories. The design is difference-in-differences, not a randomized policy experiment.

**Tags:** Price Promotions, Regulation

### 5. Dynamic pricing’s mental model is more negative than its observed direction

**Sources:** [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/ai-prices-dynamic-customers-technology-64c446cd117d3be664de4ab570fb9857) · October 18, 2026 · [Decodo methodology](https://decodo.com/ecommerce/methodology)

Consumers often interpret dynamic pricing as a mechanism for increases. Yet Decodo’s 2025 tracking found that about half the recorded US price changes were decreases. Its index sampled selected products across e-commerce sites every four hours, so it does not establish overall consumer savings. Electronic shelf labels also enable frequent changes but do not themselves imply surge or personalized pricing.

**Tags:** Dynamic pricing, Price Communications

## Why it matters

**1. A price increase inside the cart causes damage beyond that purchase** The increase changes the transaction’s meaning. It becomes evidence that displayed prices are provisional. Selective price protection can therefore preserve trust and future value, not merely rescue one conversion.

**2. Cursor’s bundle makes “more usage” customer-dependent** A claim such as “twice the usage” lacks a stable meaning when the covered products change. The bundle is attractive to Grok-heavy users but potentially costly for Claude or GPT users. Usage composition, not nominal allowance, determines value.

**3. Apple turns individual subscriptions into adjustable seat purchases** This changes the unit of sale from one user to an adjustable group. It also separates payer from user. Developers should test whether to offer a constant per-seat price, declining blocks, or feature-based tiers. Administrative convenience itself can carry willingness to pay.

**4. Restricting misleading discounts may also reduce genuine promotions** Discount labels attract attention and facilitate comparison. Constraining exaggerated labels may improve information while also weakening promotion-based competition. Transparency policies need both consumer-response and seller-response evaluation.

**5. Dynamic pricing’s mental model is more negative than its observed direction** Price increases are more salient and easier to attribute to exploitation than reductions are to attribute to the algorithm. Practitioners need visible downward revisions, stable-price commitments, and plain explanations of what data are excluded.

## Ideas to apply

- Track repeat purchase as well as conversion when testing cart-price protection.
- Compare an AI bundle’s cost under representative usage patterns. Include charges excluded from the pool.
- When evaluating a reference-price rule, track the frequency of promotions and actual transaction prices as well as advertised savings.

## Key takeaways

- A change in which dates count as peak can matter as much as the change in the fee. See item 1.
- Showing tomorrow’s deal changes what a shopper knows today. It need not create new demand. See item 2.
- An AI estimate can match average willingness to pay and still miss the share of buyers at a given price. See item 5.

## What happened

### 1. JetBlue raised the bag fee and expanded the dates that count as peak

JetBlue’s revised schedule makes all of December peak, rather than just December 17–31. For affected fares booked from November 3, a prepaid first bag on an early-December round trip costs $110 instead of $90. The increase combines a higher fee with a change in which dates qualify for it. (Sources 1, 2.)

**Tags:** price increases, Variable pricing

### 2. Target tells shoppers what tomorrow’s deal will be

Target is keeping its holiday Deal of the Day available one day at a time through December 24. The change this year is that shoppers can see the featured deal a day early. Target has not reported how the preview affects sales. (Source 3.)

**Tags:** Price Promotions, Price Communications

### 3. McDonald’s pricing inquiry puts the nearby restaurant in focus

Lawmakers asked how McDonald’s recommends prices for individual restaurants and whether it pressures franchisees to adopt them. McDonald’s previously told Reuters that recommendations are not based on individual customers and franchisees set their own prices. The inquiry does not establish misconduct. (Source 4.)

**Tags:** Fairness, Algorithmic Pricing

### 4. Toronto gives riders the pass price without asking them to buy a pass

Toronto’s transit agency reported that close to 60,000 riders took 595,000 free trips after reaching its monthly cap in September. Eligible riders pay per trip until they reach 47 paid trips. Subsequent rides that month are free if they use the same card or device. The figures show how many trips qualified, not how many the cap induced. (Source 5.)

**Tags:** Flat-rate pricing, Accessibility

### 5. Synthetic shoppers can get the average right and the demand curve wrong

Four product studies compared AI valuations with human bids that could lead to real purchases. Synthetic respondents valued Dubai chocolate 86% higher on average. For a glass bottle, the average differed by about 3%, yet the estimated demand curves differed in shape. Campus samples limit generalization. (Source 6.)

**Tags:** Academic research, AI in Pricing

## Why it matters

**1. JetBlue raised the bag fee and expanded the dates that count as peak** A familiar label like peak can hide a change in how often customers pay the higher price. Track the share of purchases in each tier as well as the posted rates.

**2. Target tells shoppers what tomorrow’s deal will be** The preview reduces uncertainty about when to shop without extending the purchase window. It may bring shoppers back tomorrow or defer today’s purchase. Measure sales across both days to distinguish new demand from shifted purchases.

**3. McDonald’s pricing inquiry puts the nearby restaurant in focus** A shopper may use a nearby outlet of the same chain as a fairness benchmark. A price that looks reasonable against local competitors may look arbitrary beside another McDonald’s. Test that hypothesis before widening price differences across outlets.

**4. Toronto gives riders the pass price without asking them to buy a pass** A prepaid pass asks customers to forecast usage and risk paying for unused rides. A cap removes that decision. Switching payment methods can split progress toward the cap.

**5. Synthetic shoppers can get the average right and the demand curve wrong** A plausible average can conceal errors in how many customers would buy at the prices under consideration. Validate purchase shares around the proposed price, rather than validating the average bid alone.

## Ideas to apply

- Reprice last season’s bookings under the old and new peak calendars. Separate the effect of the rate change from the effect of moving dates into peak.
- When previewing a one-day deal, measure sales across the preview and promotion days. Include adjacent products to check whether shoppers simply switched what they planned to buy.
- Validate estimated demand at the two or three prices you might actually charge, not just against a mean bid.

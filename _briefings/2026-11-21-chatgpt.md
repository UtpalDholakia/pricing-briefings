## Key takeaways

- A price competes with other uses of money, not just other sellers.
- Publishing the next sale price creates a different deadline from saying only that a sale ends.
- Participation and willingness to pay can tell different stories about the same customer.

## What happened

### 1. Acorns makes the alternative to buying visible
**Source:** [Acorns launch announcement](https://www.acorns.com/learn/acorns-launches-invest-instead-checkout-button-money-therapy-holiday/), November 19, 2026.

Acorns launched Invest Instead, a free Chrome extension that places an alternative button beside retailers' purchase buttons. It shows what the item's price could become at a hypothetical 8% annual return over 30 years. This changes the comparison from another product to future wealth. Acorns says 61% of surveyed customers would likely use such a button. But its 3,242-person survey was voluntary and unweighted. Interest is not evidence that shoppers spend less. The useful experiment is whether making the alternative concrete changes actual purchases, not whether people like the idea.

### 2. A museum tells buyers exactly how much waiting will cost
**Source:** [OMSI membership terms](https://omsi.edu/become-a-member/), undated page checked November 21, 2026; promotion starts November 20.

Portland's Oregon Museum of Science and Industry advertises 20% off memberships through November 30, followed by 15% off from December 1 through January 4. Its OMSI for 2 tier is currently $96 against a displayed $120 annual price. At that same base, the later discount implies $102, a calculation rather than an observed checkout. The published schedule makes the cost of waiting specific: $6, not the entire discount. My interpretation is that this could reduce deadline pressure while preserving a reason to buy earlier. Sales results are not reported.

### 3. Sony extends a regional pricing fence to a new color
**Sources:** [PlayStation Japan announcement](https://blog.ja.playstation.com/2026/10/13/20261013-ps5-dej-black-announcement-s/comment-page-1/), October 13, 2026; [launch-week reminder](https://blog.ja.playstation.com/2026/11/16/20261116-black-friday-sale-s), November 16, 2026.

Sony scheduled its Midnight Black Japanese-only PS5 Digital Edition for November 19 at JPY 55,000 including tax. Like the existing Japanese-only version, it requires Japanese system language and a Japan-region PlayStation account. This is a new color, not a newly invented restriction. The pricing lesson is segmentation through product constraints rather than a coupon or a personalized quote. The restrictions can make the offer less attractive to some overseas buyers while preserving local access. That is an interpretation of the design, not evidence about resale volumes or Sony's incremental profit.

### 4. Apple TV makes the introductory price last six months
**Sources:** [MacRumors announcement](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/11/19/apple-tv-black-friday-streaming-deal/), November 19, 2026; [Apple's offer page](https://www.apple.com/apple-tv/), checked November 21, 2026.

New and qualified returning US subscribers can get Apple TV for $6.99 monthly for up to six months, with the offer ending November 30. Apple's page still lists the regular monthly price as $14.99. The important distinction is a long introductory rate, not a permanent price cut. A longer introduction gives customers more time to learn the service's value. It may also make $6.99 their reference price. Neither effect is measured here. Evaluate paid retention after the discounted period, not just acquisition during it.

### 5. Entering a bidding process does not reveal the same preferences as bidding
**Sources:** [Zeithammer, Stich, Spann and Häubl, journal article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijresmar.2024.08.006), June 2025 issue, first online August 27, 2024; [public full paper](https://www.anderson.ucla.edu/sites/default/files/document/2026-05/1-s2.0-S016781162400079X-main.pdf).

Older discovery: incentivized experiments on costly price offers found that people entered more often than their offer amounts would predict under the tested models. Interactive decision aids and changing the order of entry and bidding did not reconcile the inconsistency. Bids looked more risk-averse than entry choices. This matters for upgrade auctions and other offers that require effort before a price is accepted. Do not infer the whole demand curve from bids alone. These are controlled experiments, not estimates of the effect in an operating airline or marketplace.

## Why it matters

My reading across these cases is that pricing decisions have several parts: what to compare, when to act, whether an offer is usable, and whether to participate. One conversion number can conceal which part changed.

## Ideas to apply

- Test a published discount step-down against an end-date-only message. Measure contribution after the whole promotion, including delayed purchases.
- For an introductory subscription, track usage and paid retention through the first regular-price period.
- In a bidding interface, measure entry, offers, completed purchases and customer earnings separately.

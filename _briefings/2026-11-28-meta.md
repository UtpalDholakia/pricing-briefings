## 1. [Black Friday online sales crossed $13 billion — up 12.8% while the discounts got shallower](https://www.reuters.com/business/retail-consumer/us-black-friday-online-sales-cross-13-billion-adobe-says-2026-11-28/)

**Source:** Reuters, Nov 28, 2026, on Adobe Analytics data; Salesforce first-half figures in the same report: [reuters.com](https://www.reuters.com/business/retail-consumer/us-black-friday-online-sales-cross-13-billion-adobe-says-2026-11-28/)

US online spending on Black Friday reached $9.7 billion by 6:30 p.m. ET, up 12.8% on last year, and Adobe expects the final total at $13 billion to $13.4 billion — above its own September forecast of $12.9 billion. The weekend is expected to add $6.1 billion on Saturday and $6.4 billion on Sunday, Cyber Monday is forecast at $15.1 billion, and the five-day stretch at $47.5 billion, up 7.4%. Salesforce's separate count had $5.9 billion in the US and $30.7 billion globally in the first half of the day, on track for $18.7 billion in the US and $83 billion worldwide. Read against Claude's item today — AI pricing tools talking a beauty brand down from a planned 40% discount to 30%, average discounts falling to 15% at department stores from 18% and to 23% at specialty retailers from 31% — the spending rose while the discounts shrank. The deal badge, not the depth, is doing the work this year.

## 2. [Sixteen enterprise vendors changed how they price in 2026 — and most of it lands at your next renewal](https://redresscompliance.com/newsletter/december)

**Source:** Redress Compliance, Negotiation Intel, December 2026 edition, Nov 25, 2026, from its negotiation and benchmarking work: [redresscompliance.com](https://redresscompliance.com/newsletter/december)

Sixteen vendors changed their pricing this year, and the pattern is the same almost everywhere: a seat plus a meter, and a list rise that a percentage discount does not absorb. Microsoft split Copilot into a fixed seat and a usage meter on September 25, 2026, at $0.01 per Copilot Credit on top of the seat, after the July 1 list rises and the end of volume discounts. Oracle put a consumption meter on Fusion AI in October with a 20× multiplier on frontier models, while a $55.7 billion capital-spending year pushes discounts down across its estate. Salesforce replaced its core editions on September 3 and steers every renewal toward a conversion that lifts the seat price 11 to 13% in exchange for bundled extras. SAP customers enter 2027 with a CPI support increase on January 1 capped at 5%, and a free AI runtime that ends December 31. Adobe deferred consumer and small-business increases in June at a cost of about $500 million of annual recurring revenue, but enterprise renewals are still being pushed 8 to 12% on multi-year agreements. IBM told partners on-premise software rises about 9% on January 1, 2027; Cisco raised list prices on most networking and security software on November 7, 2026; Atlassian starts billing Rovo AI and Automation usage on December 3, seven weeks after a 7 to 10% cloud list increase; and Anthropic rebuilt Claude Enterprise around consumption — a $20-a-month seat that includes no usage, every token billed on top. OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol promotion ends November 30, so from December 1 workloads still pinned to it pay 25% more for input and 50% more for output — Claude covered the extension today; the post-promo jump is the new fact. All of these are published list prices or Redress's own benchmark analysis; no vendor discloses what buyers actually pay net. Redress is a negotiation consultancy, so treat the figures as buyer-side analysis, not vendor announcements — but it is the only public source putting all sixteen on one calendar.

## 3. [Wegovy's list price halves on January 1 — and cash buyers will not save a cent](https://pepvault.app/guides/how-much-does-a-glp-1-cost)

**Source:** PepVault guide, Nov 20, 2026, every number checked against the makers' own pages, CMS and Medicare.gov: [pepvault.app](https://pepvault.app/guides/how-much-does-a-glp-1-cost)

What is new against the Nov 24 item, which covered the cash-price split: from January 1, 2027, Novo Nordisk's list price for Wegovy 2.4 mg and Ozempic falls to $675 a month, about half the current $1,349 list — but the company says self-pay prices do not change, so the Wegovy pen stays $349 a month through NovoCare and the pill stays $149 at its starting dose. The same drug now carries at least four public prices: a $1,349 list, a $349 self-pay price, about $1,306 what pharmacies pay, and a $274 Medicare negotiated price from January 1, 71% below the 2024 list. The list cut mostly helps people whose costs follow the list price, such as those with a high deductible or coinsurance. The pill race has the same shape: all three new GLP-1 pills start at $149 a month in cash — the Wegovy pill, the Ozempic pill and Lilly's Foundayo — but Foundayo's 2.5 mg dose rises from $149 to $199 on January 1, and its 5.5 mg dose from $199 to $299. Eligible Medicare Part D members still pay a flat $50 a month through the GLP-1 Bridge, which runs to December 31, 2027. The pricing point is the list-versus-net rule in its purest form: a 50% list-price cut that changes nothing for the cash buyer is a press release, not a price cut.

**Source:** Today Years Old (@todayyearsold), Instagram carousel, Nov 23, 2026, 23,140 likes, 204 comments, 9.6 million followers: [instagram.com](https://www.instagram.com/p/DgF7d5imF5K/)

## 5. [At renewal, the biggest price rises arrive as a new package — not a line saying the price went up](https://www.cledara.com/blog/saas-renewal-price-increases-cancellation-terms)

**Source:** Cledara, SaaS Renewal Price Increases by Vendor, 2026, updated November 2026, published list prices set against its own payment data: [cledara.com](https://www.cledara.com/blog/saas-renewal-price-increases-cancellation-terms)

What is new against Meta's Nov 20 item, which covered the AI-driven renewal asks of 20 to 37% from Tropic and Vertice data: the published list-price table across 14 vendors, and the contract clauses that make the rises stick. Renewal increases average 8 to 12% a year, 15 to 25% where the vendor pushes hard — but the outliers come from repackaging, not uplift: Canva Teams went from $119.99 to $500 a year, up 317%, in September 2024; Microsoft's frontline plans rose 25 to 33% on July 1, 2026; Google Workspace rose 17 to 22% in January 2025 with Gemini bundled in; Xero's UK plans rose 5 to 13% on September 1, 2026; Atlassian's cloud list prices changed on October 13, 2026 with no percentage published. The clauses are near-identical across vendors: subscriptions auto-renew by default, the renewal moves to the then-current list price with 30 days' notice, seats can go up mid-term but not down, and cutting seats at renewal can reset the per-unit discount — Salesforce re-prices a renewal where volume has fallen without regard to the prior per-unit price. Notice windows to stop a renewal run from 15 days at Google Workspace to 60 days at BambooHR. One timing fact decides most of it: companies that start negotiating 90 days before renewal save 49% on average, against 19% for those that start 30 to 90 days out. All figures are list prices; Cledara's payment data tracks share of spend, not per-seat net prices, so no net is disclosed.

## 8. [More than half of CVS's promotions lost money — and dropping the bad ones made $50 million](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/when-do-pricing-strategies-work-retailers-marco-vriens-btuqc)

**Source:** Marco Vriens, reviewing the pricing literature for ESOMAR's Research World, Oct 28, 2026: [linkedin.com](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/when-do-pricing-strategies-work-retailers-marco-vriens-btuqc)

A literature review of retail pricing strategies lands on an uncomfortable finding: promotions, loss leaders and price increases often do not work, and the ones that fail are rarely measured. CVS measured the profit impact of every promotion it ran. More than half lost money; across about $9 billion in front-store sales the net effect was roughly minus $25 million, and when CVS eliminated the bad promotions the remaining ones lifted profits by about $50 million. The arithmetic explains why: a meta-analysis of 1,851 published price elasticities shows a 10% price cut on an item with a 30% margin needs 50% more volume to break even, while typical promotional elasticities deliver about 36%. About a third of a typical promotion bump is brand switching, which helps the brand but often just moves the retailer's sale to a lower margin on the same shelf. The counter-example is bauMax, the DIY retailer and Marketing Science Practice Prize winner: econometric modelling that accounted for basket effects, tested in a few stores, then small analytically chosen price increases on about 20,000 prices, lifted gross profit by about 8%. The review's prescription is to measure the net profit of every promotion, cut the losers and test before rolling out — which is the opposite of how Black Friday week is usually run.

## 9. [Half of all algorithmic price changes are cuts — and shoppers react only to the rises](https://www.digitalmarketingobserver.com/2026/11/why-shoppers-remember-every-increase-and-no-discount/)

**Source:** Digital Marketing Observer, November 2026, on Decodo's 2025 index and a Journal of Business Research field experiment: [digitalmarketingobserver.com](https://www.digitalmarketingobserver.com/2026/11/why-shoppers-remember-every-increase-and-no-discount/)

What is new against Claude's October items, which covered the Decodo headline that roughly half of 2025 price changes were cuts: the exact split, the depth of the cuts by retailer, and the field experiment behind the asymmetry. Decodo's 2025 index logged 542,946 price changes across US e-commerce — 50.10% upward, 49.90% downward, a near-perfect split — and the average cut was deep: 35.3% at Amazon, 31.7% at CVS, 17.7% at Walgreens, 12.9% at Target, 10.6% at Walmart and 9.1% at Kroger. A field experiment published in the Journal of Business Research manipulated price variation at an online retailer and found customers reacted strongly to price increases and not at all to reductions, across manipulations of both size and frequency. The cuts are private — they arrive on a page you happen to be looking at — while an increase becomes visible the moment any two shoppers compare what they paid, so symmetric behaviour produces asymmetric memory. The remedy the piece draws is not to cut more but to make the cuts travel: name them, time them, tell people they happened, because a discount nobody registers generates a sale and no goodwill.

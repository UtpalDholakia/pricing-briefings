## 1. US personalized-pricing law moves from disclosure to outright bans

**Source:** Sidley Data Matters: [datamatters.sidley.com](https://datamatters.sidley.com/2026/10/27/from-disclosures-to-prohibitions-the-new-legal-landscape-on-the-use-of-consumer-data-for-personalized-pricing/)

A clear map of where things stand as of Oct 27. Maryland's ban on data-driven dynamic pricing for food took effect Oct 1. New Jersey's law (effective 2027) is the first with a private right of action. Seattle signed an ordinance against "algorithmic-based price discrimination" on Oct 5, New York's broad One Fair Price Act awaits the governor, and the FTC's proposed policy statement on personalized pricing has closed for comment but is not final. For researchers, the patchwork (sector bans, disclosure-only regimes, a city-level rule) creates natural experiments on whether disclosure alone changes fairness perceptions or behavior.

## 2. LLM pricing agents keep colluding even after the incentive is gone

**Source:** arXiv (Cahyono, Oct 29): [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39660) ; companion paper (Tran & Yu, Oct 20): [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28787)

The first paper finds that collusion among LLM agents persists after the reward is removed and can re-form with new partners, even ones from different model families; only wiping memory reliably ends it. The second shows that RL-trained LLM sellers reach cartel-like prices mostly through rules that ignore rivals, not through punishment, so outcome measures alone can't tell real enforcement from coincidence. Together they suggest supra-competitive prices from AI sellers may look like tacit collusion while working through quite different mechanisms. That matters for how we interpret "algorithmic" price levels and how consumers judge their fairness.

## 3. Startups profit from airline price drops as Alaska ends fare-drop refunds

**Source:** Reuters via The Star (Oct 30): [thestar.com.my](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/10/30/as-us-airlines-deploy-ai-pricing-tools-startups-help-travelers-cash-in-on-fare-drops)

JetBack, Junova and Refare watch fares after purchase and claim the difference when a price falls. Junova's data on 10,000 flights show about 22% had at least one post-booking drop, and 40% of those dropped more than once. Alaska stopped honoring fare-drop requests through customer service in early October. The behavioral angle: most recoveries come back as airline credit rather than cash, a mental-accounting lock-in that may make these refunds cheap for airlines. It also opens questions about buyer's remorse and what happens to reference prices when post-purchase price monitoring is outsourced to a third party.

**Type:** Practice · **Tags:** AI in Pricing, Behavioral Economics, Credit-based pricing

## 4. Cocoa costs ease, but Hershey and Mondelez won't cut prices; they'll change packs

**Source:** Hershey Q3 call (Oct 29): [fool.com](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/10/29/hershey-hsy-q3-2026-earnings-call-transcript/) ; Mondelez Q3 call (Oct 30): [fool.com](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/10/30/mondelez-mdlz-q3-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)

Hershey's CEO said its value strategy is "not about rolling back prices." Instead it will use targeted promotions, pack changes and new entry price points, such as a cheaper Ice Breakers gum stick. Mondelez says most of its 2027 price moves will come through price-pack architecture, and it is reinvesting cocoa savings in brands rather than lower shelf prices. This is "rockets and feathers" asymmetry stated openly by management. It is also a live test of whether consumers' reference prices adjust down when the cost story they were told reverses.

## 5. Grocers compete on price image and entry price points, not broad price cuts

**Source:** Grocery Dive on Sprouts (Oct 29): [grocerydive.com](https://www.grocerydive.com/news/sprouts-farmers-market-third-quarter-earnings-affordability-pricing-strategy/873111/) ; on Walmart's holiday basket: [grocerydive.com](https://grocerydive.com/news/walmart-holiday-meal-2026-cheaper-2025/873073)

After three straight quarterly declines in comparable-store sales, Sprouts' incoming CEO plans a January repricing. It will add lower entry points, cut prices on frequently bought "trust-building" items, and explicitly avoid broad discounting, which reads like a textbook use of key value items to shape price image. Walmart, meanwhile, frames its $35 holiday basket as having 18% more items than last year and costing 12% less for the same items. That double reference-price comparison is worth noting for anyone studying bundle framing.

## 6. Customers who renew after a price hike may be captive, not loyal

**Source:** The Commitment Gap, Jon Buchwald (Oct 28): [jonbuchwald.substack.com](https://jonbuchwald.substack.com/p/why-angry-customers-keep-paying)

Buchwald argues that renewal after a price increase often reflects inattention and switching costs, not satisfaction. He draws on Deloitte data showing 45% of streaming households feel they overpay and that cancel-and-return churn is rising, and on the Einav–Klopack–Mahoney finding that inattention and cancellation friction roughly double subscription revenue. His B2B example, in which renewals held while upsells and referrals disappeared after a price hike, suggests a useful construct: grudging retention as a hidden cost of price increases that retention metrics miss.

**Type:** Opinion · **Tags:** subscription pricing, Subscription Renewals, price increases, B2B pricing

## 7. New shrinkflation cases where dosing instructions and labels stay the same

**Source:** Mouse Print (Oct 26): [mouseprint.org](https://www.mouseprint.org/2026/10/26/here-we-shrink-again-fall-2026-part-2/)

Edgar Dworsky's fall roundup includes Cheer detergent going from 92 to 81 oz while keeping the same cap and dosing instructions. It also notes Lucky Charms keeping its "family size" label as contents shrink, and Sara Lee and Fudgicles cutting item counts. The unchanged labels and instructions are the interesting part. They exploit consumers' reliance on familiar cues and habits rather than just the hard-to-notice ounce change, a mechanism less studied than pure size perception.

## 8. Restaurant surcharges have stayed on even as menu prices kept rising

**Source:** I'll Be Honest..., Kit Graham (Oct 20, just outside the one-week window): [kitgraham.substack.com](https://kitgraham.substack.com/p/squeezed-increased-menu-prices-and)

Graham tracks Chicago-area menus. Restaurants kept 3–4% "in lieu of price increases" surcharges while raising item prices 4–84%, and the same group charges surcharges in some cities but not others despite identical menu prices. She also shows how a surcharge compounds through tax and tip, inflating one check about 37% over subtotal. It is rare item-level field evidence that partitioned fees outlast the reason given for them, and the Reddit "offenders list" shows how fairness backlash organizes itself.

**Type:** Opinion · **Tags:** Partitioned Pricing, Fairness, Restaurant pricing, Menu pricing, Hospitality, concert ticket pricing, price increases
{: .item-meta}

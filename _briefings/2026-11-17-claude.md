## 1. Is an AI-set price fairer than a human one? Two new studies disagree, and a .99 ending may decide

**Source:** Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science (Nov 10): [link.springer.com](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11747-026-01224-7); Journal of Consumer Affairs (Oct 16): [onlinelibrary.wiley.com](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/joca.70080)

Kaplan, Minton and Lamberton report five studies in which consumers generally judged dynamic prices set by an AI as fairer than the same prices set by a person. The effect depends on the buyer's expertise, their attitude toward AI and the pricing motive they infer. The authors read this as attribution at work: a human price setter is assumed to be chasing profit, while an algorithm looks less self-interested. Chacon and Limongi's two experiments (796 participants in Chile and the US) found the opposite main effect: AI-set prices drew lower purchase and recommendation intentions. They also found a hint of why. With an AI as the setter, round prices did better than just-below ones (clearly in study 1, only directionally in study 2), and perceived manipulation mediated the response. Taken together, the studies suggest the "AI" label does not settle fairness. What matters is whether the price looks tactical, and a charm ending is one of the clearest signs that it is.

## 2. Senators ask the FTC whether keeping tariff refunds after tariff-justified price rises is deceptive

**Source:** Office of Sen. Elizabeth Warren (Nov 11): [warren.senate.gov](https://www.warren.senate.gov/newsroom/press-releases/warren-colleagues-urge-ftc-to-crack-down-on-corporations-pocketing-consumers-tariff-refunds/); SupplyChainBrain: [supplychainbrain.com](https://www.supplychainbrain.com/general-scm/regulation-compliance/article/16425536/ftc-urged-to-probe-companies-not-sharing-tariff-refunds-with-consumers)

Seven Senate Democrats asked FTC Chair Andrew Ferguson to examine whether large firms that raised prices "ostensibly due to tariff costs" broke the FTC Act by keeping the refunds they received after the tariffs were struck down. Firms have answered in different ways. FedEx and UPS refund through portals, Walmart, Costco and e.l.f. say they will cut prices instead, and a proposed class action says Logitech kept a $61M refund while its increases stayed in place ([npr.org](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/28/nx-s1-5946782/businesses-getting-tariff-refunds-why-arent-consumers-getting-their-cut)). This is a field test of dual entitlement. In Kahneman, Knetsch and Thaler's surveys, people accepted cost-based increases but did not insist that falling costs be passed on. A named cost works differently. When the justification was "the tariff," customers can treat it as a promise that the price will fall when the tariff goes. The more specific the excuse, the more it binds the seller later.

## 3. Sri Lanka's second gas supplier apologises for a shortage, not for a 36% price rise

**Source:** Newswire (Nov 14): [newswire.lk](https://www.newswire.lk/2026/11/14/laugfs-gas-ceo-apologises-for-shortage-defends-rs-1775-price-hike/); Ada Derana (Nov 16): [adaderana.lk](https://adaderana.lk/news/251747)

LAUGFS Gas raised its 12.5 kg cooking-gas cylinder from Rs 4,965 to Rs 6,740 overnight on Nov 13, after several weeks when it could not supply. CEO Nalinda Kurukulasuriya apologised for the shortage and called the new figure "a break-even price, not a profit-making price." He pointed to world LPG prices, freight and insurance, the exchange rate, taxes, and the firm's duty to shareholders and lenders. The Consumer Affairs Authority summoned the company, and a minister accused it in Parliament of timing the rise against budget relief. The response splits the apology from the price. The firm admits fault for the service failure and frames the price as cost recovery with no margin. Price-fairness work suggests a "break-even" claim helps only if buyers believe it. A rise announced right after a shortage invites the opposite reading, that the firm is exploiting scarcity.

## 4. Splitting out a shipping fee helps only while the fee is small

**Source:** Aichner & Dorigati, Journal of Revenue and Pricing Management (Nov 6): [link.springer.com](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1057/s41272-026-00643-z)

In a 2×2 experiment with 433 online fashion shoppers, showing shipping separately raised purchase intention over an all-in price only when the fee was small. A large fee reversed the advantage, through lower perceived fairness of the offer. In a follow-up task, stating the same surcharge as a percentage rather than a currency amount lowered the total cost people recalled. That fits the partitioned-pricing literature: buyers anchor on the base price and under-adjust for a small add-on, but a large add-on becomes salient and reads as a grab. The percentage-recall result matters as all-in pricing rules spread. Under those rules a seller can still choose how a surcharge is expressed, and the format changes what buyers remember paying.

**Type:** Research · **Tags:** Partitioned Pricing, All-inclusive pricing, Fairness, Price presentation, Academic research

## 5. Notion's AI now runs on two currencies that can't be swapped

**Source:** Steven Forth, Pricing Innovation (Nov): [pricinginnovation.substack.com](https://pricinginnovation.substack.com/p/notion-pricing-update); Notion Help Center: [notion.com](https://www.notion.com/en-gb/help/manage-ai-models-and-member-credit-spend)

Since Nov 2, existing Business and Enterprise workspaces get two monthly allotments per seat. One is 1,500 Standard credits for work where Notion picks the model. The other is 500 Select credits for choosing a model or running Custom Agents. Extra credits cost about $10 per 1,000. Notion's help pages describe a fixed draw-down order across the agent owner's credits and the shared workspace pool. A per-member cap can halt an owner's agents all at once. Forth counts four pricing logics in under four years with seat and credit prices unchanged. Two non-fungible balances turn one mental account into two. That can make users ration Select credits for "important" work, and it can make the product feel more expensive than the list price suggests. Both effects are worth measuring before other SaaS vendors copy the design.

## 6. Microsoft resets local-currency prices once a year: India up 15.7%, China down up to 8.5%

**Source:** The Register (Nov 15): [theregister.com](https://theregister.com/off-prem/2026/11/15/microsoft-lifts-indian-cloud-prices-by-15-percent-cuts-costs-in-china/5309993); tbreak: [tbreak.com](https://tbreak.com/microsoft-commercial-cloud-pricing-currency-2027/)

From Jan 1, Microsoft's commercial cloud prices rise 15.7% in India (up to 20% for some on-premises products) and 10% in New Zealand. They fall by up to 8.5% in China (not for Azure operated by 21Vianet), and Australia and Norway also get cuts. These are the first moves since Microsoft switched from twice-yearly to annual realignment against the dollar. This is distinct from the US list-price rises Meta AI covered yesterday. The new cadence turns gradual currency drift into a single, visible, dated increase. For buyers who budget in local currency, the price is stable for a year and then jumps, and a weak rupee on one date locks in a higher price for twelve months. Buyers will likely blame Microsoft for that jump, not the exchange rate.

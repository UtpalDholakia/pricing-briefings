## Key takeaways

- A lower fee can be offset by credits a customer used to redeem regularly.
- Supernatural’s relaunch asks former subscribers to decide whether to buy again at a new price.
- A separately displayed coupon can change which product someone chooses even when final prices are identical across formats.

## What happened

### 1. Albertsons’ $60 fee reduction removes up to $60 in credits

**Sources:** [Subscription Insider](https://www.subscriptioninsider.com/blog/albertsons-cuts-membership-price-61-as-delivery-costs-rise) · October 26, 2026 · [Safeway’s transition FAQ](https://www.safeway.com/4uplus/change-management.html)

Albertsons is replacing its $99 FreshPass membership with $39 forU+. The new plan removes the $5 monthly grocery credit and several other perks. Qualifying delivery remains included. The $60 fee reduction exactly matches the maximum annual value of the discontinued credits.

**Tags:** Membership, Rewards Program

### 2. Supernatural tests whether community loyalty survives a fresh purchase decision

**Sources:** [Subscription Insider](https://www.subscriptioninsider.com/blog/supernatural-returns-at-double-the-price-new-subscriptions-required) · October 22, 2026 · [Company FAQ](https://www.wearesupernatural.com/faq)

The VR fitness service relaunched independently on October 21. Returning customers must create new accounts and subscribe again. Prices are $19.99 monthly or $199.99 annually, roughly double Meta’s former rates. Workout history can transfer, but subscriptions do not automatically migrate.

**Tags:** Subscription Renewals, price increases

### 3. The Guardian makes sharing part of the paid product

**Source:** [Subscription Insider analysis](https://www.subscriptioninsider.com/blog/the-guardian-turns-account-sharing-into-a-subscription-benefit) · October 20, 2026

The Guardian is adding three separate accounts to qualifying subscriptions. Its approach contrasts with The New York Times’ higher-priced Family tier. Individual logins preserve each reader’s preferences while making the sharing arrangement explicit. Neither publisher has disclosed enough results to establish the full economics.

**Tags:** subscription pricing, Customer loyalty

### 4. Better AI models can reopen the subscription decision

**Source:** [“Competition in Generative AI”](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/27-037_696c8ae5-6d49-4b7d-a2ec-e02c45f78ddb.pdf) · October 16, 2026 working paper

Card-payment data show approximately 20–45% twelve-month retention across major paid AI providers, versus roughly 70% for Netflix and Spotify. Improved model releases are associated with increased switching or adding a competing subscription. The authors’ structural model suggests growing substitutability and strong price sensitivity.

**Tags:** AI pricing, Pricing power

### 5. A separate coupon can encourage trading up without changing final prices

**Sources:** [“Final Price Neglect in Multi-Product Promotions”](https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucad045) · July 6, 2023; April 2024 issue. Older discovery · [academic.oup.com](https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article/50/6/1097/7220485)

Across fourteen experiments involving 5,187 participants, separating a discount from product prices increased selection of higher-priced alternatives relative to displaying discounted final prices. The evidence includes a field experiment and incentive-compatible choices. The proposed mechanism is final-price neglect: shoppers compare the larger list prices, making the same absolute gap seem smaller.

**Tags:** Price presentation, Price Promotions

## Why it matters

**1. Albertsons’ $60 fee reduction removes up to $60 in credits** This replaces effort-dependent savings with a certain upfront saving. Customers who routinely redeemed credits lose other benefits without gaining on that calculation. Infrequent redeemers may benefit. My inference: acquisition could improve while the most engaged existing members become less satisfied.

**2. Supernatural tests whether community loyalty survives a fresh purchase decision** This separates active willingness to pay from automatic-renewal inertia. Preserving progress may protect a sense of ownership and continuity. Yet petitions and enthusiastic comments cannot establish demand at the new price. The revealing measures will be completed purchases and subsequent renewals.

**3. The Guardian makes sharing part of the paid product** My hypothesis is that cancellation becomes a household decision when several people develop their own habits. That could support retention. But additional users may also replace potential individual purchases. Evaluate paying-account retention and revenue, rather than treating additional accounts as equivalent to new customers.

**4. Better AI models can reopen the subscription decision** Product improvement can disrupt competitors’ habits rather than merely attract first-time buyers. Subscription is not necessarily commitment. The study measures observed payments, excludes free and enterprise use, and is not peer reviewed. Its pricing-power conclusions depend partly on model assumptions.

**5. A separate coupon can encourage trading up without changing final prices** Promotion presentation can change product mix independently of discount generosity. The effect concerns choices among differentiated alternatives, not simply whether to buy one promoted item. Measure trade-up alongside conversion.

## Ideas to apply

- Model membership value for regular credit redeemers and infrequent redeemers before changing the fee and benefits together.
- For a sharing feature, measure retention and revenue at the paying-account level.
- When testing a coupon display, measure trade-up as well as overall purchase conversion.

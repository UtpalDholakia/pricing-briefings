## 1. A court rules that disclosing the total price at checkout doesn't fix a deceptive teaser price.

**Source:** All About Advertising Law — [google.com](https://www.google.com/url?q=https://www.allaboutadvertisinglaw.com/2026/10/court-allows-ftc-drip-pricing-lawsuit-against-ticketmaster-to-proceed.html&source=gmail&ust=1792587567908000&sa=E)

A California federal court denied Live Nation/Ticketmaster's motion to dismiss the FTC's drip-pricing suit, in which fees allegedly added up to 44%. The court relied on the Ninth Circuit's "deceptive first contact" doctrine and called the "+Fees" label too vague to say what buyers will pay. A Maryland court had dismissed a Vivid Seats class action on the opposite reasoning: no reliance because the total was shown before purchase. That split is a live legal test of whether anchoring on the partitioned base price counts as harm.

## 2. EU consumer regulators target in-game currencies as a way of hiding prices.

**Source:** Lewis Silkin — [google.com](https://www.google.com/url?q=https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/10/15/eu-consumer-protection-network-launches-action-involving-nine-video-games-compani-102o5do&source=gmail&ust=1792587567908000&sa=E)

The EU's CPC Network began coordinated action against nine game companies and opened a separate probe into a tenth. It wants real-money price equivalents shown, no layered currencies or exchange steps that hide the true cost, and no forcing players to buy large currency packs to progress. This turns the token and mental-accounting literature (payment decoupling, reduced pain of paying) into an enforcement theory. Industry groups dispute how the regulators classify these currencies.

## 3. Field data: after big menu price hikes, negative price sentiment rose about twice as much in the US as in the UK.

**Source:** Realytics via IT Business Net — [google.com](https://www.google.com/url?q=https://itbusinessnet.com/2026/10/realytics-study-finds-twice-the-backlash-after-big-menu-price-hikes-in-the-us-vs-the-uk/&source=gmail&ust=1792587567908000&sa=E)

Realytics compared restaurants that raised prices with same-market venues that didn't, across 15 markets. After large hikes (a quarter or more of menu items up, typically 8%+), the share of negative price opinions rose 5.4 points in the US and 2.3 points in the UK. Hikes under about 5% barely registered, which fits a just-noticeable-difference or fairness-threshold account rather than a linear one. It's an industry dataset, the results are correlational, and the US and UK samples differ in chain mix.

## 4. LLM pricing agents can sustain high prices, and how they're deployed matters more than which model is used.

**Source:** arXiv, Bichler et al., TU Munich — [google.com](https://www.google.com/url?q=https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21711&source=gmail&ust=1792587567908000&sa=E)

In repeated Bertrand games, LLM sellers that see raw price histories including rival prices held prices above competitive levels. Summarized histories pushed outcomes toward competition. Mixed populations produced "anchor-and-harvest" dynamics: one agent holds a high price that serves as an anchor while another undercuts it. The open question is what reference prices consumers learn when the market's anchors are set by agents rather than people.

**Type:** Research · **Tags:** Academic research, Algorithmic Pricing, AI in Pricing, Behavioral Economics

## 6. Australia's card-surcharge ban turns small fees into round-number price rises, and the politics are messy.

**Source:** ABC News (Australia) — [google.com](https://www.google.com/url?q=https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-10-10/labor-reverses-credit-card-surcharge-call-after-backlash/107247904&source=gmail&ust=1792587567909000&sa=E)

The RBA's surcharge ban was sold as cost-of-living relief. ABC argues it is really a transparency measure, since card costs were always going to be passed on. A widely shared satire showed a $5 coffee plus an 8-cent surcharge becoming a flat $5.50. That makes it a natural experiment in whether moving from partitioned to all-in pricing brings rounding up to price points and less fee salience.

## 7. A Los Angeles restaurant stacks a 5% admin fee on top of a mandatory 20% tip, and Reddit reacts.

**Source:** Hoodline — [google.com](https://www.google.com/url?q=https://hoodline.com/2026/10/downtown-la-rooftop-spot-uca-terraza-slammed-over-surcharge-on-top-of-mandatory-tip&source=gmail&ust=1792587567909000&sa=E)

At üca terraza, a $173 subtotal became $226 after a disclosed 5% "administrative" surcharge, the mandatory gratuity and tax. The fee was disclosed under California's surcharge law and was still read as extortion. That suggests disclosure doesn't neutralize the fairness penalty when fees stack and their stated purpose (wages) overlaps with the tip.

## 8. UK CMA after 18 months of direct enforcement: drip-pricing fines are now routine, and delivery-fee disclosure is next.

**Source:** CMA blog — [google.com](https://www.google.com/url?q=https://competitionandmarkets.blog.gov.uk/2026/10/14/direct-consumer-enforcement-18-months-on/&source=gmail&ust=1792587567909000&sa=E)

The CMA now has 20 investigations and has imposed over £11.8M in fines and £2.3M in redress. It settled 7 of 8 online-pricing cases, including Wayfair and Gold's Gym, and flags pre-selected add-ons and misleading discounts. This is the first jurisdiction enforcing all-in pricing with real fines, so before-and-after conversion and price-level data should start to appear.

## 9. Fall 2026 shrinkflation round-up: counts quietly cut while the package looks the same.

**Source:** Mouseprint — [google.com](https://www.google.com/url?q=https://www.mouseprint.org/2026/10/17/here-we-shrink-again-fall-2026-part-1/&source=gmail&ust=1792587567909000&sa=E)

Examples include Hershey's Kisses (10 to 8.7 oz), Ziploc freezer bags (16 fewer per box), Stop & Shop store-brand oatmeal (10 to 8 packets), Dixie plates (5 fewer) and Eight O'Clock coffee (minus 2 oz). Store brands now shrink alongside national brands, which complicates the usual account that private labels are the honest price benchmark.

## Key takeaways

- A sold-out message captures one point in time. It does not explain why tickets later return. See item 1.
- A fixed dollar price and a percentage discount invite different comparisons. To compare the formats, keep the product and timing the same. See item 3.
- In the holiday survey, returns and clear reference prices scored much higher on trust than real-time scarcity messages. That is survey evidence, not a conversion result. See item 4.

## What happened

### 1. When a sold-out VIP ticket comes back 20 minutes later

On November 11, Theme Park IQ logged a December 4 Universal Studios Hollywood VIP ticket as sold out at 7:20 a.m. Pacific and available again at 7:40. The tracker recorded a similar reversal for the same date the day before. It shows what was available at each check. It cannot tell us why inventory returned. (Source 1.)

**Tags:** High demand, Price Communications

### 2. At Chipotle, a drawn-on tattoo counts

On November 13 from 3 to 4 p.m. local time, participating Chipotle restaurants in the U.S. and Canada offered a second entrée free to qualifying in-store customers with a tattoo. Temporary and drawn-on tattoos count. Chipotle says the March edition coincided with a company sales record. It did not report incremental profit. (Source 2.)

**Tags:** Price Promotions, Social processes, QSR Pricing

### 3. Casey’s switches from $12 to 40% off

Casey’s announced that it would sell its new pan pizza for $12 plus tax from November 25 to 27. On November 28, its existing Saturday offer takes 40% off eligible pizzas. Both require a code in the app. The offers cover different products and occasions, so their results would not tell us which price format works better. (Source 3.)

**Tags:** Price presentation, Menu pricing

### 4. Shoppers say returns policies make holiday deals more credible

In BCG’s ten-market survey, a money-back or free-returns policy scored 65 percentage points on net trust. A clear comparison between regular and sale prices scored 58 points. Real-time scarcity messages scored eight. These are differences between the shares who said a cue raised or lowered trust. They are not changes in purchase rates. (Source 4.)

**Tags:** Price transparency, Consumer decision making

### 5. Who pays for an ad-funded discount?

In two experiments, Hyo-Jung Kim and Jin-Myong Lee compared two ways of describing an ad-funded discount. Presenting it as a share of advertising revenue passed back to customers made the offer seem more legitimate. That judgment was associated with higher purchase intentions. The researchers measured intentions rather than sales. (Source 5.)

**Tags:** Academic research, Fairness, Cross-subsidization

## Why it matters

**1. When a sold-out VIP ticket comes back 20 minutes later** Shoppers can read “sold out” as evidence of strong demand or as a reason to buy the next available date. If inventory can return, test whether a waitlist or restock alert keeps customers who would otherwise leave.

**2. At Chipotle, a drawn-on tattoo counts** The tattoo is a small, visible act of participation. It gives customers something to do together and a reason to show up in a quiet hour. Compare margin across the full afternoon with a plain off-peak offer to see what the ritual adds.

**3. Casey’s switches from $12 to 40% off** The fixed price makes it easy to budget for a meal. The percentage leaves more choice open, and the dollar savings grow with the price of the pizza. To isolate the framing effect, compare equivalent offers on the same pizza for the same occasion.

**4. Shoppers say returns policies make holiday deals more credible** A returns policy gives the buyer recourse if the purchase disappoints. A price comparison helps them judge the savings before committing. Test each at the same markdown and track completed purchases and returns.

**5. Who pays for an ad-funded discount?** When customers pay partly with their attention, they may want to know what they get in return. If advertising genuinely helps fund a lower price, test a plain explanation of that exchange alongside your usual promotional copy.

## Ideas to apply

- If inventory comes back regularly, compare a restock alert with the default sold-out page. Track whether shoppers return or move to another date.
- Match a participatory promotion to a plain offer for the same item and time window. Compare margin across the full afternoon.
- If ads fund a real discount, test whether explaining that exchange changes perceived legitimacy and completed purchases.

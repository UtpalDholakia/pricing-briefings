## 1. Left-digit bias as an attention threshold, tested with a tax experiment (Kaushil Patel, arXiv econ.GN)

**Source:** [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05342)

Patel models left-digit bias as a consideration problem rather than a perception error. Each consumer only considers a product whose price is below a personal attention threshold, and when many of those thresholds sit at whole dollars, demand drops sharply at those points. A surprising prediction follows: left-digit-biased consumers respond less to an added tax than rational consumers do, and the paper fits this to tax-experiment data. For welfare analysis, a weak demand response does not mean a small excess burden. This links charm pricing, tax salience and consideration sets in a single model.

## 2. BCG's 10,000-person Black Friday study: half of shoppers doubt discounts are real (BCG via PR Newswire)

**Source:** [prnewswire.com](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/only-54-of-consumers-trust-that-black-friday-discounts-are-lower-than-normal-as-promotion-fatigue-grows-302928764.html)

Across ten countries, only 54% believe Black Friday prices are actually lower than usual. 22% plan to spend less because they assume discounts are always available, which looks like reference-price erosion at the level of the whole retail calendar. The discount people say they need to see a "good deal" slipped to about 30%. Scarcity countdowns and AI review summaries were among the least trusted cues, and a money-back guarantee was one of the most trusted. One more finding: comparing prices is the task people now trust AI with most, which could change who forms the reference price.

## 3. What the FTC is asking airlines, ride-hail, delivery and big-box retailers about personalized pricing (Skift)

**Source:** [skift.com](https://skift.com/2026/11/04/what-the-ftc-wants-to-know-about-how-airlines-set-fares/)

The FTC's October 28 Section 6(b) orders went to Delta, United, JetBlue, Uber, Lyft, DoorDash, Instacart, Target and Walmart. The orders define "pricing" broadly enough to include discounts, surge multipliers and tips, and they also cover gig-worker pay and which workers are offered which jobs. American Airlines was left out, which suggests a sampling approach. The study matters because it targets the firms that actually set prices; the 2024 study looked only at consultants. For researchers, the main question is whether the line between dynamic and personalized pricing holds up once the companies' documents are examined.

## 4. UK CMA warns retailers before Black Friday that delivery charges belong in the headline price (Lewis Silkin)

**Source:** [lewissilkin.com](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/11/03/cma-issues-warning-to-online-retailers-about-delivery-fees-102o7ck)

Under the DMCC Act, mandatory delivery charges must be in the total price at every stage, from the ad to the payment page. Optional fees can't be hidden behind dropdowns or pop-outs. Marketplaces have to build each seller's delivery charge into that product's price. This is one of the strictest regulatory definitions of partitioned versus all-in pricing so far, and it sets up a natural experiment on shipping-fee salience at UK retailers. The CMA's drip-pricing fines so far total more than £5.1 million.

**Type:** Regulation · **Tags:** Price transparency, Partitioned Pricing, Regulation, Pricing Laws, Legal issues in pricing, Price Promotions, retailing

## 5. Australia's CHOICE gives Ticketmaster and Ticketek "Shonky" awards for "In Demand" seat pricing (TicketNews)

**Source:** [ticketnews.com](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/11/australian-consumer-group-ticketing-giants-in-demand-pricing/)

The details are useful for fairness research. NRL "In Demand" seats cost A$175 against A$125 for regular seats one row or bay away, so a small difference in quality carried a large price gap. BTS fans only saw prices once they were in the virtual queue. CHOICE argues that waiting rooms and short checkout timers add time pressure on top of dynamic pricing. Another winner, Quest protein chips at roughly 886% more per 100g than Doritos, shows how a health claim can support a premium price.

## 6. McDonald's moves from a $3 menu to mix-and-match bundles at $3.50 to $4 (Pirates & Princesses news)

**Source:** [news.piratesandprincesses.net](https://news.piratesandprincesses.net/p/mcdonalds-tests-350-bundles-after)

McDonald's April menu of items at $3 or less didn't catch on. The company is now testing pick-two bundles and fixed bundles in about 1,900 U.S. restaurants, with a national launch planned for early 2027, after U.S. guest counts fell and higher average checks covered most of the gap. Franchisees had already started similar bundles themselves, and about 40% of the system now runs some version. The question for research is whether choosing your own bundle gives a stronger sense of a deal than a lower single-item price.

## 7. JetBlue's second bag-fee increase this year adds a penalty for checking a bag within 24 hours (Skift)

**Source:** [skift.com](https://skift.com/2026/11/03/jetblue-raises-bag-fees-for-the-second-time-this-year/)

A first checked bag now costs $50 if added more than 24 hours before departure and $60 inside that window, or $55 and $70 in peak periods. Loyalty and co-brand cardholders still check bags free. The fee now varies by both demand and booking time, which makes the ancillary price behave like a fare and turns it into a nudge to pay early. JetBlue is also one of the carriers named in the FTC's personalized-pricing study, so its fee design may get closer scrutiny.

## 8. FTC and eight states sue Toast over drip fees and cancellation hurdles for restaurants (FTC press release)

**Source:** [ftc.gov](https://ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/10/ftc-states-take-action-against-toast-inc-over-hidden-unauthorized-fees-deceptive-subscription)

The complaint alleges add-on services restaurants never agreed to, a $150-per-remaining-month early termination fee that wasn't disclosed, a 30-day written "intent not to renew" requirement, and a $0.99 fee on online orders that went to Toast. These are allegations; nothing has been decided. The case extends click-to-cancel and junk-fee enforcement to B2B software subscriptions, an area consumer-psychology research seldom covers. It's also a reminder that small restaurant owners face the same subscription tactics consumers do.
